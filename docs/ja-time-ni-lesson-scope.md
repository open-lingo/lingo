# Scope: the に time-marker distinction (JA)

Origin: TestFlight #35 (2026-09-06). Spencer built あした**に** つかうから… in m30
and asked why it was rejected, then whether "in July" style uses make に a soft
rule. It is a hard rule, and the course never teaches it explicitly — the
learner meets に-with-time in m6 (three o'clock, Monday) and bare relative
time words from m5 on, but no step ever puts the two side by side.

## The rule (what the teaching note must say)

- に marks **absolute / calendar** time: 三時に, 月曜日に, 七月に, 2020年に,
  たんじょうびに.
- **Relative / deictic** time words take **no** に: きょう, あした, きのう,
  今朝, 今晩, 今週, 来月, 去年, 毎日, いつも.
- The test: if you can point at it on a clock or calendar without knowing
  what day it is now, it takes に. If it only makes sense relative to now,
  it does not.
- Optional (advanced) contrasts: 明日**には** ("by tomorrow", emphatic) and
  明日**にでも** ("even as early as tomorrow") exist, but they are particle
  stacks, not bare に. Keep them out of the beginner beat.

## Lesson shape (one lesson, ~12 steps, build-heavy per Spencer)

Slot: right after the absolute-time debut (m6 clock/weekday module, confirm
exact neo lesson) as a reinforcement beat; or a `-review` tail in m7. Do not
put it in m30 — the learner should have it long before ておく.

1. rule card (the test above, two columns of examples).
2. 6–8 `build` steps alternating absolute and relative time, SAME verb phrase
   each pair so only the time slot changes: 三時に いく / あした いく;
   月曜日に べんきょうする / 今週 べんきょうする; 七月に りょこうする /
   来月 りょこうする. The bank always offers a に tile; on relative-time
   sentences it is the trap, on absolute ones it is required.
3. 2 particle-clozes with options [に, ∅(なし), は] on a relative-time stem —
   the ∅ option is the honest answer; check the cloze widget supports an
   empty tile (it does for は-drop in m24? verify).
4. 1 listening-comp: hear a sentence, pick the English; distractors swap
   "on Monday" / "this week".
5. Reactive tip (`pitfall`) on every relative-time build: wrong あしたに,
   why "あした already means 'the day after now' — に only points at a
   clock or calendar".
