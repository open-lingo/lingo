## 2. Module thesis (design authority for this dispatch)

*"«Cent» stays «cent» — until it doesn't."* The learner has known `cent`
since m12 as a fixed ceiling, explicitly taught as invariant, explicitly
contrasted against the numbers that count up to it (vingt-deux,
cinquante-sept) and against the euro/euros rule (the NOUN takes -s, never
the number). m21's teaching claim: multiply `cent` by a bare round number
(deux, trois, ... neuf) with NOTHING following, and it takes an -s (deux
cents, trois cents) — the exact number-takes-an--s exception the course has
never shown before — but the instant another number follows, the -s drops
again (deux cent un, trois cent cinquante), and `cent` goes back to
"invariant." `mille` (1000) is the clean counter-case that closes the
lesson: it NEVER takes an -s, ever (deux mille, trois mille) — so the
learner leaves with a real contrast, not just a longer list of numbers.

Two beats, taught in this order, never mixed silently:
1. **The ceiling gets a floor plan (L1–L4):** debut plain multiplication —
   `deux cents`, `trois cents`, up through `neuf cents` — bridging directly
   off m12's own "cent stays cent" card (quote it) and m12's own
   `euro`/`euros` "-s goes on the noun, never the number" rule (quote it) as
   the exact expectation this module breaks. Interleave the rule against
   m12's price frame («ça coûte deux cents euros») so the payoff is
   concrete, not abstract.
2. **The -s comes and goes (L5–L7):** debut compounds with a trailing
   number — `cent un`, `deux cent un`, `trois cent cinquante` — where the -s
   drops the instant something follows `cent`, contrasted directly against
   L1–L4's bare-multiple forms in the same lesson. Close with `mille` as the
   never-changes counter-case (`deux mille`, `mille`) so the whole
   inflection story resolves: `cent` sometimes takes -s, `mille` never does,
   and both never take a leading `un` the way English does ("cent," not
   "un cent" — a genuine EN/FR contrast worth a bridge card, mirroring the
   euro-note's own "the noun takes -s, never the number" discipline).

## 5. Homophone / liaison risks

- **`cent` [sɑ̃] and `cents` [sɑ̃] are pronounced IDENTICALLY in any context
  where the following word does not force liaison** (i.e. before a
  consonant-initial word, or utterance-final) — a genuine, UNVERIFIED (§6)
  near/exact-homophone pair, the same risk CLASS m20's `viens`/`vient`
  finding was (subject/inflection carried by spelling/context, not sound).
  Before a vowel-initial word (`deux cents euros`) the -s DOES liaise
  audibly ([sɑ̃z‿øʁo]) — so the distinction is partially recoverable by ear
  ONLY in that specific phonetic environment, never in the bare "deux
  cents" vs "deux cent [un]" case, where nothing follows to liaise against
  until the NEXT word is heard. No `speaking` step should ever ask the
  learner to prove "cents" vs "cent" from the number word alone — the
  following context (or its absence) is what's actually gradable.
- **`cent`/`cents` vs `sang`/`sans`/`sent`** (all [sɑ̃], none registered in
  this course) — no course vocabulary collision exists today (grepped:
  none of those three surfaces appear anywhere in m1–m20), so no in-course
  confusion risk, but flagged for completeness since it's the same sound
  family; not actionable without a competing atom.
- **No new elision landmine inside this module's own scope** — if the
  author's chosen sentences stay inside m12's existing consonant-onset
  price-frame nouns (`euro`, per precedent), no d'-elision exposure occurs;
  flag explicitly in the module header if any new noun is introduced,
  mirroring m20's own «habiter»-exclusion discipline.
- **No new `liaisonListen` item** planned by default — consistent with
  m12–m20 precedent (no liaison_listen bank in the numbers modules so far)
  — BUT the `deux cents euros` liaison ([sɑ̃z‿øʁo]) is a genuine, audible,
  correct-French liaison the module's own sentences will produce whether or
  not a dedicated step is authored for it; author should consider whether
  it merits one given it's more phonetically salient than anything m12/m17
  shipped without a liaison_listen bank of their own — a judgment call for
  whoever authors, not decided here.

  its own 70–99 content and stayed written-only as a result — same

  consumed as one token — it would be read as "10" + "1" (→ "dix" + "un" by

  "1000" fares no better. **This is a real, reasoned risk from reading the
  code, not an assumption of safety by analogy — flagged honestly as
