already ratified replacing it. Do not invest in per-lesson fixes below without
first deciding whether A1–A3 land instead.

### Finding 3 — MAJOR: `ja-m30-6-2-self-explain` teaches that polite register drops よ/ね — contradicting m29 and the language

**Where:** `ja-m30-6-2-self-explain`. The correct option and `ruleExplanation`
state the register bundle as: *"casual drops か and keeps よ/ね/contractions;
polite adds ます/か back and drops よ/ね. Flip all three together, or the
sentence reads as mixed register — its own kind of mistake."* The learner's own
course disproves this: `ja-m29-neo-9` (「〜んですよ」と「〜んですね」) taught
よ/ね ON polite んです forms one module earlier, and そうですね/いいですよ are
among the most common polite sentences in Japanese. A learner who internalized
m29 must affirm a claim they know to be false to get this step right. This is
the rule-card-contradicts-N5-card class the walk was asked to watch for
(the module's 6-2 has no card; the self-explain is doing card duty).

### Finding 4 — MAJOR: `ja-m30-7-1` marks どうしたのですか as impossible, contradicting m29's んです teaching

**Where:** `ja-m30-7-1-self-explain` (and `-mcq-3`'s framing). The question is
*"Why can't you just add ですか onto どうしたの to make it polite
(どうしたのですか)?"* and the graded-correct answer asserts polite conversion
must reform the verb (どうしましたか) rather than "patching です onto the
casual pattern." But どうしたのですか／どうしたんですか is standard, extremely
common polite Japanese — and it is precisely the 〜んです(か) pattern m29-neo-9
taught (STATE.md m23–m29: んだ/んです). The mcq itself survives (its wrong
options どうしたのか／どうしたですか are genuinely wrong; どうしましたか is
genuinely right), but the self-explanation teaches an explicit falsehood that
collides with the learner's freshest N5 module. Same class as finding 3.

   fix in `src/shared/tts/index.ts`; affects any `…` content course-wide.
2. **Never generated (6 sentences / 7 strings):** いそがしいから、ちょっと…

## What the learner actually experiences

The module is *pleasant* — and that is mostly because it re-teaches things this
learner already owns. Casual questions, ない？ invitations, and register
awareness were the entire back half of m29; the learner sails through pairs 1,
5, 6 and 7 on m29 muscle memory, and the genuinely new material (よ vs ね
function, の-softener, the register VOCABULARY けいご/ためぐち/ていねい/
しつれい and the workplace social nouns) lands well, with recognition-first
debuts that are better-disciplined than most N5 modules. The pain is at the
edges: sentences quietly assume a July-course vocabulary (つかう, てつだう,
つかれる, しゅくだい…) this learner never met, so every few steps a tile or an
accepted answer contains a word they can only place by elimination; the 2-2
translate is the one place elimination cannot save them and they simply fail;
6-2's drills go silent mid-lesson; and two self-explanations ask them to affirm
claims their own m29 disproves. Nothing they learn here will ever be reviewed:
the five rule cards write to no grammar registry, and the module has no review
lessons. It feels like a well-made module for a different course — which is
exactly what it is.
