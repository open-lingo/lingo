## Lesson 2 — The connector — て (ja-m8-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — The connector — て
   The て-form is the shape everything else attaches to — requests now, whole chains of actions later. る-verbs make it by swapping る for て: たべる → たべて, みる → みて. On its own it is what you say to a friend: たべて = 'eat', 'go on, eat'.
      · しゃしんを みて。  —  Look at the photo.
      · たべて。  —  Go on, eat.
   NOT: しゃしんを みるて。 — る-verbs drop る before て — みる→みて, never みるて.
2. [conjugation_transform] Change 【たべる】 (to eat) into: て form

## Lesson 3 — The sound-change table (ja-m8-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — The sound-change table — う-verbs
   う-verbs change their last kana before て, and which change depends on that kana: む・ぶ・ぬ → んで (のむ→のんで, あそぶ→あそんで); う・つ・る → って (かう→かって); く → いて (きく→きいて); ぐ → いで. Learn this table once and it pays twice — the past form た uses exactly the same rows.
      · みずを のんで。  —  Drink the water.
      · ほんを かって。  —  Buy the book.
      · きいて。  —  Listen.
   NOT: みずを のみて。 — のむ ends in む, so it takes the んで row — のんで, never のみて.

## Lesson 4 — The rebels — いって, して, きて (ja-m8-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — The rebels — いって, して, きて
   Three verbs ignore the table. する → して and くる → きて are the two irregulars you already know as a pair. The third is いく: it ends in く, so the table promises いいて — but it is いって, borrowing the う・つ・る row. Memorise these three as a set.
      · いって。  —  Go on, go.
      · これを して。  —  Do this one.
      · きて。  —  Come here.
   NOT: いいて。 — いく looks like a く-verb but skips the く→いて row — its て-form is いって.

## Lesson 7 — Please — てください (ja-m8-neo-4)

   て + ください = a polite request: 'please do X'. It is a fixed FORMULA — you attach ください to the て-form and nothing else changes. Drop ください and the bare て-form is the casual version friends use: みて = 'look', みてください = 'please look'.
      · みてください  —  Please look.
      · たべてください  —  Please eat.

## Lesson 8 — Food and meals (ja-m8-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Food and meals
   A request needs something to request, and food is where a beginner starts. ごはん is cooked rice — and, by extension, the meal built round it. しょくじ is the meal itself, the sit-down occasion: しょくじを する is 'to have a meal'. たべもの is food in general, and ちゃ is tea. Whatever you eat, drink or ask for takes を. And to ask someone NOT to do something, you swap the て-form for the ない-form and add で: たべて → たべないで = 'don't eat it'. Same shape, opposite instruction — 「ミカの たべものを たべないで」 is 'don't eat Mika's food'.
      · しょくじを する。  —  I have a meal.
      · ちゃを のむ。  —  I drink tea.

## Lesson 9 — At the shop (ja-m8-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — At the shop
   で marks WHERE an action happens, and for a beginner that is the shop: みせで かう is 'buy it at the shop'. What you buy there is こめ — uncooked rice, the bag on the shelf, not the cooked bowl on the table (that one is ごはん). Getting to the shop is still に, not で: みせに いく.
      · いえで たべる。  —  I eat at home.
      · こめを かう。  —  I buy rice.
   NOT: いえに たべる。 — に is where a thing IS; the place you do something takes で — いえで たべる.

## Lesson 13 — Friends don't say ください (ja-m8-neo-8)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Friends don't say ください
   ください is the polite half, and it detaches. みてください → みて: same verb, same て-form, and the whole difference in politeness lives in whether ください is there. Bare て for friends and family; keep ください for teachers, shopkeepers and people you have just met.
      · みて。  —  Look. (to a friend)
      · みてください。  —  Please look. (polite)
