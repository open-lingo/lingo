# m24 — Can & let's

Module 24 · Ability

「おさけが のめる」 — I can drink. The potential is a FORM, not a word: う-verbs slide to the え row and add る, る-verbs attach られる, くる becomes こられる, and する hands the job to できる. The thing you can do stops being を and becomes が. Every can-form is itself a る-verb, so 「のめない」 is module 6's rule on a new stem. You will also hear people drop the ら — 「たべれる」 — and you need to understand that without saying it. Then 見える against 見られる, 〜のが じょうず against 〜のが へた, and one invitation in three registers: 「たべない？」, 「たべませんか」, 「たべましょう」.

## Lesson 1 — 「おさけが のめる」 — the え row and る (ja-m24-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「おさけが のめる」 — the え row and る
   Japanese has a form that means CAN, and for an う-verb you build it the same way you have built everything else: move the last kana along the chart and add an ending. ます took the い row (のむ → のみます). ない took the あ row (のむ → のまない). The potential takes the え row and adds る. のむ ends in む, so slide to め: のめる, I can drink. いく → いける. およぐ → およげる. あるく → あるける. あそぶ → あそべる. はなす → はなせる. One rule, every う-verb, no exceptions worth worrying about. There is a second half nobody warns you about, and it matters more than the ending. The thing you can do stops being を and becomes が. 「おさけを のむ」 is I drink sake; 「おさけが のめる」 is I can drink sake. The reason is worth one sentence: のめる is not really about doing anything — it says the drinking is POSSIBLE — and が is the particle that has marked the thing under discussion since 「かぎが ある」 in module 6. Same が, same job. And the form you end up with is itself a る-verb, which is going to make the negative free.
      · はたちだから おさけが のめる。  —  I'm twenty, so I can drink sake.
      · げんきだから かわで およげる。  —  I'm well, so I can swim in the river.
      · じかんが あるから きっさてんに いける。  —  I have time, so I can go to the coffee shop.
      · あさは コーヒーが のめるけど ばんは ぎゅうにゅうを のむ。  —  I can drink coffee in the morning, but at night I drink milk.
      · じてんしゃが ないけど がっこうまで いける。  —  I have no bicycle, but I can get as far as school.
   NOT: はたちだから おさけが のむれる。 — the potential of のむ moves む to め and adds る — のめる, never のむれる: はたちだから おさけが のめる。

## Lesson 2 — 「すしが たべられる」 — られる, and the ら people drop (ja-m24-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「すしが たべられる」 — られる, and the ら people drop
   る-verbs are easier than う-verbs and the irregulars are shorter than the list makes them look. For a る-verb you do not touch the stem at all: drop る, add られる. たべる → たべられる. みる → みられる. おしえる → おしえられる. That is the whole rule — it is attachment, not a row on the chart. くる is the one that changes shape: こられる. And する has no potential form at all, which is the next lesson's word. が does exactly what it did with う-verbs: 「すしが たべられる」. Now the part you will hear long before you are ready for it. In ordinary speech people drop the ら and say 「たべれる」, 「みれる」. It is called ら抜きことば — ら-dropped words — and it is everywhere, on television, in shops, from your friends. So you need to UNDERSTAND it from today. You do not need to say it. Speak たべられる and you are correct with every person in Japan; speak たべれる and some of them will hear a mistake. Recognise the short one, produce the long one, and you never have to think about it again.
      · きっさてんで すしが たべられる。  —  You can eat sushi at the coffee shop.
      · としょかんで えいがが みられるから よく いく。  —  You can watch films at the library, so I go often.
      · ひまだから あした ミカも こられる。  —  Mika's free, so she can come tomorrow too.
      · すしが たべれる。  —  I can eat sushi. — the casual ら-dropped form; recognise it, but say たべられる
      · えいがが みれる。  —  I can watch a film. — the casual ら-dropped form; recognise it, but say みられる
   NOT: きっさてんで すしが たべるられる。 — a る-verb DROPS る before られる — たべられる, never たべるられる: きっさてんで すしが たべられる。

## Lesson 3 — 「りょうりが できる」 — the verb that replaces する (ja-m24-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「りょうりが できる」 — the verb that replaces する
   する is the one verb with no potential form, and the reason is that Japanese already had a word for the job: できる. It is not built from anything. You learn it once and you own the whole する family with it — 「さんぽが できる」, 「かいものが できる」, 「りょこうが できる」. Which shows you the bigger half. できる takes a NOUN in front of it with が, and that noun does not have to be a する-word at all: 「にほんの りょうりが できる」 means I can cook Japanese food, with no verb anywhere in the sentence. English needs a different verb for each of those — cook, shop, travel — and Japanese needs one word and a noun. できる is also a る-verb, like every potential form, so it behaves itself everywhere else. And が is not optional here in the way it sometimes is elsewhere: できる has never taken を in its life.
      · にほんの りょうりが できる。  —  I can cook Japanese food.
      · げんきだから あした さんぽが できる。  —  I'm well, so I can go for a walk tomorrow.
      · あさは じかんが あるから かいものが できる。  —  I have time in the morning, so I can do the shopping.
      · なつやすみに りょこうが できるから きっぷを かう。  —  I can travel in the summer holidays, so I'm buying a ticket.
      · ひまな とき うちで りょうりが できる。  —  When I'm free, I can cook at home.
   NOT: にほんの りょうりが すれる。 — する has no regular potential — the word is できる: にほんの りょうりが できる。

## Lesson 5 — 「おさけが のめない」 — every can-form is a る-verb (ja-m24-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「おさけが のめない」 — every can-form is a る-verb
   Here is the payoff for the line you have heard three times now: every potential form is a る-verb. のめる, いける, たべられる, できる — all of them end in る and all of them behave like たべる from this moment on. So the negative is module 6's rule, unchanged, applied to a new stem. Drop る, add ない. のめる → のめない. およげる → およげない. たべられる → たべられない. できる → できない. Nothing new to learn, which is the whole argument for treating the potential as a FORM rather than a pile of words. Keep が: 「おさけが のめない」, I can't drink. You will use these more than the positives, because half of what anyone says about ability is what they cannot do. Two things worth adding while you are here. ぜんぜん means not at all and it only ever appears in front of a negative, so 「ぜんぜん りょうりが できない」 is I can't cook a thing. And もの means thing, so 「たべられない ものが ある」 — there are things I can't eat — puts a whole potential clause in front of a noun, which is module 15's relative clause doing a job module 15 never asked of it.
      · おさけが のめないから ぎゅうにゅうを のむ。  —  I can't drink sake, so I'll have milk.
      · たまごが たべられないから とりにくを かう。  —  I can't eat eggs, so I'll buy chicken.
      · ぜんぜん りょうりが できない。  —  I can't cook at all.
      · たべられない ものが あるけど すしは だいじょうぶだ。  —  There are things I can't eat, but sushi is fine.
      · ねつが あるから きょうは さんぽが できない。  —  I have a fever, so I can't go for a walk today.
   NOT: おさけが のめるないから ぎゅうにゅうを のむ。 — ない REPLACES the final る, it never follows it — のめない: おさけが のめないから ぎゅうにゅうを のむ。

## Lesson 6 — 「やまが みえる」 — visible is not the same as allowed (ja-m24-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「やまが みえる」 — what you can see is not what you get to watch
   みられる and みえる are both about seeing and they are not the same sentence. みられる is the potential you built two lessons ago: it says the OPPORTUNITY exists. 「としょかんで えいがが みられる」 — you can watch films at the library, because they have them and you are allowed in. Somebody arranged that. みえる is not about you at all. It says the thing is VISIBLE, and whether anyone is looking is beside the point: 「まどから やまが みえる」, you can see the mountain from the window, which is a fact about the window. Ears divide the same way. きける is I get to listen; きこえる is it reaches my ear. 「こえが きこえる」 means a voice arrives, not that you chose to hear it. Both take が and both are る-verbs, so nothing else changes. And the test is one question: did a person decide? If yes, it is the potential. If the world is simply like that, it is みえる or きこえる.
      · まどから やまが みえる。  —  You can see the mountain from the window.
      · ばんは そらに ほしが みえる。  —  You can see stars in the sky at night.

## Lesson 7 — 「およぐのが じょうずだ」 — the word for other people (ja-m24-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「およぐのが じょうずだ」 — the word you say about other people
   じょうず means skilled, and it is a な-adjective, so it finishes with だ the way げんきだ does. To say what someone is good AT, take the verb, turn it into a thing with の, and mark that thing with が: 「およぐのが じょうずだ」, good at swimming. That の is module 13's の — the one that turned たべる into something you could like — and here it turns a verb into something you can be good at. The frame is 〜のが じょうずだ and it does not vary. A noun can sit there instead with no の at all: 「うたが じょうずだ」, good at singing. Now the rule that matters more than the grammar. じょうず is what you say about OTHER people. Calling yourself じょうず lands as boasting in a way the English good at simply does not, so Japanese speakers use it generously about their friends and stay quiet about themselves. Say it about other people as often as you can — it is one of the kindest words in the language — and use the next lesson's word about yourself.

## Lesson 9 — 「するのが へただ」 — the word for yourself (ja-m24-neo-9)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「するのが へただ」 — the word you say about yourself
   へた is the opposite word and it is the one you use about yourself. Same frame exactly — 〜のが へただ — and the same な-adjective ending: 「あるくのが へただ」. Where じょうず is for other people, へた is for you, and it is not the confession English makes it sound like: 「ぼくは りょうりを するのが へただ」 is modest, ordinary and completely unremarkable to say. It is also how a Japanese speaker deflects a compliment, so you will hear it constantly right after somebody has praised something. Two more things. First, へた says a skill is not there yet; it is not a judgement about a person. Second, if you want to say someone ELSE is not good at something, へた is too blunt and the polite move is a negative instead: 「あまり じょうずじゃない」, not very good. あまり has been sitting in your vocabulary since module 9 waiting for a negative to stand in front of, and this is it.

## Lesson 10 — 「いっしょに たべましょう」 — the plan everyone agrees with (ja-m24-neo-10)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「いっしょに たべましょう」 — the plan everyone already agrees with
   ましょう is let's. Take the ます-form, take ます off, put ましょう in its place: たべます → たべましょう, いきます → いきましょう, のみます → のみましょう, ききます → ききましょう. する is irregular here as everywhere and becomes しましょう. Two things about it are worth knowing before you use it. It is POLITE — ましょう is built on the ます layer, so it belongs with a teacher, in a shop, with a group you have only just met. And it is CONFIDENT: ましょう assumes everyone already agrees. 「いっしょに たべましょう」 is let's eat together, said as a decision that has gone your way rather than a question. いっしょに means together and sits at the front of the sentence, in front of the object and everything else. So use ましょう when the plan is obvious and welcome — the bill has arrived, the lesson has finished, everybody is hungry. When you are not sure the other person wants to, ましょう is too strong, and the next lesson has the word for that.
      · いっしょに たべましょう。  —  Let's eat together.
      · きっさてんで コーヒーを のみましょう。  —  Let's drink coffee at the coffee shop.
      · あした いっしょに としょかんに いきましょう。  —  Let's go to the library together tomorrow.

## Lesson 11 — 「たべませんか」 と 「たべない？」 — one invitation, three registers (ja-m24-neo-11)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「たべませんか」 と 「たべない？」 — one invitation, three registers
   ませんか is the same invitation with the door left open. It is literally the polite negative plus か — won't you eat? — and that negative is doing real work: by asking whether you will NOT, it makes saying no easy, which is exactly why it is the polite way to invite somebody who might not want to come. Put the two side by side. 「いっしょに たべませんか」 offers. 「いっしょに たべましょう」 decides. Offer first; decide once they have said yes. Now the casual twin, which you will use far more than either. Drop the polite layer entirely, use the ない-form, and let your voice rise: 「いっしょに たべない？」 — wanna eat? That is the plain-form invitation and it is what friends actually say to each other. So there are three rungs and one meaning: 「たべない？」 to a friend, 「たべませんか」 to anybody else, 「たべましょう」 once it is settled. Nothing else moves — いっしょに still sits at the front, the object still takes を, and the verb is the only thing that changes register.
      · いっしょに たべませんか。  —  Won't you eat with me?
      · あした えいがを みませんか。  —  Won't you watch a film tomorrow?
      · いっしょに たべない？  —  Wanna eat together? — to a friend
      · あした きっさてんに いきませんか。  —  Won't you go to the coffee shop tomorrow?
      · あした うちに こない？  —  Wanna come over tomorrow? — to a friend
   NOT: いっしょに たべるませんか。 — ません attaches to the ます-form, never the dictionary form: いっしょに たべませんか。
