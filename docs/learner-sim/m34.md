# m34 — Volitional — よう/おう, とおもう, ことにする

Module 34 · JLPT N4

You already own more of this than it looks like: のみましょう has been yours since module 24, and it turns out that was always the polite dress on a plainer word — のもう. This module hands you that plainer word for every verb you know, then puts it to work — inviting a friend, deciding to quit a job, admitting an attempt fell short, setting a goal for the year ahead. Two real conversations carry it, so the same suggestion lands casual with a friend and polite with a teacher, on purpose.

## Lesson 1 — 「のもう」 — the will behind ましょう (ja-m34-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「のもう」 — the will behind ましょう
   This is the form ましょう has been hiding. A u-verb turns its last sound from the u-row to the o-row and takes う: のむ→のもう, いく→いこう, かう→かおう, けす→けそう. The meaning is your own will, thrown forward: 「コーヒーを のもう」 is "let's have a coffee" with somebody, or "right — I'll have a coffee" alone to yourself. Nothing else in the sentence changes: whatever it had — a を, a に, a から — stays exactly where it was. Only the verb's ending moves, and it moves the same way every time.
      · コーヒーを のもう。  —  Let's have a coffee.
      · としょかんに いこう。  —  Let's go to the library.
      · あたらしい かばんを かおう。  —  I'll buy a new bag.
      · でんきを けそう。  —  Let's turn off the light.
   NOT: コーヒーを のむう。 — the volitional is not う stuck on the dictionary form — the last sound itself moves to the o-row first: のむ → のも + う. のもう.
2. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「のもう」 — the will behind ましょう
   Now the reveal. You have said のみましょう since module 24 — and のみましょう is just のもう wearing the polite layer. Same word, same will, dressed up. This is the course's oldest claim about Japanese — politeness is a LAYER over the real sentence, not a different language — landing on a form you learned backwards. You met the polite version first; here is the base it was built on. So nothing new to memorise: everywhere you would say のみましょう to your teacher, you say のもう to a friend. The pair 「のもう / のみましょう」 works exactly like 「のむ / のみます」.
      · いっしょに たべよう。  —  Let's eat together.
      · いっしょに たべましょう。  —  Let's eat together. (polite)
      · えきまで あるこう。  —  Let's walk to the station.
   NOT: いっしょに たべますよう。 — the polite suggestion is not ます with よう stacked on top — the layers FUSE: ましょう is the volitional's polite form entire. いっしょに たべましょう。

## Lesson 2 — 「たべよう・しよう・こよう」 — the ru-verbs and the two you memorise (ja-m34-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「たべよう・しよう・こよう」 — the ru-verbs and the two you memorise
   The ru-verbs do what ru-verbs always do: drop る, take よう. たべる→たべよう, みる→みよう, でる→でよう. And the two irregulars are two words to know, not a rule: する→しよう, くる→こよう. That closes the whole system — every verb you own can now carry your will. Notice the shape against the u-verbs: のもう ends in おう, たべよう ends in よう. If you hear 〜よう, you are looking at a ru-verb or an irregular; 〜おう and it was a u-verb.
      · ばんごはんを たべよう。  —  Let's eat dinner.
      · しゅくだいを はじめよう。  —  Let's start the homework.
      · あした また こよう。  —  Let's come again tomorrow.
      · そろそろ しよう。  —  Let's do it, it's about time.
   NOT: ばんごはんを たべろう。 — たべる is a ru-verb — it drops る and takes よう, not the u-verb's おう: たべよう. たべろう treats it as a u-verb ending in べる.

## Lesson 3 — 「いこう？」 — the plainest invitation there is (ja-m34-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「いこう？」 — the plainest invitation there is
   Said flat, 「いこう」 is let's-go. Said with a question's rise — 「いこう？」 — it is an INVITATION, the plainest one Japanese has. You have owned the polite rungs of this ladder since module 24: いきませんか asks "won't you go?", いきましょうか offers "shall we?". 「いこう？」 is the same move with the layers off, and it is what friends actually say. Pick by audience, exactly as always: to Ken, いこう？ To たなかせんせい, いきませんか. The move never changes; the dress does.
      · いっしょに えいがを みよう？  —  Want to watch a film together?
      · こうえんに いこう？  —  Shall we go to the park?
      · いっしょに いきませんか。  —  Won't you come along? (polite)
   NOT: せんせい、いっしょに いこう？ — the bare volitional invitation is friend-register — inviting your teacher takes the polite rung of the same ladder: いきませんか.

## Lesson 5 — 「さがそうとおもう」 — intent, held at arm's length (ja-m34-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「さがそうとおもう」 — intent, held at arm's length
   Put とおもう behind a volitional and you get intent held in the mind: 「しごとを さがそうとおもう」 — I think I'll look for a job. You have quoted thoughts with とおもう since module 18; this is the same machine with a volitional inside the quote. The difference from つもり (module 23) is temperature: つもり is a settled plan you could put on a calendar; 〜ようとおもう is the will still forming — "I'm thinking I'll…". That is why it pairs so naturally with しょうらい and ゆめ: futures are exactly the things we hold at arm's length.
      · しごとを さがそうとおもう。  —  I think I'll look for a job.
      · らいげつから まいにち あるこうとおもう。  —  I think I'll walk every day from next month.
      · しょうらい にほんで はたらこうとおもう。  —  I think I'll work in Japan in the future.
   NOT: しごとを さがすとおもう。 — with the dictionary form inside, とおもう reports a guess about the world — "I think (someone) will look for a job". Your own intent puts the VOLITIONAL in the quote: さがそうとおもう.

## Lesson 6 — 「やめることにした」 — deciding, hands on the wheel (ja-m34-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「やめることにした」 — deciding, hands on the wheel
   「〜ことにする」 is deciding, with your hands on the wheel. Take the whole action as a こと — a thing, module 15's nominalizer — and する it: 「かいしゃを やめることにした」, I decided to quit the company. する is the tell that YOU pushed: the decision has an owner, and it is the speaker. Present tense decides now (ことにする); past tense reports the decision already made (ことにした), which is how it usually appears — we mostly tell people about decisions after making them.
      · まいにち れんしゅうすることにする。  —  I've decided to practise every day.
      · かいしゃを やめることにした。  —  I decided to quit the company.
      · ちょきんを はじめることにした。  —  I decided to start saving.
   NOT: まいにち れんしゅうするにする。 — する needs a THING to decide on, and a bare verb is not a thing yet — こと does that job first: れんしゅうすることにする.

   TILES: やめる | が | かいしゃ | で | に | を

## Lesson 7 — 「けっこんすることになった」 — when the decision arrives (ja-m34-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「けっこんすることになった」 — when the decision arrives
   Same shape, different engine: 「〜ことになる」 is the decision arriving from outside. なる is the verb of the world changing by itself — you have had it since module 27's くなる/になる — so 「けっこんすることになった」 says the marriage got decided, without pointing at who decided. Japanese reaches for this constantly, and not only for modesty: transfers, schedules, plans made by families and companies genuinely are decided around us. する-version: I own it. なる-version: it landed on me. The particle-perfect minimal pair with ことにする is the whole lesson.
      · らいげつ けっこんすることになった。  —  It's been decided we're getting married next month.
      · アメリカで はたらくことになった。  —  It's been settled that I'll be working in America.
      · らいねん そつぎょうすることになる。  —  It'll turn out that I graduate next year.

## Lesson 9 — 「あけようとしたけど、あかなかった」 — the attempt itself (ja-m34-neo-9)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「あけようとしたけど、あかなかった」 — the attempt itself
   「〜ようとする」 is the attempt itself — will meeting the world, with no promise the world gave way. 「ドアを あけようとした」: I went to open the door. Maybe it opened; usually the sentence continues with what got in the way — けど、あかなかった. Keep it apart from module 30's てみる, because English "try" flattens them into one word: あけてみた means you DID open it, to find out what would happen; あけようとした means you may never have got it open at all. Do-and-see versus almost-did. You need only recognise this one for now — production comes later in the tier.
      · ドアを あけようとした。  —  I tried to open the door.
      · あけようとしたけど、あかなかった。  —  I tried to open it, but it wouldn't open.
      · でんわを かけようとした とき、ともだちが きた。  —  Just as I was about to make a call, my friend came.
   NOT: ドアを あけるとした。 — the attempt puts the VOLITIONAL in front of とする — the dictionary form belongs to guesses (あけるとおもう), not attempts: あけようとした.
