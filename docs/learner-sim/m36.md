# m36 — Looks like — そう, がる, やすい/にくい, ながら

Module 36 · JLPT N4

One seam, four attachments, all riding the same stem you've owned since module 7. おいしそう isn't the food tasting good — it's how it LOOKS before the first bite, and そう lives exactly in that gap between what your eyes report and what you actually know, on adjectives (かたそう, looks tough) and on verbs about to happen (ふりそう, about to rain) alike. Then the door N5 locked shut on purpose: you could always say what YOU want, never what somebody else does. おとうとは いきたがっている finally opens it — がる turns a private feeling into a visible behavior instead of pretending to read minds. Two more ride the same stem: やすい/にくい turns any verb into its own difficulty rating, and ながら lets one person do two things at once. A dinner at Mika's puts the whole gap to work — you say what looks good before you know if it is.

## Lesson 1 — 「おいしそう」 — what the eye reports (ja-m36-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「おいしそう」 — what the eye reports
   Take an adjective, cut its tail, add そう — and you are reporting how something LOOKS before you have tried it. おいしい → おいしそう: it looks tasty, says the eye, not the tongue. たかい → たかそう, looks expensive. The い goes; そう sits on the bare stem. Two irregulars, learned now while the rule is fresh: いい becomes よさそう (looks good), and ない becomes なさそう (looks like there isn't). What you get behaves like a な-adjective: おいしそうな りょうり, a tasty-looking dish. The whole point of the form is the gap between looks and knowledge — you say おいしそう BEFORE the first bite; after it, you just say おいしい.
      · この りょうりは おいしそう。  —  This dish looks tasty.
      · その とけいは たかそう。  —  That watch looks expensive.
      · この ほんは おもしろそう。  —  This book looks interesting.
      · きょうは てんきが よさそう。  —  The weather looks good today.
   NOT: この りょうりは おいしいそう。 — そう sits on the STEM — the い has to go: おいしそう. (Keeping the い builds a different word you will meet much later; for now it is simply not the looks-like form.)

## Lesson 2 — 「ふりそう」 — an event leaning forward (ja-m36-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ふりそう」 — an event leaning forward
   On a verb, そう takes the ます-stem — the same stem you have used since module 7 — and means it looks about to happen: ふる → ふりそう, looks like rain. おちる → おちそう, about to fall. The feel is an event leaning forward; you say it while there is still time to grab the cup. Negatives go two ways and both are worth having: あめは ふらなさそう (it doesn't look like rain) and あめは ふりそうにない (there's no sign of it raining) — the second is the stronger, flatter denial. Both are recognition-first here; production comes with use.
      · あめが ふりそう。  —  It looks like rain.
      · かばんから さいふが おちそう。  —  Your wallet looks about to fall out of your bag.
      · えいがが はじまりそう。  —  The film looks about to start.
      · あめは ふりそうにない。  —  There's no sign of rain.
   NOT: あめが ふるそう。 — the looks-like そう rides the ます-stem: ふり + そう. On the dictionary form it stops being about looks at all — ふりそう is what the sky shows you.

## Lesson 5 — 「いきたがっている」 — reading other minds (ja-m36-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「いきたがっている」 — reading other minds
   Japanese is honest about other minds: you know what YOU want, but you can only READ what others want. That is why 「おとうとは いきたい」 sounds off — たい is first-person interior. For third person, がる turns the feeling into visible behavior: いきたい → いきたがる, "he's acting like he wants to go." Almost always in ている, because the behavior is ongoing: おとうとは こうえんに いきたがっている。 The same がる works straight on emotion adjectives — こわい → こわがる (to act scared), はずかしい → はずかしがる (to act embarrassed). Cut the い, add がる, and the private feeling becomes the public verb of showing it. This closes a door N5 deliberately left shut: now you can finally say what somebody ELSE wants.
      · おとうとは こうえんに いきたがっている。  —  My little brother wants to go to the park.
      · いもうとは あたらしい くつを かいたがっている。  —  My little sister wants to buy new shoes.
      · ねこは そとに でたがっている。  —  The cat wants to go outside.
   NOT: おとうとは こうえんに いきたい。 — たい is first-person interior — you can't feel your brother's wanting from the inside. What you CAN see is the wanting-behavior: いきたがっている。

## Lesson 6 — 「つかいやすい」 — adjectives you build yourself (ja-m36-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「つかいやすい」 — adjectives you build yourself
   Stem + やすい: easy to. Stem + にくい: hard to. The same attachment site as たい (module 13) — よむ → よみやすい (easy to read), わかる → わかりにくい (hard to follow). And here is the payoff of building adjectives out of adjectives: the result IS an i-adjective, so everything you know about i-adjectives applies unchanged — よみやすかった, わかりにくくない, たべやすくて。 It is judgment about the THING, not about you: この じしょは つかいやすい praises the dictionary, not your skill. Pick the stem, pick the half, and the language does the rest with machinery you have owned for twenty modules.
      · この じしょは つかいやすい。  —  This dictionary is easy to use.
      · この ことばは おぼえにくい。  —  This word is hard to memorise.
      · ミカの せつめいは わかりやすい。  —  Mika's explanations are easy to follow.
      · この みちは あるきやすかった。  —  This road was easy to walk.
   NOT: この じしょは つかうやすい。 — やすい rides the ます-stem, exactly like たい: つかい + やすい. The dictionary form never takes the attachment.

## Lesson 7 — 「ききながら」 — two things, one person (ja-m36-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ききながら」 — two things, one person
   Stem + ながら: doing one thing WHILE doing another. おんがくを ききながら しゅくだいを する — homework, with music behind it. Same attachment site again: the ます-stem, nothing new to learn about the shape. One rule of meaning that English hides: the ながら-half is the BACKGROUND, and the sentence's real point comes second. 「たべながら テレビを みる」 is watching TV (main), over food (background); flip the halves and you flip what matters. Both actions belong to the same person — ながら never joins two different people's doings.
      · おんがくを ききながら しゅくだいを する。  —  I do my homework while listening to music.
      · テレビを みながら ごはんを たべる。  —  I eat dinner while watching TV.
      · あるきながら はなす。  —  We talk while walking.
   NOT: おんがくを きくながら しゅくだいを する。 — ながら rides the ます-stem like every attachment in this module: きき + ながら. The dictionary form doesn't take it.
