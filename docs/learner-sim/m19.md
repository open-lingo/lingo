# m19 — Getting around: motion particles

Module 19 · Motion

Where you go, how you get there and when. に marks the place you arrive at, へ points at the direction you head — and へ is written he but READ e, the one spelling in the language that catches everybody. で marks the vehicle (「バスで いく」), the ます-stem plus に いく says why you are going (「たべに いく」), and から, まで and までに hang the whole journey on the clock, minutes and all.

## Lesson 1 — 「えきに いく」 — に marks where you arrive (ja-m19-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「えきに いく」 — に marks where you arrive
   に marks the place you ARRIVE at. 「えきに いく」 = I'm going to the station. This is not a new particle — it is a third job for one you have had for a long time. You already use に for where something IS (「うちに いる」) and for WHEN something happens (「くじに いく」), and destination is the same idea once more: に pins the sentence to a point, and the point can be a place, a time, or the place you end up. The word order is the ordinary one, with the place before the verb and the verb last: 「あした きっさてんに いく」. Every verb of motion takes it — いく, くる, and かえる, which is the verb for going back where you belong: home, your own town, your country. かえる is not a formal 'return'; it is the everyday word, and Japanese reaches for it far more often than English reaches for 'go back'.
      · えきに いく。  —  I'm going to the station.
      · ミカは きっさてんに いく。  —  Mika is going to the coffee shop.
      · トムは あした だいがくに くる。  —  Tom is coming to the university tomorrow.
      · ろくじに うちに かえる。  —  I'm going home at six.
      · なつやすみに にほんに いく。  —  I'm going to Japan for the summer holiday.
   NOT: えきで いく。 — で marks where an action HAPPENS; the place you go TO is marked with に: えきに いく。

## Lesson 2 — 「にほんへ いく」 — written he, said e (ja-m19-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「にほんへ いく」 — written he, said e
   へ is the one genuinely new piece in this module, and the first thing to know about it is how it SOUNDS. Written, it is the hiragana he. Used as a PARTICLE it is read **e** — always, with no exceptions, and nothing else in the writing system does this. Learn it once and it never troubles you again. Now the meaning: へ points at the DIRECTION you are heading, に at the ARRIVAL you are heading for. 「にほんへ いく」 leans on the journey, 「にほんに いく」 leans on getting there. With いく, くる and かえる the two are close enough that either is fine, and に is what people reach for by default — treat へ as the slightly softer 'off toward' option. What へ cannot do is に's OTHER jobs. It never marks a clock time and it never marks where something exists. 「ろくじへ かえる」 is wrong; a time is always に.
      · ろくじに うちへ かえる。  —  I'm heading home at six.
      · にほんへ いく。  —  I'm heading to Japan.
      · ミカは うみへ いく。  —  Mika is heading to the sea.
      · ケンは あした がっこうへ くる。  —  Ken is coming to school tomorrow.
      · やまへ いくから くつを かう。  —  I'm heading to the mountains, so I'll buy shoes.
   NOT: ろくじへ うちへ かえる。 — へ points at a PLACE you head toward; a clock time is always marked with に: ろくじに うちへ かえる。

## Lesson 3 — 「バスで いく」 — で is how you get there (ja-m19-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「バスで いく」 — で is how you get there
   で has a second job and it is the one this module needs: で marks the MEANS — the thing you go BY. 「くるまで いく」 = I go by car. You met で as the place an action happens (「きっさてんで はなす」), and this is the same particle saying the same thing from another angle, because で always means 'using this'. A place is simply a tool you stand inside. A journey sentence often carries both jobs at once and they never collide, since に and へ own the destination while で owns the vehicle: 「くるまで だいがくに いく」. Two things で is NOT. It is not the destination, so 「えきで いく」 is wrong. And walking is not a means at all — you are using nothing, so no で appears anywhere: 「えきまで あるく」, I walk as far as the station.
      · くるまで だいがくに いく。  —  I go to the university by car.
      · ミカは じてんしゃで きっさてんに いく。  —  Mika goes to the coffee shop by bike.
      · トムは ふねで うみへ いく。  —  Tom heads out to the sea by boat.
      · えきまで あるく。  —  I walk as far as the station.
      · くるまで いくから きっぷは かわない。  —  I'm going by car, so I won't buy a ticket.
   NOT: くるまを だいがくに いく。 — を marks a thing you ACT ON, and you are not acting on the car — you are using it, so it takes で: くるまで だいがくに いく。

## Lesson 5 — 「たべに いく」 — going in order to do something (ja-m19-neo-4)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「たべに いく」 — going in order to do something
   To say WHY you are going somewhere, Japanese states the purpose first and the going second. Take the verb for what you will do, cut it back to its ます-stem — the piece that sits in front of ます — and hang に いく on the end. たべます minus ます is たべ, so 「たべに いく」 = I'm going to eat. のみます becomes のみ, giving 「のみに いく」; みます becomes み, giving 「みに いく」. The stem is not a new form and there is nothing extra to memorise: it is the piece you have been attaching ます to since module 7, now standing on its own with に behind it. Activity NOUNS skip the stem and take に directly — 「かいものに いく」, I'm going shopping; 「りょこうに いく」, I'm going travelling. くる and かえる work exactly the same way. The one thing to get right is that the DICTIONARY form never goes there: 「たべるに いく」 is wrong, it is 「たべに いく」.
      · ごはんを たべに いく。  —  I'm going out to eat.
      · ミカは ちゃを のみに きっさてんに いく。  —  Mika is going to the coffee shop to drink tea.
      · えいがを みに いく。  —  I'm going to watch a movie.
      · きょうは かいものに いく。  —  I'm going shopping today.
      · トムは ともだちと あそびに くる。  —  Tom is coming over to hang out with a friend.
   NOT: ごはんを たべるに いく。 — 〜に いく attaches to the ます-STEM, never the dictionary form — たべます minus ます is たべ: ごはんを たべに いく。

## Lesson 6 — 「はちじ じゅっぷん」 — counting minutes (ja-m19-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「はちじ じゅっぷん」 — counting minutes
   Minutes are a number plus the counter for 分, and this is the one counter where the number changes the counter's own sound. Take the two families as they come. After 2, 5, 7 and 9 it stays FUN — nifun, gofun, nanafun, kyuufun. After 1, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 10 it turns into PUN: sanpun (3) and yonpun (4) simply swap the consonant, while ippun (1), roppun (6), happun (8) and juppun (10) also double the sound in front of it. 'How many minutes' is nanpun, in the pun family. Say the list out loud a few times — the mouth learns this far faster than the eye does, and once it is in the mouth you stop thinking about it. ## Lesson 7 — 「くじから はたらく」 — から starts the clock (ja-m19-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「くじから はたらく」 — から starts the clock
   から's third job is the clock. You have used it for a reason (「さむいから いかない」) and for an origin (「にほんから くる」); put a TIME in front of it and it marks the moment something STARTS. 「くじから はたらく」 = I work from nine. That is the whole rule — から is a starting gun, and the thing it starts can be an hour, an hour and minutes, or a part of the day: 「くじ ごふんから」, 「あさから」, 「ばんから」. Give it a まで on the other end and you have the whole stretch, which is the pair module 16 taught you: 「くじから ごじまで」. A start with no end is completely normal on its own — nothing is waiting for the まで. What から never gives you is a deadline. 'From five' and 'by five' are different sentences, and the second one needs までに.
      · くじから ろくじまで はたらく。  —  I work from nine to six.

## Lesson 9 — 「ごじまでに かえる」 — by five, not until five (ja-m19-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ごじまでに かえる」 — by five, not until five
   まで and までに are one syllable apart and they are not the same word. まで is UNTIL: whatever the verb is, it carries on the whole way to that point. 「ごじまで まつ」 = I'll wait until five, and the waiting fills every minute between now and five. までに is BY: the verb happens ONCE, at some moment before the deadline, and nothing says which moment. 「ごじまでに かえる」 = I'll be home by five — it might be four, it might be ten to. The test is the verb itself. Is it something you keep DOING, like まつ, はたらく, いる or みる? Then まで. Is it something that happens once and is over, like かえる, くる, いく or かう? Then までに. And notice what makes the difference: it is the に — the same に that pins a clock time, now pinning a whole deadline to the sentence.
      · ごじまでに うちに かえる。  —  I'll be home by five.
      · くじまでに がっこうに いく。  —  I'll get to school by nine.
      · あしたまでに きっぷを かう。  —  I'll buy the ticket by tomorrow.
      · ろくじまで きっさてんで まつ。  —  I'll wait at the coffee shop until six.
      · はちじ じゅっぷんまでに えきに いく。  —  I'll get to the station by 8:10.
   NOT: ごじにまで うちに かえる。 — the deadline particle is までに — まで with に on the END of it, never に in the middle: ごじまでに うちに かえる。

## Lesson 10 — 「どこへ いく？」 — asking about a journey (ja-m19-neo-8)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「どこへ いく？」 — asking about a journey
   Every piece of a journey has a question word, and asking is easier than you expect: you drop the question word into the slot the answer will occupy and change nothing else. Destination — 「どこに いく？」 or 「どこへ いく？」, where the place becomes どこ and the particle stays put. Means — 「なにで いく？」, where the vehicle becomes なに and で stays put. Time — 「なんじに いく？」, plus 「なんじから？」 and 「なんじまでに？」 for the two ends of a stretch. Duration — 「なんぷん？」. Nothing moves to the front and nothing is added, which is exactly why your listener can answer by saying the very same sentence with the word filled in. ## Lesson 11 — 「えきは どこですか」 — asking a stranger (ja-m19-neo-9)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「えきは どこですか」 — asking a stranger
   There is one situation where this course's plain form is simply the wrong tool, and it is the situation this module is about: stopping a stranger to ask the way. You do not know them and they do not know you, so です and ます are not decoration here — they are the only forms that will not land as rude. The shape is always the same three moves. Open with すみません. Ask with です or ます. Close with ありがとうございます. 「すみません えきは どこですか。」 — excuse me, where is the station? 「バスで いきますか。」 — do I go by bus? Watch the か: a casual question rides its rising contour alone, but a polite question REQUIRES it. Everything else you have learned stays exactly where it is — the particles do not change and the word order does not change, only the last word does. To a friend the same journey is 「えきは どこ？」
      · すみません えきは どこですか。  —  Excuse me, where is the station?
      · びょういんは あそこですか。  —  Is the hospital over there?
      · バスで がっこうに いきますか。  —  Do you go to school by bus?
      · でんしゃは なんじに きますか。  —  What time does the train come?
      · ちかてつで だいがくに いきますか。  —  Do you go to the university by subway?
   NOT: すみません えきは どこです。 — a polite question REQUIRES か — without it the sentence is a statement, and an unfinished one: すみません えきは どこですか。
