# m38 — て + helper II — てしまう/ちゃう + ていく/てくる

Module 38 · JLPT N4

The second helper beat, eight modules after module 30's て + みる/おく — and it opens on the same slot, sixty seconds of rehash before the new resident. しまう behind a て-form wears down to COMPLETELY, and the whole lesson orbits one pair on the SAME verb: ぜんぶ たべてしまった at your own dinner is pride, every bite gone; ともだちの ケーキを たべてしまった is the identical form and it winces — the tell was never the grammar, only what got eaten. Casual speech contracts it to ちゃう/じゃう, drilled as production because that's what your friends actually say back, and it hosts a confession scene: こわしちゃった owns the broken umbrella, こわれちゃった dodges it. Then the trajectory system you've owned since module 19 gets a て behind it: もっていく takes a thing away, もってくる brings it here, つれる does the same job for people — a party-errand sim runs the exchange from both ends, なにを もってくる？ answered with のみものを もっていく, one journey, two anchors. A second read on てくる/ていく trades motion for time: ふえてきた is a rise you've been watching arrive, かわっていく is change still heading on.

## Lesson 1 — 「ぜんぶ たべてしまった」 — done, through, all of it (ja-m38-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ぜんぶ たべてしまった」 — done, through, all of it
   Module 30 taught you the slot: a て-form with an ordinary verb parked behind it, its meaning worn away. Here is the second resident. しまう on its own means to put away, to stow; behind a て-form it wears down to COMPLETELY — done, through, all of it. 「ぜんぶ たべてしまった」: ate it all up, nothing left. 「しゅくだいを してしまった」: homework finished, off the desk, put away. Everything you know about the slot carries over unchanged — glued to the て-form, carries all the tense, stays in kana. What is new is only the flavor: an action taken all the way to its end.
      · ぜんぶ たべてしまった。  —  I ate it all up.
      · しゅくだいを してしまった。  —  I got my homework completely done.
      · この ほんは きょう よんでしまう。  —  I'll finish this book today.
   NOT: ぜんぶ たべるしまった。 — the helper parks behind the て-form, never the dictionary form — the slot's oldest rule: たべて + しまった. たべてしまった.

## Lesson 2 — 「なくしてしまった」 — the same form, wincing (ja-m38-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「なくしてしまった」 — the same form, wincing
   Now the same surface, second reading. When what got completed is a LOSS or a MISTAKE, てしまった stops sounding proud and starts sounding sorry: 「かぎを なくしてしまった」 — I went and lost the key. 「ともだちの ケーキを たべてしまった」 — I ate my friend's cake, and the form itself winces. Nothing in the grammar changed; the TELL is the content. ぜんぶ たべてしまった at your own dinner is satisfaction; the same verb pointed at somebody else's cake is a confession. Japanese leans on this constantly — てしまった is how the language says "it slipped out, it went and happened" — and module 40's passive will give you a second grammar for the same wince.
      · かぎを なくしてしまった。  —  I went and lost the key.
      · ともだちの ケーキを たべてしまった。  —  I (regrettably) ate my friend's cake.
      · でんしゃの なかで ねてしまった。  —  I fell asleep on the train.
   NOT: かぎを なくしてしまいたい。 — the regret reading lives over things that HAPPENED — た-shaped. Wanting to lose the key on purpose (〜たい) contradicts the form's whole wince: なくしてしまった.

## Lesson 3 — 「なくしちゃった」 — what people actually say (ja-m38-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「なくしちゃった」 — what people actually say
   What people actually say. てしまう contracts to ちゃう, でしまう contracts to じゃう, and in casual speech the contraction is the MAJORITY form — たべちゃった for たべてしまった, のんじゃった for のんでしまった, なくしちゃった for なくしてしまった. Both readings come along: completion (ぜんぶ たべちゃった) and the wince (ケーキ たべちゃった…). You have met this move twice: なければ→なきゃ (module 28), ておく→とく (module 30). Same law: the contraction is not slang bolted on, it is the spoken shape of the exact grammar you already own, and you drill it as production because that is what your friends will say and expect back.
      · コーヒーを のんじゃった。  —  I drank the coffee (all of it / oops).
      · ぜんぶ たべちゃった。  —  I ate it all up.
      · かぎを なくしちゃった。  —  I went and lost the key.
   NOT: コーヒーを のんちゃった。 — the contraction follows the て-form's own sound: のんで + しまう fuses to のんじゃう, voiced like its で. のんじゃった.

## Lesson 5 — 「もっていく・もってくる」 — take, and bring (ja-m38-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「もっていく・もってくる」 — take, and bring
   いく and くる have anchored you in space since module 19: いく moves away from here, くる moves toward here. Park them behind a て-form and the anchor comes with them: 「かさを もっていく」 — take the umbrella (carry it AWAY with you); 「かさを もってくる」 — bring it (carry it TOWARD here). For people, Japanese uses つれる: 「いもうとを つれていく」 take my sister along, 「ともだちを つれてくる」 bring a friend. English take/bring is exactly this split, so for once your instinct maps straight on. The helper keeps every slot rule — glued, tense-carrying, kana.
      · かさを もっていく。  —  I'll take an umbrella.
      · あした おかしを もってくる。  —  I'll bring sweets tomorrow.
      · ともだちを つれてくる。  —  I'll bring a friend along.
   NOT: ともだちを もってくる。 — もつ carries THINGS — people are not carried, they are led along: つれてくる. The pair does the same trajectory work, split by what travels.

## Lesson 6 — 「つれてきて」 — people come along, and きて stays きて (ja-m38-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「つれてきて」 — people come along, and きて stays きて
   One bump to smooth while the trajectory system is fresh: くる's て-form is きて — the irregular you have known since module 8 — and it keeps being きて inside every chain built on it. 「もってきて」: bring it here, please (もって + きて + the bare-て request from module 35's ladder). 「つれてきて」: bring them along. Learners who autopilot くって or こって break the only truly irregular verb in the system; say the chain out loud until きて is automatic.

## Lesson 7 — 「さむく なってきた」 — change, arriving and leaving (ja-m38-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「さむく なってきた」 — change, arriving and leaving
   The half of the trajectory system textbooks skip: it works in TIME. てくる is change that has been arriving — up to now, toward the present: 「さむく なってきた」, it has been getting cold (and here we are, in it). ていく is change heading onward — from now, away into the future: 「これから さむく なっていく」, it is going to go on getting colder. Same anchor as space: くる toward here-and-now, いく away from it. Watch prices, weather, your own Japanese: ふえてきた (has been increasing), へっていく (will keep decreasing), かわっていく (will go on changing). Once you hear time flowing through いく and くる, half of written Japanese opens up.
      · さむく なってきた。  —  It's been getting cold.
      · にほんごが じょうずに なってきた。  —  My Japanese has been getting better.
      · まちは かわっていく。  —  The town will go on changing.
   NOT: あしたから さむく なってきた。 — てくる runs UP TO the present — it cannot start tomorrow. Change heading onward takes ていく: あしたから さむく なっていく.
