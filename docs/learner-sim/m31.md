# m31 — Give & receive I — あげる・くれる・もらう

Module 31 · JLPT N4

English has one word for this and Japanese has three, and the thing that picks between them is not politeness — it is which way the present was travelling when it went past you. あげる sends it away from your side, くれる brings it in, and もらう tells the same story from the receiving end. Then the one sentence Japanese will not let you build: あげる can never point back at you. By the end you can say what you gave, what somebody gave you, and what you got — and hear the polite pair, くださる and いただく, without having to produce them yet.

## Lesson 1 — 「あげる」と「くれる」 — one present, two directions (ja-m31-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「あげる」と「くれる」 — one present, two directions
   Japanese has no single verb for give. Which one you reach for depends on WHICH WAY the thing moved past you — not on who is talking, and not on politeness. There is a circle drawn around you and the people who belong to you (うち, inside); everybody else stands そと, outside.
      · ともだちに プレゼントを あげる。  —  I'll give my friend a present.
      · ともだちが プレゼントを くれる。  —  My friend gives me a present.
      · ははは せんせいに はなを あげる。  —  My mother gives the teacher flowers.
      · ちちが おおきい とけいを くれる。  —  My father gives me a big watch.
      · あには がくせいに じしょを あげる。  —  My older brother gives the student a dictionary.
   NOT: ともだちが プレゼントを あげる。 — あげる's arrow leaves your side and can never land back on it, so this sentence never arrives anywhere — a thing coming in from outside takes くれる: ともだちが プレゼントを くれる。

## Lesson 2 — 「せんせいに じしょを もらう」 — the receiver takes the topic (ja-m31-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「せんせいに じしょを もらう」 — the receiver takes the topic
   Third verb, and it rearranges the sentence rather than just pointing it. The subject of もらう is whoever ENDS UP HOLDING THE THING. Then the part that catches everybody: the person it came FROM also takes に. One rule, not two — に marks the OTHER PERSON in the transfer, and the verb decides which end they are standing at. English does the same trick: you give TO somebody, you hear it FROM somebody.
      · せんせいに じしょを もらう。  —  I get a dictionary from the teacher.
      · いしゃに くすりを もらう。  —  I get medicine from the doctor.
      · いしゃに くすりを もらう。  —  I get medicine from the doctor.
      · あねは ともだちに おみやげを もらう。  —  My older sister gets a souvenir from her friend.
      · かいしゃの ひとに おかしを もらう。  —  I get sweets from the people at the company.
   NOT: せんせいは じしょを もらう。 — the subject of もらう is whoever ends up holding the thing, so this says the TEACHER is the one who got a dictionary — if you are the one who received it, you are the subject: せんせいに じしょを もらう。

## Lesson 3 — 「ともだちから もらう」 — に or から, and both are right (ja-m31-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ともだちから もらう」 — に or から, and both are right
   The source of a もらう can be marked に or から, and both are correct: 「ともだちに おみやげを もらう。」 「ともだちから おみやげを もらう。」 Nobody will correct either. から is module 4's word and has always meant the starting point — 「にほんから きた」 — so all that has changed is that a person is now allowed to be one. に is the tighter, more ordinary choice and what you hear most in speech; から is more explicit about the source, and it is what people reach for when the giver is an organisation. There is one case where から is the answer rather than a preference: when the source is not a person at all. 「かいしゃから きねんの とけいを もらう。」 A company is a place as much as a giver, and に there starts to read as a destination. あげる and くれる have no source slot, so から never appears with them — a から is a quiet signal that もらう is coming.
      · ともだちから おみやげを もらう。  —  I get a souvenir from my friend.
      · かいしゃから きねんの とけいを もらう。  —  I get a commemorative watch from the company.
      · せんせいから おいわいの カードを もらう。  —  I get a congratulations card from the teacher.
      · がくせいから てがみを もらう。  —  I get a letter from a student.
      · こんど いしゃから くすりを もらう。  —  I'll get medicine from the doctor next time.
   NOT: ともだちから プレゼントを あげる。 — から marks where a thing came from and あげる has no source slot — only もらう can take a から: ともだちから プレゼントを もらう。

## Lesson 5 — ×「わたしに あげる」 — the one thing あげる cannot do (ja-m31-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — ×「わたしに あげる」 — the one thing あげる cannot do
   Here is the one hard ban in this module. あげる CANNOT POINT AT YOU. 「わたしに プレゼントを あげる」 is not awkward or impolite — it is broken, the way I gave me a present is broken in English, and a Japanese speaker will not work out what you meant. あげる's arrow leaves your circle, so there is nowhere for it to land if the destination is inside the circle it just left. The moment a thing is arriving at you, あげる is off the table and you pick between two verbs — and the choice is about what the sentence is ABOUT, not about the event. くれる if the giver is the news: 「ともだちが プレゼントを くれる。」 もらう if what you ended up with is the news: 「ともだちに プレゼントを もらう。」 The circle stretches too, and your family is inside it. 「ともだちが いもうとに おかしを くれる」 is fine — the sweets came in to my side even though they did not come to my hand. Say あげる there and you have pushed your own sister outside the circle.
      · ともだちが わたしに プレゼントを くれる。  —  My friend gives me a present.
      · ともだちに プレゼントを もらう。  —  I get a present from my friend.
      · せんせいが いもうとに おかしを くれる。  —  The teacher gives my little sister sweets.
      · がくせいに ノートを あげる。  —  I'll give the student a notebook.
      · ともだちが ケーキを くれるから うれしい。  —  My friend is giving me a cake, so I'm happy.
   NOT: ともだちが わたしに プレゼントを あげる。 — あげる's arrow leaves your side and can never land back on you — a thing arriving at you takes くれる: ともだちが わたしに プレゼントを くれる。

## Lesson 6 — 「もらった」「くれた」「あげた」 — after the present is handed over (ja-m31-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「もらった」「くれた」「あげた」 — after the present is handed over
   Put the three verbs in the past and the module becomes usable, because most of what anybody says about a gift is said afterwards. The forms are module 11's. あげる and くれる are る-verbs: drop る, add た — あげた, くれた. もらう is an う-verb ending in う, so it takes かう's った row: もらった. Nothing new to memorise. What IS worth watching is that the direction does not soften in the past. 「ともだちが けいたいを くれた」 still points in at me; 「ともだちに けいたいを あげた」 still points out. English collapses both back to gave, so say the arrow to yourself before you pick the verb. 「もらった」 is the most useful of the three, because it answers the question the past tense invites: 「その とけい、いいね。」 「たんじょうびに ちちに もらった。」 Nice watch — I got it from my father for my birthday.

## Lesson 9 — 「せんせいが くださった」 — the polite pair you only need to recognise (ja-m31-neo-9)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「せんせいが くださった」 — the polite pair you only need to recognise
   Two more words, and for now you only have to RECOGNISE them. When the giver is somebody you look up to — a teacher, a customer, somebody much older — Japanese swaps the verb rather than adding a polite ending. くれる becomes くださる, もらう becomes いただく. 「せんせいが じしょを くださった。」 The teacher was kind enough to give me a dictionary. 「せんせいに じしょを いただいた。」 I received a dictionary from the teacher. Notice the axis has not moved an inch: くださる points in at me exactly like くれる, and いただく makes the receiver the subject exactly like もらう. Respect is a layer sitting on top of direction, which is why direction comes first. Meet them now because you will hear them constantly and cannot derive them — no rule you own gets you from くれる to くださった. When you are the one speaking, keep using くれる and もらう. Nobody will think you rude.
      · せんせいが じしょを くださった。  —  The teacher was kind enough to give me a dictionary.
      · せんせいに じしょを いただいた。  —  I received a dictionary from the teacher.
      · たなかさんが おいわいの はなたばを くださる。  —  Tanaka is kind enough to give me a congratulatory bouquet.

   NOT: ともだちが おかしを くださった。 — くださる is for somebody you look up to, so using it of a friend puts a stranger between you — a friend takes くれる: ともだちが おかしを くれた。

## Lesson 10 — 「わたしは」 が きえる — the verb has already said who (ja-m31-neo-10)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「わたしは」 が きえる — the verb has already said who
   Because くれる and もらう already say which way the thing moved, Japanese stops mentioning you. 「ともだちが けいたいを くれた。」 There is no わたしに in that sentence and there does not need to be — くれる cannot point anywhere else. Same with もらう: 「ちちに おかねを もらった」 has no わたしは, because the subject of もらう is the speaker unless somebody else is named. This is not an abbreviation of a longer sentence; it is the payoff for having direction built into the verb. And it works the other way round. If you DO say わたしは, you are contrasting: 「わたしは かさを もらった」 lands as I got an umbrella, whatever everyone else got — module 3's は marking a topic against a background. So when you hear くれる with no receiver, the receiver is the speaker or somebody on their side; when you hear もらう with no subject, the subject is the speaker.
      · ともだちが けいたいを くれた。  —  My friend gave me a mobile phone.
      · ちちに おかねを もらった。  —  I got money from my father.
      · わたしは かさを もらった。  —  I got an umbrella.
      · いもうとが ケーキを くれたから うれしかった。  —  My little sister gave me a cake, so I was happy.
      · ともだちに かさを かりた。  —  I borrowed an umbrella from my friend.
   NOT: ともだちが わたしに けいたいを あげた。 — くれる is what carries a thing to your side, and it carries it without needing わたしに at all: ともだちが けいたいを くれた。

## Lesson 11 — 「あげたい」「もらいたい」 — wanting to give, wanting to get (ja-m31-neo-11)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「あげたい」「もらいたい」 — wanting to give, wanting to get
   You have had 〜たい since module 13 and it works on these three verbs unchanged: drop る or う, add たい. あげたい. もらいたい. What is worth a lesson is that wanting cuts across the direction axis rather than sitting on it. 「せんせいに はなたばを あげたい。」 I want to give the teacher a bouquet — the arrow still points out, and たい only says I have not done it yet. 「たんじょうびに ケーキを もらいたい。」 I want to get a cake for my birthday — still the receiver's side, still もらう's shape. There is one gap worth knowing about now rather than being surprised by later: 〜たい is for YOUR OWN wanting. You cannot use it to say what somebody else wants, which is why every example here is about you. And くれる has no たい at all, because it would mean wanting somebody else to do something — a different form entirely, and not this module's.
      · せんせいに はなたばを あげたい。  —  I want to give the teacher a bouquet.
      · たんじょうびに ケーキを もらいたい。  —  I want to get a cake for my birthday.
      · がくせいに ノートを あげたい。  —  I want to give the student a notebook.
      · はなが すきだから はなたばを もらいたい。  —  I like flowers, so I want to get a bouquet.
      · ともだちに おれいの カードを あげたい。  —  I want to give my friend a thank-you card.
   NOT: せんせいに はなたばを あげる たい。 — 〜たい attaches to the verb stem, not to the finished dictionary form — あげる loses its る before たい: せんせいに はなたばを あげたい。
