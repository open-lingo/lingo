# m30 — Casual register

Module 30 · Casual

Casual questions, よ/ね, casual の, register awareness, invitations, and mixed-register drills — when plain form is (and isn't) socially correct.

## Lesson 1 — Casual questions — Intro (ja-m30-1-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Casual questions — drop か, let the rise carry it
   Polite ます-questions keep か (たべますか). Casual questions among friends drop か entirely — the plain form alone, said with rising intonation (written ？), IS the question: たべる？ (Gonna eat this?), いく？ (Coming?). Plain non-past asks about intent, habit, or the future — for a happening-RIGHT-NOW question Japanese uses 〜てる instead (してる？, later this module). Tacking か onto plain form in casual speech (たべるか？) reads blunt, like an interrogation — the taught casual pattern relies on the rise alone, not か.
      · あした がっこうに くる？  —  Are you coming to school tomorrow?
      · コーヒーを のむ？  —  Gonna drink coffee?
      · きょう ひま？  —  Are you free today?
   NOT: あした がっこうに くるか？ — か marks a question in polite/formal registers (きますか). Adding it back onto a casual plain-form question among friends breaks the register this pair teaches — the rise alone (くる？) is the natural casual pattern.
   Note: This is the single most common casual-speech move: drop か, keep the ？. Almost every casual question you'll hear from friends follows this shape.

## Lesson 3 — よ / ね — Intro (ja-m30-2-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — よ vs ね — new information vs shared agreement
   よ and ね do ONE job each. よ asserts something the LISTENER doesn't already know — you're informing them. ね checks or invites agreement on something you assume the listener already feels or knows too — you're confirming, not informing. Pick よ when you're the one with new information; pick ね when you expect the listener to already agree.
      · この みせは やすいよ。  —  This shop is cheap, you know! (telling you)
      · この みせは やすいね。  —  This shop is cheap, isn't it? (agreeing)
      · かばん、わすれたよ！  —  You forgot your bag! (heads up)
   NOT: この みせは やすいね。 — The speaker is asserting NEW information the listener doesn't already know — that calls for よ. ね would wrongly assume the listener already shares that awareness, undercutting the whole point of telling them.
   Note: よ and ね stack onto almost every casual sentence-ender. Mixing up their function is a classic 'off' feeling even when every other part of the sentence is perfect.
2. [build_sentence] Say to a friend, casually, telling them something new: This shop is cheap!

## Lesson 5 — Casual の question — Intro (ja-m30-3-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Casual の question + してる (している contraction)
   Casual speech contracts ANY -ている form by dropping the い: している → してる, はなしている → はなしてる, つかれている → つかれてる — same phonological drop, every verb. Adding の to the end of a casual question softens it into a curious, personal-interest tone: なにしてるの？ ('What are you up to?') sounds warmer than the bare なにしてる？. Pattern: [casual verb/adjective] + の？
      · なにしてるの？  —  What are you doing?
      · どこ いくの？  —  Where are you going?
      · だれと はなしてるの？  —  Who are you talking with?
   NOT: なにしていますのか？ — の already casual-softens the question; piling ています (polite) and か (polite question marker) on top of it collides two registers in one sentence instead of picking one.
   Note: This casual の is a question-softener, not the possession の from earlier modules — same kana, completely different job depending on where it lands in the sentence.

## Lesson 7 — Register awareness — Intro (ja-m30-4-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — When casual is wrong — register awareness
   Casual speech (plain form, dropped か, よ/ね, の) is for なかま / おさななじみ / したしい ともだち — people you're close with as equals. With せんぱい, じょうし, and people you've just met (しりあい), casual speech reads as しつれい (rude) — use ていねい polite ます-form / けいご instead. どうりょう and こうはい often start polite and shift casual once closeness is established.
      · したしい ともだちに: あした くる？  —  To a close friend: Are you coming tomorrow?
      · じょうしに: あした きますか？  —  To your boss: Are you coming tomorrow?
      · せんぱいに ためぐちを つかうと、しつれいです。  —  Using casual speech with a senior is rude.
   NOT: じょうしに: あした くる？ — The sentence is grammatically perfect casual Japanese — the error is social, not grammatical. Casual register signals closeness/equal footing; aiming it upward at a じょうし breaks the expected hierarchy and reads as rude.
   Note: Every sentence in this pair is grammatically correct either way. The mistake this pair teaches is entirely about WHO you're talking to, not how well-formed the sentence is.

## Lesson 9 — Casual invitations — Intro (ja-m30-5-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 〜ない？ — casual invitations (same logic as ませんか)
   Casual invitations use the plain ない-form + rising intonation, dropping か exactly like pair 1's casual questions: いかない？ ('Wanna go?'). This mirrors the polite invitation pattern from m23 — ませんか (いきませんか, 'won't you go?') — both use a NEGATIVE question to soften a suggestion into an invitation, giving the listener room to decline. Casual just drops the か the same way every casual question does.
      · いっしょに たべない？  —  Wanna eat together?
      · えいが みない？  —  Wanna watch a movie?
      · しゅうまつ あそばない？  —  Wanna hang out this weekend?
   NOT: いっしょに たべないか？ — Casual invitations rely on rising intonation alone, exactly like casual questions (pair 1) — adding か back breaks the register even though ない makes it an invitation rather than a plain question.
   Note: This is invitation logic doubled: the negative form softens a suggestion into an invitation (as in ませんか), and casual speech drops か (as in pair 1). Put them together and you get いかない？ — the single most common way friends invite each other to do something.
