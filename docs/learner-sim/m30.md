# m30 — て + helper I — 〜てみる / 〜ておく

Module 30 · JLPT N4 begins

The N4 line opens on something you already own. A て-form can have a second verb parked behind it — and that verb, whose own meaning has worn away to almost nothing, takes all the endings while the first one never moves again. 「たべてみる」 is eat it and see; 「たべてみた」 puts the past on the helper. Then the same slot with a different verb in it: 「よやくを しておく」 books it in advance, 「でんきを つけておく」 leaves the light on. And the trap worth naming — 〜てみる is give-it-a-go, never try-and-fail.

## Lesson 1 — 「たべてみる」 — eat it and see (ja-m30-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「たべてみる」 — eat it and see
   You have had the て-form since module 8 and it has always been the front half of something — 〜てください, 〜ている, 〜てから. Here is what it actually is. A て-form is an unfinished verb, and what finishes it can be another ORDINARY VERB parked behind it. The first one is みる. 「たべてみる」 is たべて with みる sitting behind it, and みる is the verb you already know — to look, to watch. Except that in this position its meaning has been worn away almost to nothing. What is left is the idea of FINDING OUT. So 「たべてみる」 is not eat and look at it. It is eat it and see: do the thing, and find out what it is like. Two facts about the shape, and they are the whole module. First, the main verb never moves again. Once it is in the て-form it is finished — たべて will look exactly like that in every sentence you ever build with it. Second, the HELPER carries everything. Tense, negation, politeness: all of it lands on みる and nowhere else. That is why this is worth a module rather than a footnote. You are not learning an expression, you are learning a SLOT, and later modules drop different verbs into that same slot. Nothing else in the sentence notices. Whatever it had — a topic, an object with を, a place with に — stays exactly where it was: 「この りょうりを たべる」 becomes 「この りょうりを たべてみる」 and only the end of the verb changed. One writing note. The helper is joined to the て-form with no gap, because the two of them are one predicate — and that is also why you see it in kana: a verb doing this job stops being written in its own kanji.
      · この りょうりを たべてみる。  —  I'll give this dish a try.
      · とりあえず コーヒーを のんでみる。  —  I'll have some coffee for a start and see.
      · わからないから せんせいに きいてみる。  —  I don't understand, so I'll ask the teacher and see.
      · この カメラを つかってみる。  —  I'll give this camera a try.
      · じかんが あるから としょかんに いってみる。  —  I've got time, so I'll go to the library and see.
   NOT: この りょうりを たべるみる。 — the helper attaches to the て-form and never to the dictionary form — the main verb has to be たべて before みる can sit behind it: この りょうりを たべてみる。

## Lesson 2 — 「のんでみた」 — the helper carries the tense (ja-m30-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「のんでみた」 — the helper carries the tense
   Now watch which half of the sentence moves. 「この りょうりを たべてみる」 says I'll give this dish a try. To say you already gave it a try you put the た-form on みる — 「この りょうりを たべてみた」 — and たべて does not change at all. That is the rule the rest of this course is built on: the HELPER carries the tense, and the main verb, once it is in the て-form, is finished for good. It feels wrong at first, because the pastness belongs to the eating and Japanese is putting the mark on a verb that barely means anything. Turn it round and you can see why it has to be so. たべて is not a tense, it is a JOIN — there is nothing on it to make past. So the tense goes to the only verb still able to carry one, which is the one at the end. That will be true of every helper you meet between here and the end of N4, and it is true of the negative and of politeness as well. The た-form itself is module 11's and has not changed: みる is a る-verb, so it drops る and adds た, giving みた. Watch out for the wrong answer your ear will want, though, because it is a good guess and it is wrong: 「たべたみる」 puts the past on the main verb, which is exactly the half that cannot take one. One more thing worth noticing: because the helper carries everything, a sentence can be past and still not be about the past. 「さいしょに のんでみた」 tells somebody where you started.
      · さいしょに この コーヒーを のんでみた。  —  I gave this coffee a try first.
      · おもしろい りょうりを たべてみた。  —  I gave an interesting dish a try.
      · せんせいに きいてみたけど だめだった。  —  I asked the teacher and saw, but it was no good.
      · かわで およいでみた。  —  I gave the river a swim.
      · さいしょに この えいごの うたを きいてみた。  —  I gave this English song a listen first.
   NOT: さいしょに この コーヒーを のんだみた。 — the tense belongs to the HELPER and the main verb stays in the て-form — のんだ cannot sit in front of a helper: さいしょに この コーヒーを のんでみた。

## Lesson 3 — 「しらべてみる」 — drop る, add て (ja-m30-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「しらべてみる」 — drop る, add て
   A slot only pays out if you can put verbs into it, and this module brings five new ones. Three are る-verbs, which is the easy half of module 8's table, so here is the whole rule again in one line: drop る, add て. しらべる gives しらべて. きめる gives きめて. つづける gives つづけて. Nothing else happens — no sound change, no exception, the same move you already make for たべる and みる. The three verbs are worth having on their own account. しらべる is to look something up or check it: a dictionary, a timetable, a price. きめる is to decide, and it is what Japanese reaches for where English says I'll go with that. つづける is to keep something going — a class, a job, a habit. Once each one is in its て-form the helper goes straight behind it, joined up, with no gap: 「じしょで しらべてみる」, I'll look it up in the dictionary and see. Notice what that sentence is doing, because it is not the tautology it looks like. しらべる already means find out and みる also means find out, but the helper is not repeating it — it adds the sense of GIVING IT A GO, of not being certain it will work. That shade is what you are buying every time you spend a helper, and it is why 「しらべてみる」 sounds tentative where 「しらべる」 sounds settled.
      · じしょで しらべてみる。  —  I'll look it up in the dictionary and see.
      · わからないから えいごで しらべてみる。  —  I don't understand, so I'll look it up in English and see.
      · あした きめてみる。  —  I'll have a go at deciding tomorrow.
      · むずかしいけど じゅぎょうを つづけてみる。  —  It's difficult, but I'll keep the class going and see.
      · ことばを じしょで しらべてみた。  —  I looked the word up in the dictionary and saw.
   NOT: じしょで しらべるみる。 — a る-verb drops る and adds て before a helper can attach — しらべる has to become しらべて first: じしょで しらべてみる。

## Lesson 5 — 「よやくを しておく」 — get it out of the way (ja-m30-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「よやくを しておく」 — get it out of the way
   Second helper, same slot, and this is the one that proves the slot is real. Park おく behind a て-form instead of みる and the meaning changes completely. おく on its own means to put something down, to set it somewhere. Bleached into this position what survives is the LEAVING: do the thing now, and leave the world in the state where it is done. 「よやくを しておく」 is I'll book it in advance — not merely I'll book it, but I'll get the booking out of the way so that later it is already there. That gives two senses, and they are one idea seen from either end. Looking forward, ておく is PREPARATION: you do it now because of something coming later. 「じゅんびを しておく。」 「きっぷを かっておく。」 Looking backward, ておく is LEAVING AS IS: something is already how you want it, so you do not undo it. 「でんきを つけておく」 is leave the light on. Everything you learned about the shape last week still holds, unchanged, and that is the whole return on learning a slot instead of an expression: the main verb stays in its て-form, the helper takes the tense, and おく is a う-verb, so its past is おいた. 「よやくを しておいた」 — I'd booked it in advance. Nothing in front of the verb moves at all. One caution: leave 「〜ておかない」 alone for now. ておく is about leaving something DONE, and its natural opposite is simply not doing the thing.

## Lesson 6 — 「かえる まえに でんきを けしておく」 — before, and left as it is (ja-m30-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「かえる まえに でんきを けしておく」 — before, and left as it is
   ておく almost never travels alone, because preparing is preparing FOR something and the something usually gets said. Module 15 gave you 〜まえに, before, and it is the natural partner: the deadline clause comes first, まえに closes it, and the preparation comes last. 「かえる まえに でんきを けしておく。」 「じゅぎょうの まえに ことばを おぼえておく。」 Two mechanical reminders, because both come back to bite. A VERB in front of まえに stays in the plain non-past however far in the past the whole sentence is — かえる まえに, never かえった まえに. A NOUN in front of it takes の — じゅぎょうの まえに, never じゅぎょう まえに. This is also where the second sense of ておく earns its keep. 「でんきを つけておく」 is leave the light on: you are not preparing anything, you are declining to undo something. Both senses come out of one word because both are about the STATE the world is left in, and Japanese does not split them. English does, which is why the two halves feel unrelated until you stop translating and start picturing the room afterwards. Preparation and leaving-as-is are the same sentence pointed at two different moments.

## Lesson 7 — 「たべてみた」 is not 「I tried to eat it」 (ja-m30-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「たべてみた」 is not 「I tried to eat it」
   Here is the one thing 〜てみる does not do, and it is the mistake almost every learner makes. It does NOT mean try TO. 「たべてみた」 means I gave it a taste. It does not mean I attempted to eat it and could not. The difference is that English uses one word, try, for two completely different ideas: making an ATTEMPT at something that might fail, and giving something a GO to find out what it is like. Japanese splits them, and 〜てみる is only ever the second. So 「およいでみた」 is I had a swim to see what it was like, never I tried to swim and sank. There is a separate shape for attempting and it arrives at module 34; until then, if the point of your sentence is that the thing did not come off, do not reach for 〜てみる. There is a test that always works. If you can say and see after the English verb and the sentence still means what you meant, 〜てみる is right. Ask and see. Taste and see. Have a look and see. If you cannot — if the point is that the thing was HARD, or that it FAILED — then this is not the shape you want. That is also why 〜てみる suits the words in this module so well. 「しつもんを して こたえを きいてみる」, ask the question and see what the answer is. You are not attempting anything. You are finding out.

## Lesson 9 — 「ならってみる」「おくっておく」 — the う-verbs' turn (ja-m30-neo-9)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ならってみる」「おくっておく」 — the う-verbs' turn
   The other two new verbs are う-verbs, which is the half of module 8's table with the sound changes in it. Here is what actually matters: the LAST kana of the dictionary form picks the ending, and nothing else does. ならう ends in う, and う・つ・る all give って — so ならって. おくる ends in る, which LOOKS like a る-verb and is not one, and it lands on the same って — so おくって. Two different final kana, one row of the table. That is worth seeing side by side, because it is the row you will hit most often and it is the row that catches people, since a verb ending in る is not automatically a る-verb. The verbs themselves. ならう is to learn something FROM somebody — a language, an instrument, a skill you are being taught — and it takes に for the person teaching you: 「せんせいに ならう」. It is not the same as おぼえる, which is the memorising you do on your own. おくる is to send: a letter, a photograph, something that travels to somebody, and it takes に for whoever it goes to. Both drop straight into the slot once they are in the て-form, and both are joined up with no gap: 「せんせいに ならってみる」, I'll take lessons from the teacher and see how it goes. 「しゃしんを おくっておく」, I'll send the photo in advance.

      · おんがくを ならってみたけど だめだった。  —  I took music lessons and saw, but it was no good.
      · あした つかうから てがみを おくっておいた。  —  I'm using it tomorrow, so I sent the letter in advance.
      · まいにち れんしゅうを つづけてみる。  —  I'll keep the practice going every day and see.

## Lesson 10 — 「わからないから」か「あした あるから」か — which helper the reason picks (ja-m30-neo-10)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「わからないから」か「あした あるから」か — which helper the reason picks
   Two helpers, one slot, and the question you will actually be asking yourself is which one. The answer is almost always sitting in the から clause, so this lesson is about listening to the reason. If the reason is that you DO NOT KNOW something, the helper is みる. 「わからないから じしょで しらべてみる。」 You look it up because the answer is not in your head yet, and the whole point of the sentence is finding out. If the reason is that something is COMING, the helper is おく. 「あした じゅぎょうが あるから ことばを おぼえておく。」 You learn the words because a class is on its way, and the point of the sentence is being ready for it. Ignorance takes みる. A deadline takes おく. Everything module 16 taught about から is unchanged: the reason clause comes FIRST, から goes on its end, and the main clause comes last — the reverse of English, which puts because in the middle. What is new is only that the main clause now ends in a helper. There is a third case worth knowing, and it is the one where both are true: you want to know AND you will need it later. Japanese lets you pick whichever is the point and neither is wrong — the reason simply tells the listener which one you cared about.
      · わからないから じしょで しらべてみる。  —  I don't understand, so I'll look it up in the dictionary and see.
      · あした じゅぎょうが あるから ことばを おぼえておく。  —  I've got a class tomorrow, so I'll learn the words in advance.
      · だいがくが ちかいから じてんしゃに のってみる。  —  The university is near, so I'll give the bicycle a go.
      · らいげつ りょこうが あるから じゅんびを しておく。  —  I've got a trip next month, so I'll get ready in advance.
      · せつめいが むずかしいから せんせいに はなしてみる。  —  The explanation is difficult, so I'll talk to the teacher and see.
   NOT: わからない じしょで しらべてみる。 — the reason clause needs から on its end — without it the two halves are just two sentences pushed together: わからないから じしょで しらべてみる。

## Lesson 11 — 「たべてみない？」 — won't you give it a go? (ja-m30-neo-11)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「たべてみない？」 — won't you give it a go?
   The helper takes the negative too, and the negative is where 〜てみる stops being about you and starts being about the person in front of you. みる is a る-verb, so module 6's rule applies unchanged: drop る, add ない. みる gives みない. 「たべてみない」 is I'm not going to give it a try — and the main verb, as always, does not move. Now say it with the rising question contour module 3 taught you and it stops being a refusal. 「たべてみない？」 is won't you give it a try? That is an INVITATION, and it is one of the most useful sentences in casual Japanese, because it offers somebody an experience without pushing them into it. The reason it is gentle is structural rather than cultural: the whole meaning of 〜てみる is that nothing is at stake — you are only finding out. Compare 「たべない？」, which asks whether they are eating, with 「たべてみない？」, which asks whether they fancy giving it a go. One more skin worth recognising while you are here. Because the helper carries EVERYTHING, it carries politeness as well: 「たべてみます」 to somebody you owe it to, 「たべてみる」 to a friend. You already own ます, so there is nothing new to learn — which is exactly what learning a slot buys you.
      · この りょうりを たべてみない？  —  Won't you give this dish a try?
      · とりあえず この コーヒーを のんでみない？  —  Won't you give this coffee a try for a start?
