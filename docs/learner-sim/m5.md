# m5 — ⚙️ Verbs I: the dictionary form

Module 5 · Rewrite

A bare dictionary verb is a whole casual sentence: たべる。 Add を to say what you act on; meet the CEJC seed verbs and the もの word family.

4. [speaking] Say this in Japanese: I'll watch.
5. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — The dictionary form IS the verb
   たべる = 'I'll eat' all on its own — a verb is already a whole sentence. Back in m3 a NOUN needed だ to finish (ねこだ, 'it's a cat'); a verb never does. It stays たべる, never たべるだ. Raise the tone — たべる？ — and it's a question, m3-style. (たべる and みる are る-verbs — that'll matter when we start bending verbs soon.)
      · たべる。  —  I'll eat.
      · ねこだ。  —  It's a cat. (a NOUN takes だ)
      · たべる？  —  Gonna eat? (voice rises)
   NOT: たべるだ。 — だ finishes a NOUN — ねこだ, ほんだ. A verb is already finished, so たべるだ jams two endings together. Just たべる.
   Note: Who eats? Whoever the moment points at — say it flat and it's usually you; aim it at a friend with a rise and it's them. Japanese trusts the room; no 'I' or 'you' needed.

## Lesson 2 — を — marking what you act on (ja-m5-neo-2)

1. [word_image_mcq] Which word means this picture? (rice / a meal)
   A) 🎩 ぼうし   B) 🍚 ごはん   C) 👓 めがね   D) 📸 しゃしん
2. [speaking] Say this in Japanese: rice / a meal
3. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — を — the action's target
   を marks the thing the verb acts on: ごはんを たべる = 'I'll eat the rice.' Order is the skeleton of Japanese — thing + を, verb LAST, always.
      · ごはんを たべる。  —  I'll eat the rice.
      · しゃしんを みる。  —  I'll look at the photo.
   NOT: たべるを ごはん。 — を rides on the THING, never the verb — and the verb closes the sentence. Thing + を + verb: ごはんを たべる.
   Note: Written を, said 'o' — this kana has exactly one job in the whole language, and this is it (same deal as は saying 'wa').

## Lesson 4 — いく・くる — off and coming (ja-m5-neo-4)

2. [speaking] Say this in Japanese: I'm off.
3. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — いく — away, くる — toward
   いく = moving AWAY from here; くる = moving TOWARD here. Bare, they're complete: いく。 'I'm off.' くる？ 'you coming?' No place-word needed yet — that machinery arrives later. (いく is a う-verb. くる is one of Japanese's only TWO irregular verbs — remember its face; it bends strangely later.)
      · いく。  —  I'm off.
      · くる？  —  You coming? (voice rises)
      · うん、くる。  —  Yeah — I'm coming.
   Note: The anchor is wherever the SPEAKER stands: your いく is their くる. Phone a friend from home — くる？ — and you've pulled the anchor to your side.

## Lesson 5 — する・やる — do (ja-m5-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — する・やる — the all-purpose 'do'
   する = do, the verb for everything that has no verb of its own: なにを する？ 'what are you gonna do?' やる is the same 'do' in street clothes — between friends it's everywhere. (する is the OTHER of Japanese's two irregular verbs — remember its face, like くる.)
      · なにを する？  —  What are you gonna do?
      · これを する。  —  I'll do this one.
      · これを やる。  —  I'll do this one. (casual)
   Note: やる ranks among the most-spoken verbs in real Japanese conversation — casual talk reaches for it constantly. Both are fine between friends; する is the one that also wears a suit.
