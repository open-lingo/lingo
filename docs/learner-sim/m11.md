## Lesson 2 — Eleven to ninety-nine (ja-m11-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Eleven to ninety-nine
   Past ten, Japanese counts by ARITHMETIC. Stack a digit on the RIGHT of じゅう and you add: じゅう + ご = じゅうご (15). Stack a digit on the LEFT and you multiply: さん + じゅう = さんじゅう (30), よん + じゅう = よんじゅう (40), ご + じゅう = ごじゅう (50), なな + じゅう = ななじゅう (70). Combine both for everything in between: ろくじゅうご = 65. There is nothing new to memorise — you already know every piece.
      · じゅうご  —  fifteen
      · さんじゅう  —  thirty
      · ろくじゅうご  —  sixty-five
   NOT: いちじゅう — ten on its own is just じゅう — いちじゅう is not a number

## Lesson 4 — At three o'clock — 〜じに (ja-m11-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — At three o'clock — 〜じに
   A point on the CLOCK takes に: さんじに いく. It is the same に you already use for a destination — Japanese treats a moment in time like a place you arrive at. RELATIVE time words — きょう, あした, いま — take NO に at all: きょう いく, あした いく. (A named point on the calendar takes に the way a clock time does; those arrive later.) ごぜん (a.m.) and ごご (p.m.) sit in front of the hour: ごご さんじに.
      · さんじに いく。  —  I'll go at three.
      · くじに たべる。  —  I eat at nine.
   NOT: さんじ いく。 — a clock time needs に — さんじに いく

## Lesson 5 — The week — 〜ようび (ja-m11-neo-10)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — The week — 〜ようび
   Every day of the week ends in 〜ようび, and the piece in front is an element you can picture: げつようび Monday (げつ is the moon of つき), かようび Tuesday (fire), すいようび Wednesday (water — みず), もくようび Thursday (wood — き, the tree), きんようび Friday (gold), どようび Saturday (earth), にちようび Sunday (the sun). A day name is a NAMED point on the calendar, so it takes に like a clock time: どようびに いく. Two days joined by と share ONE に at the end of the pair: げつようびと かようびに はたらく — never a に on each day. あさって — the day after tomorrow — is RELATIVE like きょう and あした, so it takes no に at all.
      · げつようび かようび すいようび もくようび  —  Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday
      · きんようび どようび にちようび  —  Friday, Saturday, Sunday
      · どようびに みせに いく。  —  I'll go to the shop on Saturday.
      · げつようびと かようびに はたらく。  —  I work on Monday and Tuesday.
      · あさって うみに いく。  —  I'll go to the sea the day after tomorrow.
   NOT: あさってに いく。 — あさって is relative, like きょう and あした — it takes no に; only a NAMED day does (どようびに)

## Lesson 7 — た — the same table, a second payout (ja-m11-neo-4)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — た — the same table, a second payout
   Plain past is た, and it runs on the SAME sound-change table you bought for て. Swap て for た and で for だ, and you are done. る-verbs: たべる → たべた. う-verbs: む/ぶ/ぬ → んだ (のむ → のんだ); う/つ/る → った (かう → かった); く → いた (きく → きいた); ぐ → いだ. する → した, くる → きた, and いく is the same rebel it was for て: いった. A past sentence usually opens with the time it happened: きのう is 'yesterday', so きのう のんだ is 'I drank it yesterday'.
      · みずを のんだ。  —  I drank water.
      · ごはんを たべた。  —  I ate rice.
      · うたを きいた。  —  I listened to a song.
      · みせに いった。  —  I went to the shop.
   NOT: みずを のみた。 — のむ ends in む, so it joins the んだ group — のんだ, not のみた

## Lesson 16 — This year, last year — the wider calendar (ja-m11-neo-11)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — This year, last year — the wider calendar
   The wider calendar stacks from pieces. せん points back: せんしゅう is last week, せんげつ last month. らい points forward: らいげつ is next month. まい means every: まいとし is every year. ことし is this year and きょねん last year. All of them are RELATIVE words like きょう and あした, so none of them takes に: せんしゅう かった, らいげつ いく. One more calendar word rides along: はじめて means 'for the first time' and sits in front of the verb phrase — はじめて たべた is 'I ate it for the first time'.
      · せんしゅう せんげつ きょねん  —  last week, last month, last year
      · ことし らいげつ まいとし  —  this year, next month, every year
