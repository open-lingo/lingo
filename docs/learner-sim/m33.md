# m33 — Transitivity I — 自動詞/他動詞, が vs を

Module 33 · JLPT N4

The wall everyone hits, taken down one brick at a time. Japanese has two verbs where English has one: ドアが あく is the door opening, ドアを あける is you opening it. They are not two forms of one word — they are two different verbs, and the particle in front tells you which one you are looking at every single time. Nine pairs, and five of them you already own half of. Then the payoff: 自動詞 + ている, the shape that lets you walk into a room and say what state you found it in.

## Lesson 1 — 「ドアが あく」と「ドアを あける」 — two verbs, one door (ja-m33-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ドアが あく」と「ドアを あける」 — two verbs, one door
   Japanese verbs come in two kinds. Some describe something HAPPENING — the door opens. Others describe somebody DOING it — I open the door. English uses "open" for both; Japanese uses two verbs. あく is the door opening, あける is you opening it. They are not two forms of one word — they are separate entries about the same event from two sides, the way English has "rise" and "raise". And the particle in front always tells you which: the happening verb takes が, the doing verb takes を. ドアが あく。 ドアを あける。
      · ドアが あく。  —  The door opens.
      · ドアを あける。  —  I open the door.
      · まどが あく。  —  The window opens.
      · まどを あける。  —  I open the window.
      · ミカが ドアを あける。  —  Mika opens the door.
   NOT: ドアを あく。 — あく is the door opening by itself, so nobody is doing anything to it and を has no job — が is the only particle it takes: ドアが あく。

## Lesson 2 — 「まどが しまる」と「まどを しめる」 — read the particle, both ways (ja-m33-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「まどが しまる」と「まどを しめる」 — read the particle, both ways
   The second pair, same shape: しまる is the door closing, しめる is you closing it. There is nothing to memorise about which is which — you read it off the sentence. Find the noun in front of the verb and look at its particle. が means it happened. を means somebody did it. It works in both directions: given 「まどが しまる」 you know nobody closed it, and to say "I closed the window" you know you need を, and therefore しめる. Learners who struggle with pairs are trying to FEEL the difference. Do not feel it. Read the particle.
      · ドアが しまる。  —  The door closes.
      · ドアを しめる。  —  I close the door.
      · まどが しまる。  —  The window closes.
      · まどを しめる。  —  I close the window.
      · ミカが まどを しめる。  —  Mika closes the window.
   NOT: まどが しめる。 — しめる needs somebody doing the closing, and が says nobody is — either say 「まどが しまる」 (it closes) or 「まどを しめる」 (I close it).

## Lesson 3 — 「へやに はいる」と「かばんに ほんを いれる」 — the doer arrives (ja-m33-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「へやに はいる」と「かばんに ほんを いれる」 — the doer arrives
   The third pair, and the last one you get half of free. はいる you have had since module 16 — 「へやに はいる」, I go into the room. いれる is the other side of it, putting a thing in: 「かばんに ほんを いれる」. Watch what the particles do. With はいる the thing that moves takes が, and where it goes takes に. With いれる the thing that moves takes を, because somebody is moving it, and where it goes still takes に. The に never changes. Only the が/を slot does, and it changes because a doer arrived.
      · かばんに ほんを いれる。  —  I put a book in my bag.
      · へやに はいる。  —  I go into the room.
      · かばんに かぎが はいる。  —  The key goes in the bag.
      · ミカが かばんに さいふを いれる。  —  Mika puts her wallet in her bag.
      · へやに おおきい つくえが はいる。  —  A big desk goes in the room.
   NOT: かばんに ほんが いれる。 — いれる needs somebody doing the putting, and が does not supply one — the book being put takes を: かばんに ほんを いれる。

## Lesson 5 — 「でんしゃが とまる」と「くるまを とめる」 — who stopped it (ja-m33-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「でんしゃが とまる」と「くるまを とめる」 — who stopped it
   Two pairs about stopping, both half-owned already. とまる you met last module — 「でんしゃが とまる」, the train comes to a halt. Its partner とめる is what a driver does: 「くるまを とめる」, I stop the car. Same が/を tell, no new machinery. Worth noticing: the pair is asymmetric in real life. Trains stop by themselves constantly and cars almost never do, so とまる turns up with trains and machines, とめる with cars and people. That is not a rule to learn — it falls out of what the verbs mean.
      · でんしゃが とまる。  —  The train stops.

      · きかいが とまる。  —  The machine stops.

## Lesson 6 — 「じかんが きまる」と「じかんを きめる」 — settled, by nobody in particular (ja-m33-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「じかんが きまる」と「じかんを きめる」 — settled, by nobody in particular
   きめる you have owned since module 30: I decide. Its partner きまる has no clean English word — it means the thing got decided, without saying by whom. 「じかんが きまる」 is "the time is settled". Japanese reaches for this constantly where English would use a passive, and it is your first look at a habit that runs through the language: when who-did-it is not the point, Japanese puts no doer in the sentence at all. It just uses the が-verb.
      · じかんが きまる。  —  The time is settled.
      · じかんを きめる。  —  I decide the time.
      · りょこうが きまる。  —  The trip is settled.

## Lesson 9 — 「ドアが あいている」 — the state a change left behind (ja-m33-neo-9)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ドアが あいている」 — the state a change left behind
   Here is the payoff — the piece module 14 could not finish. ている has two readings: an action still running (たべている, is eating) and a state left behind by a change (しっている, knows). Module 14 could not say WHICH verbs do which, because the answer is what you just learned. A 自動詞 + ている is almost always the second kind: the change already happened, and you are describing how the thing is NOW. 「ドアが あいている」 is not a door in the act of opening — it is a door that is open.
