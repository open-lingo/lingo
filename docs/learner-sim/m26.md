# m26 — Comparisons II

Module 26 · Superlatives

「たべものの なかで すしが いちばん おいしい」 — of all foods, sushi is the most delicious. A superlative in Japanese is just a comparison with the field named: なかで says what group you are choosing from, が marks the winner, and いちばん sits in front of the adjective without changing a thing about it. Ask it with a question word, hang it on a verb you nominalise with の, scale a verb with it, concede the rest of the field with も…けど — and know when to reach back for last module's ほうが…より instead.

## Lesson 1 — 「すしが いちばん おいしい」 — the top of a whole group (ja-m26-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「すしが いちばん おいしい」 — the top of a whole group
   To say something is the MOST, Japanese names the field it is the most of and then points at the winner. 「たべものの なかで すしが いちばん おいしい」 — of all foods, sushi is the most delicious. Three pieces, and you already own all three. なか is the ordinary noun 'inside' from module 6, so it takes の in front of it exactly as ほう did last module, and で behind it: 「たべものの なかで」 is 'inside the set of foods'. The winner takes が, because picking one thing out of a group is what が has always done — は would make the winner the TOPIC, which is a different sentence. And いちばん is an adverb meaning 'number one', which sits directly in front of the adjective. Here is the good news, and it is the same good news as last module: いちばん does not touch the predicate. たかい stays たかい. There is no ending to add, nothing to conjugate, and no word for 'most' hiding inside the adjective. Say the group, say the winner, say いちばん, say the adjective. The field can be a plain noun (かぞくの なかで), a list you name out loud (はると なつと あきと ふゆの なかで), or a set you are pointing at (この みっつの なかで).
      · たべものの なかで すしが いちばん おいしい。  —  Of all foods, sushi is the most delicious.
      · のみものの なかで ちゃが いちばん やすい。  —  Of the drinks, tea is the cheapest.
      · かぞくの なかで ちちが いちばん いそがしい。  —  In my family, my father is the busiest.
      · はると なつと あきと ふゆの なかで ふゆが いちばん さむい。  —  Of spring, summer, autumn and winter, winter is the coldest.
      · この みっつの なかで これが いちばん たかい。  —  Of these three, this one is the most expensive.
   NOT: たべものの なかで すしが おいしい いちばん。 — いちばん goes in FRONT of the word it scales, never after it — it is an adverb, not an ending: たべものの なかで すしが いちばん おいしい。

## Lesson 2 — 「なにが いちばん おいしい？」 — asking which one tops the group (ja-m26-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「なにが いちばん おいしい？」 — asking which one tops the group
   To ASK which one tops a group, put a question word where the winner would have gone and leave everything else exactly as it was. 「かぞくの なかで だれが いちばん いそがしい？」 — who is the busiest in your family? The question word takes が and never は, for the reason module 20 gave for どっち: a question word cannot be what a sentence is ABOUT, because you do not yet know what it is. Pick the question word by what kind of thing you are asking after. なに for a thing, だれ for a person, どこ for a place, いつ for a time, and どれ when the options have already been named out loud. That last one is worth watching: どれ picks from a set in front of you, so 「この みっつの なかで どれが いちばん たかい？」 works, while なに asks with nothing named at all. The answer comes back in the ordinary frame with が on the winner, and it needs no なかで at all, because the question already named the field: 「ももが いちばん たかい」.
      · たべものの なかで なにが いちばん おいしい？  —  Of all foods, what's the most delicious?
      · かぞくの なかで だれが いちばん いそがしい？  —  Who's the busiest in your family?
      · にほんの なかで どこが いちばん さむい？  —  Where's the coldest place in Japan?
      · はると なつと あきと ふゆの なかで いつが いちばん すずしい？  —  Of spring, summer, autumn and winter, which one is the coolest?
      · この みっつの なかで どれが いちばん やすい？  —  Of these three, which is the cheapest?
   NOT: かぞくの なかで だれは いちばん いそがしい？ — a question word can never be the topic of a sentence, so だれ always takes が: かぞくの なかで だれが いちばん いそがしい？

## Lesson 3 — 「およぐのが いちばん すきだ」 — the thing you like doing most (ja-m26-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「およぐのが いちばん すきだ」 — the thing you like doing most
   The winner of a group does not have to be a thing — it can be something you DO, and module 13 already gave you the way to say so. Put の straight after a plain verb and the verb becomes a thing: およぐ becomes およぐの, the swimming of it. Then it takes が like anything else, and いちばん goes in front of the adjective exactly as before: 「およぐのが いちばん すきだ」 — swimming is what I like most. The verb stays in plain form, because の is doing all the nominalising, and everything you hang off the verb comes along with it: 「かわで およぐのが いちばん たのしい」, 「えいがを みるのが いちばん すきだ」. すき is a な-adjective rather than a verb, which is why what you like is marked が and never を, and why it keeps its だ at the end. きらい behaves identically. Two habits worth building here. First, this is the most natural way to answer 「なにが いちばん すき？」 when the honest answer is an activity. Second, たのしい and おもしろい take the same frame, so you can say a thing is the most FUN to do without ever saying you like it.
      · なつやすみに およぐのが いちばん すきだ。  —  Swimming is what I like most in the summer holidays.
      · えいがを みるのが いちばん たのしい。  —  Watching films is the most fun.
      · かぞくと りょこうを するのが いちばん たのしい。  —  Travelling with my family is the most fun.
      · じてんしゃに のるのが いちばん おもしろい。  —  Riding a bike is the most interesting.
      · にほんの りょうりを たべるのが いちばん すきだ。  —  Eating Japanese food is what I like most.
   NOT: なつやすみに およぐが いちばん すきだ。 — a verb cannot be marked が on its own — の turns it into a thing first: なつやすみに およぐのが いちばん すきだ。

## Lesson 5 — 「ほうが」 と 「いちばん」 — two things, or a whole group (ja-m26-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ほうが」 と 「いちばん」 — two things, or a whole group
   You now have two comparison frames and the only thing to get right is which one you are in. TWO things take last module's frame: the winner marked ほうが, the loser marked より, and nothing else. 「バスより ちかてつの ほうが やすい」 — the underground is cheaper than the bus. THREE OR MORE take this module's: the field named with なかで, the winner marked が, and いちばん in front of the adjective. 「ちかてつと バスと じてんしゃの なかで じてんしゃが いちばん やすい」. Everything else is shared. Neither frame touches the adjective, both put the winner before the predicate, and both let you drop whatever the listener already knows — 「バスより やすい」 is a whole answer to a question about the underground, and 「じてんしゃが いちばん やすい」 is a whole answer once the three have been named. The one thing you must not do is stack them. ほうが has already picked the winner of a pair, so there is nothing left for いちばん to top, and 「ほうが いちばん」 is not a sentence anybody says. Pick your frame by counting the things on the table: two, or more than two.
      · バスより ちかてつの ほうが やすい。  —  The underground is cheaper than the bus.
      · ちかてつと バスと じてんしゃの なかで じてんしゃが いちばん やすい。  —  Of the underground, the bus and the bike, the bike is the cheapest.

## Lesson 6 — 「どれが いちばん おもい？」 — naming the field out loud (ja-m26-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「どれが いちばん おもい？」 — naming the field out loud
   When the field is not a ready-made word like たべもの, name it out loud with と. 「かばんと くつと ふくの なかで どれが いちばん たかい？」 — of the bag, the shoes and the clothes, which is the most expensive? と is module 8's と and it is doing module 8's job: it ties a CLOSED list, so the listener knows those are all the options there are. Put it between every pair of items and let the last item run straight into の なかで. There is no limit of two here — と will tie three, four, or as many as you can say in one breath. What DOES depend on the count is the question word. どっち asks about exactly two and cannot stretch: 「ちゃと コーヒーと どっちが やすい？」. The moment a third item joins the list, the question word becomes どれ. Their polite twins behave the same way, どちら for two and どれ for more. And the answer to either one comes back with が on the winner: 「くつが いちばん たかい」.
      · かばんと くつと ふくの なかで どれが いちばん たかい？  —  Of the bag, the shoes and the clothes, which is the most expensive?
      · ちゃと コーヒーと どっちが やすい？  —  Which is cheaper, tea or coffee?
      · やまと かわと いけの なかで どれが いちばん とおい？  —  Of the mountains, the river and the pond, which is the furthest?
      · じしょと てがみと しゃしんの なかで どれが いちばん ふるい？  —  Of the dictionary, the letter and the photo, which is the oldest?
      · この みっつの なかで どれが いちばん おおきい？  —  Of these three, which is the biggest?
   NOT: かばんと くつと ふくの なかで どっちが いちばん たかい？ — どっち asks about exactly TWO things — with three on the table the question word is どれ: かばんと くつと ふくの なかで どれが いちばん たかい？

## Lesson 7 — 「としょかんが いちばん しずかだ」 — な-adjectives keep their だ (ja-m26-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「としょかんが いちばん しずかだ」 — な-adjectives keep their だ
   な-adjectives go into the superlative frame with nothing removed and nothing added, and they keep their だ. 「がっこうの なかで としょかんが いちばん しずかだ」 — of everywhere in the school, the library is the quietest. This is module 12's rule and module 20's promise meeting each other: a な-adjective is not a verb and cannot finish a sentence on its own, so it needs だ; and いちばん is an adverb, so it cannot supply one. しずか is a quality, しずかだ is a statement, and いちばん しずかだ is a statement about the top of a group. Nothing about the frame changes that in either direction. The words worth practising here are the ones English hears as verbs but Japanese treats as な-adjectives: すき and きらい, which take が on the thing liked, and じょうず and へた, which do the same. 「のみものの なかで ちゃが いちばん すきだ」 is 'tea is my favourite drink' — literally, of the drinks, tea is the most likeable. And the negative behaves exactly as module 12 left it: しずかじゃない.
      · がっこうの なかで としょかんが いちばん しずかだ。  —  Of everywhere in the school, the library is the quietest.
      · にほんの なかで さくらが いちばん ゆうめいだ。  —  In Japan, the cherry blossom is the most famous.
      · きょうだいの なかで いもうとが いちばん げんきだ。  —  Of my siblings, my younger sister is the liveliest.
      · のみものの なかで ちゃが いちばん すきだ。  —  Tea is my favourite drink.
      · この みっつの なかで これが いちばん きれいだ。  —  Of these three, this one is the prettiest.
   NOT: がっこうの なかで としょかんが いちばん しずか。 — a な-adjective cannot finish a sentence on its own — it needs だ, and いちばん does not supply one: がっこうの なかで としょかんが いちばん しずかだ。

## Lesson 9 — 「どれも いいけど これが いちばん」 — agree first, then choose (ja-m26-neo-9)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「どれも いいけど これが いちばん」 — agree first, then choose
   Real preferences are polite about the things they did not pick, and Japanese has a tidy shape for that. Put も on every member of the field to concede that all of them are true, then hang the choice off the end with けど. 「にくも さかなも おいしいけど さかなが いちばん すきだ」 — meat and fish are both delicious, but fish is what I like most. も is module 3's 'too', and repeating it is what turns two separate 'too's into 'both'. The move matters more than the grammar does: saying flatly that one thing is the best implies the others are not, and conceding first takes that sting out. It also works with the field already named — 「どれも たかいけど これが いちばん たかい」, they are all expensive but this is the most expensive — where どれも means 'all of them' and needs no list in front of it. Name the field with a noun and 〜の なかで does the same job: 「ことばの なかで えいごが いちばん むずかしい」 — of all the languages, えいご, the English language, is the hardest. Two things to watch. も REPLACES は and が rather than stacking with them, so it is 「にくも」 and never 「にくがも」. And すき still takes が on the thing liked, however many も have come before it.
      · にくも さかなも おいしいけど さかなが いちばん すきだ。  —  Meat and fish are both delicious, but fish is what I like most.
      · どれも たかいけど この とけいが いちばん たかい。  —  They're all expensive, but this watch is the most expensive.
      · はるも あきも すずしいけど あきが いちばん すずしい。  —  Spring and autumn are both cool, but autumn is the coolest.
      · ちかてつも バスも やすいけど じてんしゃが いちばん やすい。  —  The underground and the bus are both cheap, but the bike is the cheapest.
      · いぬも ねこも すきだけど ねこが いちばん すきだ。  —  I like both dogs and cats, but cats are what I like most.
   NOT: にくも さかなも おいしいけど さかなを いちばん すきだ。 — すき is an adjective, not a verb — the thing you like is marked が, never を: にくも さかなも おいしいけど さかなが いちばん すきだ。

## Lesson 10 — 「ちちが いちばん はたらく」 — the most, of DOING (ja-m26-neo-10)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ちちが いちばん はたらく」 — the most, of DOING
   いちばん scales a VERB exactly the way it scales an adjective, and this is where the frame stops being about qualities and starts being about behaviour. 「かぞくの なかで ちちが いちばん はたらく」 — of everyone in my family, my father works the most. Put いちばん directly in front of the verb and leave the verb alone. It stays in the plain dictionary form, and — the thing worth restating because English speakers reach for it — it takes NO だ. A dictionary-form verb has been a complete sentence since module 5; だ finishes NOUNS. 「はたらくだ」 is wrong here for the same reason it was wrong then, and いちばん changes nothing about it. Add よく in front of the verb when you mean 'the most often' rather than 'the most': 「いぬが いちばん よく あるく」. And note what the frame is really claiming — 「おとうとが いちばん たべる」 says he eats more than the rest, not that eating is his favourite thing. For that you want の from the earlier lesson: 「たべるのが いちばん すきだ」.

## Lesson 11 — 「どれが いちばん やすいですか」 — asking a stranger which one (ja-m26-neo-11)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「どれが いちばん やすいですか」 — asking a stranger which one
   The place you will actually need a superlative first is a shop, and a shop means polite. Everything about the frame stays where it is — なかで names the field, が marks the winner, いちばん sits in front of the adjective — and only the ending changes: 「どれが いちばん やすいですか。」 Two halves make that polite question work, and beginners drop one of them constantly. です carries the register, and か makes it a question. Leave か off and you have said a polite STATEMENT and left it hanging; leave です off and you have asked a friend, not a shop assistant. A casual question needs neither, because its rising contour does the work: 「どれが いちばん やすい？」 Same question, different room. The answer comes back polite too, and こちら is the polite twin of これ when you are pointing at goods: 「こちらが いちばん やすいです。」 One habit worth stealing from Japanese shoppers: 「すみません」 in front of the question is not an apology, it is how you open one.
      · どれが いちばん やすいですか。  —  Which one is the cheapest?
