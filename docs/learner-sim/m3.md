6. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — だ — 'it's X', plain and simple
   You just heard it three times: noun + だ = 'it's X / X is the case.' だ closes a plain statement — the everyday register friends use with each other. One noun, one だ: a whole sentence.
      · ねこだ。  —  It's a cat.
      · みずだ。  —  It's water.
   NOT: だねこ。 — だ closes the sentence — it never leads. Noun first, だ last: ねこだ。
   Note: だ isn't literally the word 'it is' — Japanese doesn't need an 'it' at all. ねこだ is simply how Japanese says what English says with 'It's a cat.' Treat them as equivalents, not word-for-word translations.

3. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — は — the spotlight
   は shines a spotlight on the topic — the thing the sentence is about. Everything after は is a comment on it: わたしは がくせいだ = spotlight on me → comment: student. Written は, said 'wa'.
      · わたしは がくせいだ。  —  I'm a student. (spotlight: me → comment: student)
      · たなかは せんせいだ。  —  Tanaka is a teacher. (the spotlight moves to Tanaka)
      · がくせいだ。  —  (I'm) a student. (no spotlight — natural when it's already obvious who you mean)
      · ミカは にほんじんだ。  —  Mika is Japanese. (にほんじん = a Japanese person)
   NOT: はわたし がくせいだ。 — は clips onto the END of the word it spotlights: わたしは, never はわたし. Japanese particles always follow their word.

## Lesson 3 — も — me too (ja-m3-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Too
   Swap は for も and the sentence gains 'too / also': トムも がくせいだ = Tom is a student TOO. も REPLACES は in that slot — the two never stack. Everything else stays the same.
      · トムも がくせいだ。  —  Tom is a student too.
      · わたしも がくせいだ。  —  I'm a student too.
   NOT: トムはも がくせいだ。 — も replaces は; they never stack. トムも already carries both jobs: topic AND 'too.'

## Lesson 4 — Asking without か (ja-m3-neo-4)

4. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Asking with your voice — ねこ？
   Casual questions need no question word: say the noun with a rising tone and it becomes a question. ねこ。 (falling) = 'It's a cat.' ねこ？ (rising) = 'Is it a cat?' Works with は-sentences too: たなかは せんせい？
      · ねこ？  —  Is it a cat? (rising tone)
      · がくせい？  —  Are you a student?
      · ねこだ？  —  It's a CAT?! (keeping だ adds surprised disbelief — real, but not a neutral question)
   Note: The answers you'll hear (recognition only for now): うん — the casual 'yeah,' the most common word in spoken Japanese; そう / そうだ — 'that's right.' Just understand them; producing them comes later.
