## Lesson 2 — One to five (ja-m9-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — One to five
   いち に さん よん ご ろく なな はち きゅう じゅう. Four and seven have two readings each — よん/し and なな/しち. Use よん and なな: they are the ones that never collide with anything, and native speakers reach for them first when counting things. A price is one of these numbers with えん — the yen — glued straight onto its end: ごえん is five yen.
      · いち に さん  —  one, two, three
      · よん ご ろく  —  four, five, six
      · なな はち きゅう じゅう  —  seven, eight, nine, ten

## Lesson 4 — Counting things — 〜つ (ja-m9-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Counting things — 〜つ
   〜つ is the ALL-PURPOSE counter for things: ひとつ ふたつ みっつ よっつ いつつ. It works for objects when you don't know the specific counter, which as a beginner is most of the time. It stops at とお (10) — past that you use the plain numbers.
      · ひとつ ください  —  One, please.
      · ふたつ ある  —  There are two.
   NOT: いちつ — the 〜つ series uses its own readings — ひとつ, not いちつ

## Lesson 7 — Cheap and expensive (ja-m9-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Cheap and expensive
   Two adjectives finish the shop exchange. たかい means expensive — and, of a thing, tall; やすい means cheap. Either one can be the whole predicate: これは たかい, politely これは たかいです. かね is the money itself, which is why かねが ない — 'there is no money' — is how you say you can't afford it.
      · これは たかいです。  —  This is expensive.
      · かねが ない。  —  I have no money.
   NOT: これは たかいだ。 — an い-adjective is already a predicate — it takes です politely and never だ: これは たかいです.
