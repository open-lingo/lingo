## Lesson 1 — Voiced k → g — Intro (ja-m1-g-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Dakuten — the voicing mark
   Two small strokes (゛) on a kana voice it: the shape stays the same but your vocal cords buzz. You'll meet 5 of these in the k→g row.
      · か → が  —  voiced k becomes g
      · き → ぎ  —  voiced k becomes g

## Lesson 4 — Voiced s → z — Intro (ja-m1-z-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — S → Z (with one twist)
   Dakuten ゛ voices s-kana: さ→ざ, す→ず, せ→ぜ, そ→ぞ. One quirk: し voiced is じ, pronounced 'ji' (not 'zi').
      · さ → ざ  —  voiced s becomes z
      · し → じ  —  voiced shi becomes ji, not zi
   NOT: し → ぜ — し voiced is じ ('ji'), following the shi→ji shift — not 'zi'.
2. [symbol_intro] TEACHES 【ざ】 = "za" — voiced さ (sa → za) (e.g. Same shape as さ with two strokes.)
3. [symbol_trace] Trace 【ざ】

## Lesson 7 — Voiced t → d — Intro (ja-m1-d-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — T → D (with two ghosts)
   Dakuten ゛ on t-kana voices them: た→だ, て→で, と→ど. ち and つ can take dakuten (ぢ・づ) too, but modern Japanese writes those sounds as じ・ず instead.
      · た → だ  —  voiced t becomes d
      · と → ど  —  voiced t becomes d
   Note: You'll see ぢ・づ in the wild — recognize them, but there's no need to write them.
