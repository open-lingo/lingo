## Lesson 2 — それ・あれ — the distance system (ja-m4-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — これ・それ・あれ — near me, near you, over there
   Three pointers, one system: これ = near ME, それ = near YOU, あれ = far from us BOTH. Swap the pointer, keep the sentence — everything else stays the same.
      · これは ほんだ。  —  This is a book. (in my hand)
      · それは ほんだ。  —  That's a book. (near you)
      · あれは ほんだ。  —  That's a book. (way over there)
   Note: こ・そ・あ is a distance ladder you'll meet again and again (me → you → yonder). A fourth rung, the question 'which one?', joins later this module.

## Lesson 3 — これ、なに？ — the pointer question (ja-m4-neo-3)

1. [dialogue_listen] DIALOGUE (audio)
      Tom: これ、なに？
      Mika: それは ほんだ。
      Q1: What does Tom want to know?
         A) Whether the book is Mika's   B) Where his book went   C) What the thing he's holding is   D) Whether Mika can read it
      Q2: What does Mika answer?
         A) That's a book.   B) Is that a book?   C) This is a book.   D) It's not a book.
2. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — なに — what?
   Point and ask: これ、なに？ = 'this — what is it?' The rising tone does the asking, m3-style. The answer flips the pointer: your これ is their それ.
      · これ、なに？  —  What's this? (voice rises)
      · それは ほんだ。  —  That's a book. (the answer flips これ to それ)
      · あれ、なに？  —  What's that over there?
   NOT: これ、なにだ？ — なに never takes だ straight — the plain question is just なに？ with a rise. When 'what' fuses with an ending it changes shape (you'll meet that form later).
   Note: 'What' has two sounds in Japanese: なに on its own, なん when fused to what follows. This module only needs なに — the fused shapes arrive with their own patterns.

## Lesson 5 — の — possession (ja-m4-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — の — the owner's mark
   の links owner to thing: わたしの ほん = my book. Owner FIRST, の in the middle, thing last — exactly English's 's: Tom's cat = トムの ねこ.
      · これは わたしの ほんだ。  —  This is my book.
      · それは トムの ねこだ。  —  That's Tom's cat.
      · ミカの かばんだ。  —  (It's) Mika's bag.
   NOT: これは ほんの わたしだ。 — Order is everything: owner before の, thing after. わたしの ほん = my book; flipping it makes the book the owner — of you.
   Note: の never translates as a separate word — it IS the apostrophe-s. Read Xの Y right to left if it helps: 'the Y of X.'

## Lesson 6 — だれ — who (and whose) (ja-m4-neo-6)

1. [dialogue_listen] DIALOGUE (audio)

      Q2: Who is it?
         A) A stranger   B) Tanaka   C) Ken's teacher   D) Tom — a friend
2. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — だれ — who? and だれの — whose?
   だれ asks 'who?': これ、だれ？ = who's this? Add の and you're asking about an owner: だれの かばん？ = whose bag? The answer can drop the thing itself: ケンのだ = it's Ken's.
      · だれの かばん？  —  Whose bag is this?
      · ケンのだ。  —  It's Ken's. (の stands in for the bag itself)
      · これ、だれ？  —  Who's this?
   NOT: だれ かばん？ — 'Whose' is always だれ+の — without の the two nouns just collide. だれの かばん？, never だれ かばん？.
   Note: ケンのだ works because の can carry the noun silently: ケンの = 'Ken's one.' You'll lean on this constantly — Japanese hates repeating a noun both sides already know.

## Lesson 8 — の — where things are from (ja-m4-neo-8)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — の again — where it's from
   You know の links an owner to a thing: ケンの かばん. The SAME の links an origin or category to a thing: にほんの くるま = a car of-Japan — a Japanese car. Xの Y always reads back-to-front in English: the Y of X.
      · にほんの くるまだ。  —  It's a Japanese car.
      · アメリカの けいたいだ。  —  It's an American phone.
   NOT: くるまの にほんだ。 — の points from origin to thing: にほんの くるま. Flipped, it says 'the car's Japan' — nonsense.
   Note: Owner or origin, Japanese doesn't care — の just ties two nouns so the second belongs to the first's world. English needs 's, 'of', or an adjective; Japanese needs one syllable.

## Lesson 9 — どれ — which one (ja-m4-neo-9)

   これ・それ・あれ point at ONE thing. When there are three or more and you don't know WHICH, ask with どれ: かばんは どれ？ = 'Which one is the bag?' Answer by pointing: これだ。それだ。あれだ。
      · かばんは どれ？  —  Which one is the bag?
