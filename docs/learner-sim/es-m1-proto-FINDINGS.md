# ES m1 prototype — learner-sim findings (2026-08-20, 4× Opus fresh-learner walk)

Four agents walked docs/learner-sim/es-m1-proto.md in full fresh-learner
character (English-only, no jargon, phone): confusion walk, memory/retention
(+ next-day self-test + JA m3 rhythm comparison), ease/flow, wildcard.
Raw reports in the session transcript; this file is the synthesis.

## THE HEADLINE NUMBERS (memory agent)

- **48% of steps (40/83) carry zero retrieval demand** — 6 info cards,
  19 read-aloud speaking steps, 15 picture-solvable MCQs.
- **1.5 genuine retrieval events per item** across the whole module.
- All 19 speaking steps print the Spanish (read-aloud); JA's 24 are
  English-only cued recall. **0% unscaffolded production** (JA m3: 41%
  production incl. 7 translate + 14 listening_build).
- **9 items are never retrieved outside their introducing lesson**
  (cero, uno, dos, tres, seis, siete, diez, sí por favor, ~buenas tardes).
- Next-day self-test: 12 items solid — **9 of them pre-known from English.
  Net new solid vocabulary from 8 lessons: ~4 items** (buenos días,
  hasta luego, mucho gusto, no gracias).
- JA rhythm the module lacks: per-lesson OFF-TOPIC review tails (last 4-6
  steps, themselves spaced, closing "match (review)"), a dedicated
  20-step zero-new review lesson, recognition-only tier, alternating-
  answer discrimination clozes, NOT: counterexamples on cards.
- ES review lives only in L7+L8 — which are the module's two SHORTEST
  lessons (6+8 steps to consolidate 29 items; JA m3's review lesson alone
  is 20).

Spencer's diagnosis confirmed and sharpened: it isn't just "too much at
once, too little review" — the module shows words instead of charging for
them, so a perfect score is achievable with almost nothing retained.

## UNANIMOUS BLOCKERS (3-4 agents each)

1. **buenos/buenas is trained toward a FALSE rule.** The L3 card presents
   them as one word; the module's only two discrimination trials BOTH
   answer "buenas" — always-answer-buenas scores 100%. L3.6 is a punished
   coin flip; L8.2 grades it again.
2. **Digit emoji are the answer key** on 10 number steps INCLUDING mastery
   L8.5 — the primary teaching steps for 11/29 items demand no Spanish.
3. **L4→L5 is the quit point**: 25 steps, zero prior-item review (L4 has
   zero prior items even shown), free-to-answer, "10 minutes spent,
   nothing earned … this is where the app feels for children."
4. **The phonetics curriculum (the module's own name) is 100% taught, 0%
   tested.** Five cards; no step checks a vowel, stress, silent h, or c.
   A learner can pronounce everything wrong and score 8/8 on mastery.
5. **L7 open tile-turns**: "Greet her back" with 5 tiles and no length
   cue; «¿Sí o no?» is a question about nothing.
6. **perdón is structurally a leftover-answer** everywhere it appears,
   never plays as audio, vanishes L3–L7, resurfaces cold in mastery.

## OTHER LOAD-BEARING FINDINGS

- Every listening step replays the immediately-prior map/step → passes on
  10-second memory; "no evidence I can hear Spanish and I know it."
- First Spanish AUDIO plays at L1.9 — after the learner has spoken three
  words aloud from respellings (gracias risk: "GRAY-shus", never corrected).
- Confusable-pair blurs measured: seis/siete (coin flip), y/o (rescued
  only by o≈or), buenos/buenas (false rule), tardes/noches, cero/cinco
  (the soft-c card ITSELF raises the pair, then never exercises it).
- All 8 lessons end on match_pairs — the tap-heaviest step as the closer
  every time; L7's conversation triumph stapled to a chore; module ends
  on a grid, not on Ana.
- World coherence: Diego "introduced for the first time" in L6 after the
  L2 subway apology; Ana says «Mucho gusto» in L7 after knowing you for
  six lessons; a 38-step humanless stretch L2→L6.
- Turn-2 cliff: the learner can OPEN a conversation and immediately lose
  it — no ¿cómo estás?, no «no entiendo», no «me llamo» (scope decision:
  m1 additions vs m2).
- Phone-in-public: 41–57% of steps are audio/voice-gated with no silent
  fallback (engine items: tap-to-reveal transcript for listening → step
  counts as review; "later" affordance for speaking; slot-count ghost for
  sim tile replies).
- L8 coverage holes: gracias, sí, no, no gracias, sí por favor and most
  numbers are never tested in mastery.
- L4.10 map's English order ≠ audio order (deliberate taught-first
  prompting — the agents are right that it breaks the mechanic's one
  rule; fix by card-feeding cero and keeping natural order).
- Cheap wins: ¿¡ never explained (a free "this language is different"
  delight beat); «» unexplained; lesson titles are inventory labels, not
  promises; module never states its promise; word_map instructions repeat
  verbatim ×10; días glossed "morning" two steps after the card says
  "day"; three different glosses for buenas noches.

## KEEP (what worked, per the in-character walks)

- The vowel card paying off — self-pronouncing «seis» and «mucho gusto»
  was "the reason I'd come back."
- Word_map first-phrase wins; the coffee scene and the Ana-leaving scene
  ("the 'she'll be back tomorrow' detail quietly taught hasta luego ≠
  adiós — best teaching in the module and it wasn't a card").
- ~5-minute lessons (reliably 2 per train ride); "either way works"
  max-acceptance moments; L7.3's clean build.

## RESTRUCTURE PLAN (priority order, merged from all four)

R1. Review tails on EVERY lesson (the existing house mechanism): last 3-5
    steps off-topic prior items, closing match ≥50% prior, tail lane
    itself spaced (L4 tail carries L1-L2 courtesy; L5 carries L4 numbers;
    L6 carries seis/siete/diez...).
R2. Dedicated es-m1-review lesson after L5 (~16-18 steps, zero new) and
    grow L7 to ~16. Target: every item ≥2 post-intro retrievals; every
    item present in L8.
R3. Speaking → cued recall ("Say this in Spanish: good morning") for
    every NON-first voicing; the first voicing of a new word keeps the
    printed form + hears the clip first (fixes speak-before-any-audio).
R4. De-crutch numbers: digit emoji only on the intro MCQ; retrievals
    become audio-prompted (trains the seis/siete ear) or word→digit;
    compress L4/L5 (~cut 5 free steps); one counting scene each; never a
    digit crutch in L8.
R5. buenos/buenas: card teaches the pairing + NOT: «buenas días»
    counterexample; ≥2 trials answering buenos with buenas live;
    alternate answers across trials. Same alternating-answer treatment
    for y/o (×4 across L5-L8), seis/siete (post-L5, audio), tardes/
    noches, cero/cinco.
R6. Cash every card within 2 steps: one which-do-you-hear sound check per
    phonetics card; else cut the card.
R7. World/arc: fix Diego/Ana continuity; give «¿Sí o no?» a real
    question; state the promise in L1; titles as promises; end lessons on
    their win (match second-to-last, 4 pairs); END THE MODULE on a 2-turn
    Ana goodbye scene, not a grid.
R8. Use es translateStep (exists, unused) for ≥1 unscaffolded production
    in later lessons; separate listening checks from their source step;
    give perdón a real life (image-ish intro, mid-module tail slots, one
    discrimination against live known distractors, audio plays).
R9. Engine items (backlog): listening tap-to-reveal fallback, speaking
    "later" affordance, sim tile-reply length cue.
R10. Open scope decision for Spencer: turn-2 cliff vocabulary (¿cómo
    estás? / me llamo / no entiendo) into m1 or early m2.
