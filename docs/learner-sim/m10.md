## Lesson 2 — Three ways to say yes (ja-m10-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Three ways to say yes
   Japanese has three yeses and three noes, and they split by AUDIENCE. Casual: うん / ううん. Neutral-polite: ええ. Formal: はい / いいえ. うん and ううん are one sound apart — the difference is a rising ん versus a falling one, so listen for the shape, not the letters.
      · うん  —  Yeah. (to a friend)
      · はい  —  Yes. (to a teacher)
   NOT: せんせい、うん。 — うん to a teacher is too casual — はい or ええ

## Lesson 3 — Three ways to say no (ja-m10-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Saying no — and correcting
   ううん and いいえ are one word in two registers: they answer no to a question. ちがう is not a third register of no — it is a different move. It CORRECTS: someone has said something wrong and you are telling them so. ちがう、ミカの ほんだ = 'no, it's Mika's book'. Politely it is ちがいます. Decline with いいえ; correct with ちがう.
      · ううん、たべない。  —  No, I won't eat.
      · ちがう、ミカの ほんだ。  —  No — it's Mika's book.

## Lesson 4 — はい isn't always yes (ja-m10-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — はい isn't always yes
   はい does not always mean 'yes'. In conversation it is also AIZUCHI — the noise a listener makes to show they're still with you, like English 'mm-hm'. So はい can mean 'I'm listening', not 'I agree'. Watch what follows it: agreement continues the thought, aizuchi just keeps the speaker going.
      · はい、はい。  —  Mm-hm, go on.
      · はい、いきます。  —  Yes, I'll go.

## Lesson 6 — Softening with ちょっと (ja-m10-neo-4)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Softening with ちょっと
   ちょっと literally means 'a little', but its real job is SOFTENING. On its own — ちょっと… — it is a polite refusal that never says no. Japanese avoids flat rejection, so a trailing ちょっと is understood as 'that doesn't work for me'.
      · ちょっと、たかいです。  —  It's a little expensive.
      · きょうは ちょっと。  —  Today is… a bit difficult (trailing off = a polite no).

## Lesson 7 — No thank you — だめ and けっこう (ja-m10-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — No thank you — だめ and けっこう
   Turning something down has its own two words. だめ is the blunt one — 'no good', and by extension 'not allowed': それは だめ stops a friend mid-move. けっこう is the gentle refusal you hand a host or a shop assistant; it means 'I'm fine as I am, thank you', so ちゃは けっこうです declines the tea without ever saying no. だめ forbids, けっこう declines — they are not interchangeable.
      · それは だめ。  —  That's no good.
      · けっこうです。  —  No thank you.

## Lesson 8 — Who's I? — わたし, ぼく (ja-m10-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Who's I? — わたし, ぼく
   Even 'I' carries register. わたし is the neutral one and is safe in any company. ぼく is casual and usually male — friends and family, never a first meeting with a teacher. Most of the time Japanese drops the subject altogether: いく on its own already means 'I'm going', so reach for a pronoun only when you need the contrast.
      · わたしは がくせいです。  —  I'm a student.

## Lesson 11 — Knowing and now — しる, いま (ja-m10-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Knowing and now — しる, いま
   しる is 'to know' — having a fact, a place or a person in your head. The form you will reach for constantly is its NEGATIVE: しりません to a teacher or a stranger, しらない to a friend, both 'I don't know'. Politeness lives in the tail, exactly as it does for every other verb: しります / しりません.
      · わたしは しりません。  —  I don't know. (polite)
