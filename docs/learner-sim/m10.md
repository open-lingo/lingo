## Lesson 2 — Three ways to say yes (ja-m10-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Three ways to say yes
   Japanese has three yeses and three noes, and they split by AUDIENCE. Casual: うん / ううん. Neutral-polite: ええ. Formal: はい / いいえ. うん and ううん are one sound apart — the difference is a rising ん versus a falling one, so listen for the shape, not the letters.
      · うん  —  Yeah. (to a friend)

## Lesson 4 — はい isn't always yes (ja-m10-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — はい isn't always yes
   はい does not always mean 'yes'. In conversation it is also AIZUCHI — the noise a listener makes to show they're still with you, like English 'mm-hm'. So はい can mean 'I'm listening', not 'I agree'. Watch what follows it: agreement continues the thought, aizuchi just keeps the speaker going.
      · はい、はい。  —  Mm-hm, go on.
      · はい、いきます。  —  Yes, I'll go.

## Lesson 6 — Softening with ちょっと (ja-m10-neo-4)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Softening with ちょっと
   ちょっと literally means 'a little', but its real job is SOFTENING. On its own — ちょっと… — it is a polite refusal that never says no. Japanese avoids flat rejection, so a trailing ちょっと is understood as 'that doesn't work for me'.
      · ちょっと、たかいです。  —  It's a little expensive.
      · きょうは ちょっと。  —  Today is… a bit difficult (trailing off = a polite no).
