# m17 — Family I: your side (うち)

Module 17 · うち and そと

Japanese has two sets of family words, and which one you use depends on whose family it is. Your own side is うち — "inside" — and takes the humble set: ちち, はは, あに, あね, おとうと, いもうと. With them come the counters family talk needs immediately — 〜にん for people (with the irregular ひとり / ふたり) and 〜さい for age — plus この / その / あの / どの, the row of the ko-so-a-do table that has to lean on a noun.

## Lesson 1 — ちちと はは — the words for your own family (ja-m17-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — ちちと はは — the words for your own family
   Japanese has TWO sets of family words, and which set you use depends on WHOSE family it is — not on how polite you are feeling. Your own family is what Japanese calls うち, "inside", and it takes the plain, humble set: ちち my father, はは my mother, あに my older brother, あね my older sister, おとうと my younger brother, いもうと my younger sister. Every one of those words already means MY. 「ちちは かいしゃで はたらく」 can only ever be about your own father. Anyone else's family is そと, "outside", and takes a different, honorific set — that set is a module of its own, so for now the rule is simple and absolute: these six words are for YOUR side only. And here is the piece that surprises everybody: you do not call your own mother はは to her face. Speaking TO her she is okaasan; speaking ABOUT her to an outsider she is はは. The word tracks the AUDIENCE, not the person — and that uchi/soto split is the same machinery Japanese politeness runs on all the way up.
      · ちちは かいしゃで はたらくから いそがしい。  —  My father works at a company, so he's busy.
      · ははは せんせいだから じゅぎょうが ある。  —  My mother is a teacher, so she has classes.
      · ちちも ははも かいしゃで はたらく。  —  Both my father and my mother work at a company.
      · ははは くるまが すきだから よく かいものに いく。  —  My mother likes cars, so she often drives to go shopping.
      · ちちは からだが おおきいけど ははは ちいさい。  —  My father is big, but my mother is small.
   NOT: ちちさんは かいしゃで はたらくから いそがしい。 — ちち is ALREADY the humble word for your own father — it never takes さん. さん belongs to the honorific set you use for someone else's family: ちちは かいしゃで はたらくから いそがしい。

## Lesson 2 — あにの じてんしゃ — whose is whose (ja-m17-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — あにの じてんしゃ — whose is whose
   の glues two nouns together and the one in FRONT owns the one behind: 「あにの じてんしゃ」 = my older brother's bicycle. Two more うち words arrive with it — あに, my older brother, and あね, my older sister — and like ちち and はは they can only ever mean mine. Notice what Japanese splits on: AGE, before anything else. あに is specifically an OLDER brother and あね an OLDER sister, and there is no word that just means "brother". You can chain の as far as you like — 「あにの ともだちの くるま」 = my older brother's friend's car — and you can stop after it, letting の carry the noun on its own: 「この かばんは あねのだ」 = this bag is my older sister's.
      · あにの じてんしゃは たかかったけど あたらしい。  —  My older brother's bicycle was expensive, but it's new.
      · ちちの かいしゃは えきの まえだ。  —  My father's company is in front of the station.
      · あにの ともだちは だいがくの がくせいだ。  —  My older brother's friend is a university student.
      · あねは ははの くるまを かった。  —  My older sister bought my mother's car.
      · あねの かばんは ははのだ。  —  My older sister's bag is my mother's.
   NOT: あに じてんしゃは たかかったけど あたらしい。 — two nouns need の between them — without it あに and じてんしゃ are just two words sitting side by side: あにの じてんしゃは たかかったけど あたらしい。

## Lesson 3 — おとうとが いる — saying who you have (ja-m17-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — おとうとが いる — saying who you have
   Japanese has no verb "to have" for people. You say they EXIST: 「おとうとが いる」, literally "a younger brother exists" — and that IS how you tell somebody you have one. が marks the person who exists, いる is the verb for people and animals (ある is for things), and the negative is いない. Two more うち words land here: おとうと, my younger brother, and いもうと, my younger sister. きょうだい comes with them and covers all four sibling words at once, which you will want often, because Japanese never lets you say just "brother" — あに and おとうと are different words and you have to pick one.
      · おとうとが いるけど あねは いない。  —  I have a younger brother, but I don't have an older sister.
      · いもうとは だいがくの がくせいだから いそがしい。  —  My younger sister is a university student, so she's busy.
      · きょうだいが いるから うちは にぎやかだ。  —  I have siblings, so my house is lively.
      · おとうとは おんがくが すきだから よく きく。  —  My younger brother likes music, so he listens to it often.
      · いもうとが いるけど おとうとは いない。  —  I have a younger sister, but I don't have a younger brother.
   NOT: おとうとが あるけど あねは いない。 — people and animals take いる — ある is only for things: おとうとが いるけど あねは いない。

## Lesson 5 — かぞくは よにんだ — counting people (ja-m17-neo-4)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — かぞくは よにんだ — counting people
   People are counted with 〜にん, and the first two cells are the ones you simply have to know: ひとり for one person and ふたり for two. Nobody says ichinin or ninin. From three on it behaves: さんにん, よにん, ごにん, ろくにん — with よにん read yo-nin, never yon-nin or shi-nin. To ask, なんにん. The count is not glued onto the noun the way English glues "three" onto "siblings": it stands on its own just in front of the verb, 「きょうだいが さんにん いる」, or finishes the sentence as an ordinary noun, 「うちの かぞくは よにんだ」.
      · きょうだいが さんにん いるから うちは にぎやかだ。  —  I have three siblings, so my house is lively.
      · うちの かぞくは よにんだ。  —  There are four people in my family.
      · あねが ひとり いるけど あには いない。  —  I have one older sister, but I don't have an older brother.
      · おとうとが ふたり いるから いそがしい。  —  I have two younger brothers, so I'm busy.
      · かぞくは なんにん？  —  How many people are in your family?
   NOT: きょうだいが さん いるから うちは にぎやかだ。 — a bare number cannot count anything in Japanese — three PEOPLE needs にん: きょうだいが さんにん いるから うちは にぎやかだ。

## Lesson 6 — あねは はたちだ — how old (ja-m17-neo-5)

## Lesson 9 — この ほん — pointing at a named thing (ja-m17-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — この ほん — pointing at a named thing
   You already own これ / それ / あれ / どれ — words that stand alone and mean "this one", "that one", "which one". この / その / あの / どの are the same four distances with one difference: they can NEVER stand alone. They must have a noun right behind them. 「これ」 is "this one"; 「この ほん」 is "this book". The distances are the ones you already know from ここ / そこ / あそこ: この is next to you, その is next to the person you are talking to, あの is away from you both, and どの asks "which one of them". That is the whole lesson — it is the third row of a table you have had since module 4.

## Lesson 10 — これ と この — standing alone or attached (ja-m17-neo-8)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — これ と この — standing alone or attached
   Two rows, one job. これ / それ / あれ / どれ ARE the thing, so they take a particle straight away: 「あれは あにの くるまだ」. この / その / あの / どの are not things at all — they are labels, and a label needs something to stick to: 「あの くるまは あにのだ」. Both sentences say exactly the same thing, and the only question is whether you name the object out loud. Name it and you need この; leave it unnamed and you need これ. The same split runs through the question words: どれ asks "which one?" with nothing named, どの asks "which bicycle?" with the noun spelled out.

      · それは おとうとの けいたいだ。  —  That is my younger brother's mobile phone.

## Lesson 11 — あの ひとは だれ？ — asking about people (ja-m17-neo-9)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — あの ひとは だれ？ — asking about people
   A question word sits exactly where its answer would sit, and it never takes は. だれ asks WHO — 「あの ひとは だれ？」 — and だれ plus の asks WHOSE — 「これは だれの かばん？」. The family questions run on the same rails and you now have all of them: 「かぞくは なんにん？」 for how many, 「なんさい？」 for how old, 「どの ひとが ミカの ともだちだ？」 for which one. Watch what comes back, though. Answering ABOUT your own family, every answer lands in the うち set — ちち, はは, あに, あね, おとうと, いもうと — because the moment you are speaking to an outsider, your family is the inside group.
      · これは だれの かばん？  —  Whose bag is this?
      · あの ひとは だれ？  —  Who is that person over there?
      · かぞくは なんにん？  —  How many people are in your family?
      · どの ひとが ミカの ともだちだ？  —  Which person is Mika's friend?
      · だれが この しゃしんを かった？  —  Who bought this photo?
   NOT: これは だれ かばん？ — asking WHOSE needs の between だれ and the noun — だれ on its own only ever means "who": これは だれの かばん？
