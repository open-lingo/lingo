last year — the wider calendar"), plus the new pack-word beats inside

`getMockLessonContent` from `src/features/lesson/data/mockLessons.ts` and

`m11-neo.test.ts` all pass (130/131, 1 skipped) — intro-order and atom↔module

don't check: semantic fairness, distractor plausibility, rule consistency,

## Findings

### Finding 1 — BLOCKER-class (SRS data integrity, not in-lesson confusion): した/きた atom-id collision corrupts future 下/北 flashcards

**Where:** `ja-m11-neo-review-3-s-3` (exercisedAtoms includes `"shita"`) and
`ja-m11-neo-review-3-s-9` (exercisedAtoms includes `"kita"`). Also present but
inert in `ja-m11-neo-5-s-0`, `-s-1`, `-s-3`, `-s-5`, `-challenge` (content
lesson, not review).

**What the learner sees on screen:** nothing wrong — `ごご さんじに かいものを
した` ("I did the shopping at three in the afternoon") and `きのう たなかさんが
きた` ("Tanaka came yesterday") are both fair, correctly-taught sentences.

**What actually happens (verified by code trace, not speculation):**
`courseAtoms.ts` deliberately does NOT register した/きた (irregular past of
する/くる, taught in `ja-m11-neo-5` "The rebels") as atoms, because those kana
already belong to unrelated m17/"future" nouns — 下 (`id: "shita"`, "below",
`fromModule: "m17"`) and 北 (`id: "kita"`, "north", `fromModule: "future"`)
(`courseAtoms.ts:1014-1018`, `:507`, `:575`). The comment there says "nothing
is lost but the duplicate row" — true for display/tokenization, but the
IR/compiler still emits `"shita"`/`"kita"` as literal strings in
`exercisedAtoms`, and those strings collide with the real courseAtoms ids.
`LessonPage.tsx:602-621` grades every id in a step's `exercisedAtoms` via
`getCardState(atomId)` / `setCardState(atomId, state)`
(`srsStorage.ts:164-168`), which is a **flat, unnamespaced keystore** — no
module or kind check. For content lessons this is harmless: `ja-m11-neo-5`
gates through `shouldWriteContentReviewAtom(atomId, lessonId)`
(`reviewTailSrs.ts:63-78`), which resolves `"shita"`/`"kita"` to their REAL
courseAtoms rows (m17/future), finds those modules aren't prior to m11, and
skips the write. But **review lessons bypass that gate entirely**
(`LessonPage.tsx:611`: `if (!isReviewLesson && !shouldWriteContentReviewAtom(...)) continue;`
— `isReviewLesson` is true for `ja-m11-neo-review-3`, so the `!isReviewLesson`
short-circuits and the atom grades unconditionally). The result: completing
`ja-m11-neo-review-3-s-3`/`-s-9` writes FSRS review history onto the
localStorage cards keyed `"shita"` and `"kita"` — which are 下 and 北 — five
to six modules before the learner has ever been taught those words. This is a
direct violation of the pinned invariant "every atom must be introduced
before any SRS review exercises it" (CLAUDE.md, machine-enforced by
`moduleConformance.test.ts`), and the automated test misses it specifically
because the collision routes around the `fromModule` check rather than
failing it. Consequence at m17: the flashcard reviewer will show a card for
下 that already has (irrelevant) review reps and possibly non-"new" FSRS
state, even though the learner has never seen it.

**Fix direction:** give the irregular past-tense forms their own
non-colliding ids in the IR (the codebase already has this pattern —
`いった` is registered as `itta-iku`, not `itta`, for exactly this reason).
Rename `した`→something like `shita-suru` and `きた`→`kita-kuru` at the IR
source (`ir/m11.ir.yaml:178` and the parallel きた row) and recompile.

### Finding 2 — MAJOR: 6 of 7 new weekday words get no recognition-only rep in `ja-m11-neo-10`, and 4 are inconsistently un-blocked for image-MCQ

**Where:** `ja-m11-neo-10` as a whole; sharpest at `ja-m11-neo-10-s-0`
(どようび's very first exposure anywhere in the course, a `build_sentence`)
and `ja-m11-neo-10-s-2` (かようび's *second-ever* exposure, a `listening_build`
— audio-only reconstruction from tiles, no intervening recognition check).

**What the learner sees:** the rule card introduces all seven day names at
once (with per-word English glosses embedded in the prose — legitimate
introduction). The very next step is a `build_sentence` demanding どようびに
production. Across the whole lesson, the closing `match_pairs` review
(`ja-m11-neo-10-rev-match-review`) reviews じゅぎょう/うみ/ともだち/かいもの/
みせ/いえ — **not one of the seven weekday words**. The lesson's one
recognition-style check for a weekday word is the `particle_cloze` at
`cloze-6`, which drills only もくようび. So げつようび, かようび, すいようび,
きんようび, どようび, and にちようび each get **zero** pure
recognition-before-production rep inside this lesson — every touch is
`build_sentence`/`listening_build` (production) or embedded inside a full
sentence in `listening_comprehension`/`dialogue_listen` (which tests
comprehension of the whole sentence, not the day-word specifically).

Root cause, confirmed in `courseAtoms.ts`: three weekday atoms
(もくようび/かようび/きんようび) are correctly `blocked: true` with notes like
"day-of-week label; needs text not image" — the authors clearly recognized
that a generic 📅 emoji can't visually distinguish one weekday from another.
But **four siblings with the identical problem are not blocked**
(げつようび/どようび/にちようび/すいようび all carry emoji `📅` and no
`blocked` flag), which per `moduleBarGuards.ts:436-493` ("imageable
module-new atoms debut on word_image_mcq — invariant 30") should require a
`word_image_mcq` debut that the lesson never gives them. This isn't caught
because `m11-neo.test.ts`'s `registerModuleBarGuards()` call never passes
`requireImageFirst: true` — the one m11-specific guard invocation in the
repo, unlike sibling modules that do enable it. I can't tell from the data
whether that was a deliberate call (all seven weekday words share the same
"no glyph distinguishes" problem, so no image MCQ would help) or a gap left
when 3 of 7 were blocked and the other 4 were missed — either way, the
inconsistency (3 blocked, 4 not, guard off entirely) is a real anomaly and
the practical effect is a lesson with lighter recognition scaffolding for
new headline vocab than the course's own invariant asks for.

**Fairness note:** this is softened somewhat by build_sentence handing the
learner a readable どようび *tile* rather than requiring spelling from
nothing, so it isn't a "spell it cold" ask — but it is still a production
task (correct placement relative to に/を/する) on a word the learner has
seen exactly once, in prose form, ~30 seconds earlier.

### Finding 3 — MINOR: 「AとBに」 (two weekdays sharing one trailing に) is drilled in `review-1` before ever being modeled

**Where:** `ja-m11-neo-review-1-s-10` (すいようびと もくようびに …),
`-s-12` (きんようびと どようびに …), `-s-11` (げつようびと かようびに …) — three
separate exercises.

と (and) and に (time marker) are both individually known, but the specific
combinatorial fact — that two day names joined by と share a single trailing
に rather than each taking its own (げつようびに と かようびに would be the
naive extension) — is never modeled anywhere in the teaching lesson
(`ja-m11-neo-10`) before review-1 drills it three times. In practice this is
low-risk: all three are `build_sentence`/`listening_build` with a finite tile
set that contains exactly one に tile, so the correct structure is
discoverable by elimination rather than requiring the learner to already
know the rule. Worth a one-line mention in `ja-m11-neo-10`'s rule card or an
extra example sentence if the pack gets revisited, not urgent enough to
block ship.

## What the learner actually experiences

**`ja-m11-neo-10`:** the rule card is genuinely good — the mnemonic chain
(moon→Monday, fire→Tuesday, water→Wednesday, wood→Thursday, gold→Friday,
earth→Saturday, sun→Sunday) gives the learner something to hang all seven
names on at once, and the あさって contrast lands cleanly against lesson 3's
already-familiar relative-time-takes-no-に rule. But the drilling that
follows leans hard on production: the learner is handed a tile bank and
asked to build "I'll take a walk on Saturday" as literally the second thing
that happens in the lesson, with no lower-stakes "which one means Saturday"
checkpoint first. By the end of the lesson they've produced most of the
seven days at least once, but if asked cold "what does きんようび mean" in
isolation, nothing in the lesson specifically drilled that skill — only
もくようび got that treatment (via the particle_cloze). It's not confusing
exactly, just heavier than the course's own stated bar for new vocabulary.

**`ja-m11-neo-11`:** noticeably better-scaffolded. おぼえる gets the
textbook-correct picture-MCQ debut before it's ever asked in production, the
せん/らい/まい prefix system is taught as one coherent stackable idea with
clear directional language ("せん points back," "らい points forward," "まい
means every"), and every practice sentence reinforces the same relative-word-
takes-no-に rule the learner already trusts from lessons 3 and 10. Nothing
here produced hesitation walking it as a zero-knowledge learner.

**`ja-m11-neo-review-1` / `-review-3`:** both feel like fair, well-paced
consolidation of the new material, mixing plain and polite registers and
reusing the character canon (Mika/Tom/Ken/Tanaka) consistently. The one
thing a learner would never notice, because it happens invisibly in the
background, is finding 1 — the local storage corruption on 下/北's future
flashcards. Nothing on screen tips them off.
