## Lesson 2 — Talking up — ます (ja-m7-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Talking up — ます
   Polite verbs are built from the i-STEM: drop the final -u to the い row, then add ます. のむ → のみ + ます. る-verbs just drop る: たべる → たべ + ます. する → します, くる → きます. The MEANING doesn't change — たべる and たべます both mean 'eat'. Only the audience changes. The people you talk about politely wear さん — the everyday 'Mr./Ms.' you attach to someone else's name (たなか → たなかさん), never to your own. When you name who you did it WITH, that person takes と: 「ともだちと のみます」 = I drink with a friend. と joins people to the action; に, which you met marking where something is, never does this job. One more polite word arrives with ます: はい, the polite 'yes'. 「はい、たべます。」 = Yes, I'll eat.
      · たべます  —  I eat. (polite)
      · みずを のみます  —  I drink water. (polite)
   NOT: たべるます — the る is dropped before ます — たべます, not たべるます

## Lesson 3 — Every verb, politely (ja-m7-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Every verb, politely — the three classes
   Finding the stem is the whole trick, and each class finds it differently. る-verbs drop る: みる → み + ます. う-verbs shift the last kana up to the い row: いく → いき + ます, かう → かい + ます, きく → きき + ます. する and くる obey neither rule — they are します and きます, and you learn that pair by heart. Every polite form in the language is built on this one move. One of these verbs brings a particle with it: いく names where you are going with に — 「いえに いきます」, I'm going home. That is the same に you met marking where something IS; with a motion verb it marks where you ARRIVE. で is still for where you DO something.
      · しゃしんを みます。  —  I look at the photo. (polite)
      · ほんを かいます。  —  I buy the book. (polite)
      · いえに いきます。  —  I go home. (polite)
      · ともだちが きます。  —  A friend is coming. (polite)
   NOT: しゃしんを みるます。 — る-verbs drop る before ます — みる→みます, never みるます.

## Lesson 4 — Politely saying no — ません (ja-m7-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Politely saying no — ません
   The polite negative swaps ます for ません — same stem, different tail. のみます → のみません. It is the polite twin of plain ない: のまない = のみません.
      · のみません  —  I don't drink. (polite)

      · ごはんを たべません  —  I don't eat rice. (polite)

## Lesson 6 — です — the noun's polite finish (ja-m7-neo-4)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — です — the noun's polite finish
   です finishes NOUNS politely, the way だ finishes them plainly: ねこだ → ねこです. です is NOT 'polite だ' you can bolt onto verbs — たべるです is not a sentence. Verbs get ます; nouns get です. Any noun sits in that slot: がくせい (a student), せんせい (a teacher), アメリカじん (an American — a country name plus じん 'person' names the nationality). Place words are nouns too, so あそこ ('over there') takes です — 「じゅぎょうは あそこです」 — and, like any noun, a place can pin down another noun with の: 「あそこの ひと」 is 'the person over there', the same の you know from 「ミカの ほん」.
      · がくせいです  —  I'm a student. (polite)
      · たなかさんは せんせいです  —  Tanaka is a teacher. (polite)
   NOT: たべるです — verbs take ます, not です — たべます

## Lesson 8 — Who are you talking to? (ja-m7-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Who are you talking to?
   Japanese picks its form by WHO you're talking to, not by what you mean. Friends and family get plain (たべる). Teachers, strangers, and customers get polite (たべます). ## Lesson 12 — Names carry register too (ja-m7-neo-8)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — Names carry register too — さん, さま, くん, ちゃん
   A name carries register exactly the way a verb does. さん is the everyday default for anyone who is not family or a close friend — トムさん — and it never goes on your own name. ちゃん and くん are for people you are close to or senior to: ちゃん reads young or affectionate, くん leans male and is what a teacher calls a boy in class. さま is the formal one — envelopes, and shop staff talking to you. With a real friend you drop the suffix altogether.
      · トムさんは アメリカじんです。  —  Tom is American. (polite)
      · ミカちゃんは がくせいです。  —  Mika is a student. (affectionate)
   NOT: トムさんは わたしさんです。 — a suffix is something you give other people — never attach さん to your own name.
