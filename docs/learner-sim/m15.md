# m15 — Relative clauses + こと / とき

Module 15 · clauses in front of nouns

Japanese has no relative pronoun — you park a whole sentence in front of a noun and the verb does not change: ほんを みる ひと is the person who looks at books. The clause verb stays plain, its own subject takes が, and the same shape then gives you こと (naming an action), とき (saying when) and the ordering pair まえに / てから.

## Lesson 1 — ほんを みる ひと — a sentence in front of a noun (ja-m15-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — ほんを みる ひと — a sentence in front of a noun
   A relative clause in Japanese is just a sentence parked in front of a noun. There is no "that", no "which", no "who" — you put the whole sentence there and the noun it lands in front of is what the sentence is about. 「ほんを みる」 is a sentence; 「ほんを みる ひと」 is the person who looks at books. Nothing is added and nothing is reordered. One rule only: the verb inside the clause stays PLAIN. ます is the politeness of the whole SENTENCE, and it can only ever sit on the last verb — never inside a clause.
      · ほんを みる ひとが いる。  —  There is a person who looks at books.
      · ちゃを のむ ひとは ミカだ。  —  The person who drinks tea is Mika.
      · うみで およぐ ひとが すきだ。  —  I like people who swim in the sea.
      · ごはんを たべる じかんが ない。  —  There is no time to eat.
      · きっさてんで はたらく ひとと あそぶ。  —  I hang out with the person who works at the coffee shop.
   NOT: ほんを みます ひとが いる。 — the verb inside a clause stays PLAIN — ます is the politeness of the whole sentence and only ever sits on the LAST verb: ほんを みる ひとが いる。

## Lesson 2 — きのう かった ほん — clauses in the past (ja-m15-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — きのう かった ほん — clauses in the past
   Put the clause in the past and nothing else changes — the plain past sits in front of the noun exactly where the plain non-past did. 「きのう かった ほん」 = the book I bought yesterday. Who did the buying is usually left out and the rest of the sentence makes it obvious. And there is never a の between the clause and its noun: の joins two NOUNS (ミカの ほん), never a clause to the noun it describes.
      · きのう かった ほんを みた。  —  I looked at the book I bought yesterday.
      · あさ のんだ ちゃは おいしい。  —  The tea I drank in the morning is delicious.
      · きのう いった みせは ふるい。  —  The shop I went to yesterday is old.
      · きのう きいた うたが すきだ。  —  I like the song I heard yesterday.
      · きのう たべた すしは たかい。  —  The sushi I ate yesterday is expensive.
   NOT: きのう かった の ほんを みた。 — の joins two NOUNS — it never joins a clause to the noun it describes, so the clause sits straight in front: きのう かった ほんを みた。

## Lesson 3 — ミカが かった ほん — who did it, inside the clause (ja-m15-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — ミカが かった ほん — who did it, inside the clause
   Give the clause its own subject and that subject takes が: 「ミカが かった ほん」 = the book Mika bought. The が belongs to the clause alone and never reaches outside it. は cannot go there. は marks the topic of the WHOLE sentence and a sentence gets one topic, so a は inside a clause collides with the one outside it. Rule of thumb while you learn this: outside the clause は, inside the clause が.
      · わたしは ミカが かった ほんを みた。  —  I looked at the book Mika bought.
      · ケンが きいた うたは おもしろい。  —  The song Ken heard is interesting.
      · たなかが おしえる じゅぎょうは おもしろい。  —  The class Tanaka teaches is interesting.
      · ミカが かった いえは しずかだ。  —  The house Mika bought is quiet.
      · わたしが かった かばんは やすい。  —  The bag I bought is cheap.
   NOT: わたしは ミカは かった ほんを みた。 — one sentence, one は — the clause's own subject takes が instead: わたしは ミカが かった ほんを みた。

## Lesson 5 — たべる こと — turning an action into a thing (ja-m15-neo-4)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — たべる こと — turning an action into a thing
   You already turn a verb into a thing with の — 「たべるのが すきだ」. こと does the identical job: 「たべる ことが すきだ」. And it is the same shape you have been building all module — a plain clause in front of a noun — except the noun is こと, "the act of", so the clause becomes something you can like, hate, or make the subject of a sentence. Both are right; の is the spoken half and こと the written half. こと is also an ordinary noun meaning "thing" in the abstract sense: 「わからない ことが ある」 = there is something I don't understand.
      · うみで およぐ ことが すきだ。  —  I like swimming in the sea.
      · ほんを みる ことが すきだ。  —  I like looking at books.
      · あさ はたらく ことは きらいだ。  —  I hate working in the morning.
      · ともだちと あそぶ ことは おもしろい。  —  Hanging out with friends is fun.
      · わからない ことが ある。  —  There is something I don't understand.
   NOT: うみで およぐが すきだ。 — a verb cannot be marked が on its own — こと (or の) turns it into a thing first: うみで およぐ ことが すきだ。

## Lesson 6 — いく とき — saying when (ja-m15-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — いく とき — saying when
   とき is just the noun "time", and by now you know how to describe a noun: park a clause in front of it. 「にほんに いく とき」 = the time when I go to Japan → when I go to Japan. What sits in front follows the same rules as everything else that describes a noun — a verb goes in plain, a noun takes の (がくせいの とき = when I was a student), a な-adjective takes な (げんきな とき). While you are learning it, keep the two halves in the same tense: a 〜る とき clause with a non-past sentence, a 〜た とき clause with a past one.

## Lesson 9 — たべる まえに — before doing (ja-m15-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — たべる まえに — before doing
   まえ means "before". Put a clause in front of it — the same shape as always — add に, and you have "before doing X": 「だいがくに いく まえに ごはんを たべる」 = before going to university, I eat. Here is the piece that catches everybody: the verb in front of まえに is ALWAYS the dictionary form, even when the whole thing happened yesterday. まえに only says "earlier than"; the tense is decided by the verb at the END of the sentence. 「たべた まえに」 is never Japanese. A noun takes の, as usual: 「じゅぎょうの まえに」.
      · だいがくに いく まえに ごはんを たべる。  —  Before going to university, I eat.
      · ちゃを のむ まえに ほんを みた。  —  Before drinking tea, I looked at a book.
      · きっさてんに いく まえに かばんを かった。  —  Before going to the coffee shop, I bought a bag.
      · じゅぎょうの まえに ともだちと あそんだ。  —  Before class, I hung out with a friend.
      · うみで およぐ まえに みずを のんだ。  —  Before swimming in the sea, I drank water.
   NOT: だいがくに いった まえに ごはんを たべる。 — まえに always takes the DICTIONARY form, whatever the tense of the sentence — the verb at the end carries the tense: だいがくに いく まえに ごはんを たべる。

## Lesson 10 — たべてから — after doing (ja-m15-neo-8)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — たべてから — after doing
   The other half of the pair. Take the て-form you already own, hang から on it, and you have "after doing X": 「ごはんを たべてから だいがくに いく」 = after eating, I go to university. て on its own already links actions in the order they happen; てから adds that the first one is FINISHED before the second starts.
