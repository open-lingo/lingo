# m18 — Saying & thinking: とおもう + という

Module 18 · Quotation

と is the quotation particle: it closes a whole plain sentence and hands it to a verb of thinking or saying. 「あした いくと おもう」 = "I think I'll go tomorrow" — nothing is reordered and nothing is added, and the clause in front of と stays PLAIN however polite the rest of the sentence gets. The same と names things with いう (「トムと いう ひと」, a person called Tom), and the first kanji reading set asks you to read the glyphs you have been seeing since module 8.

## Lesson 1 — 「あした いくと おもう」 — handing a sentence to おもう (ja-m18-neo-1)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「あした いくと おもう」 — handing a sentence to おもう
   と is a QUOTATION particle. It closes a quoted clause and hands it to a verb of thinking or saying: 「あした いくと おもう」 = I think I'll go tomorrow. Look at what is in front of と — 「あした いく」 — it is a complete plain sentence, the one you would say on its own. Nothing is reordered, nothing is added, nothing is dropped. You have done this before: in module 15 you parked a whole sentence in front of a noun to describe it. This is the same move with a different landing place. One limit worth knowing now: 〜と おもう states YOUR OWN opinion. For what somebody ELSE thinks, Japanese says 'to omotte iru' — a later lesson. Everything you build here is what YOU think.
      · あした いくと おもう。  —  I think I'll go tomorrow.
      · ミカは あした くると おもう。  —  I think Mika is coming tomorrow.
      · ケンは えいがを みると おもう。  —  I think Ken will watch the movie.
      · たなかは わかると おもう。  —  I think Tanaka understands.
      · トムは うちに いると おもう。  —  I think Tom is at home.
   NOT: あした いくを おもう。 — を marks a THING you act on, and a whole sentence is not a thing — a quoted clause is handed over with と: あした いくと おもう。

## Lesson 2 — 「たべますと おもう」 は ✗ — the quote stays plain (ja-m18-neo-2)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「たべますと おもう」 は ✗ — the quote stays plain
   Politeness lives on the FINAL verb of the sentence and nowhere else. Whatever you park in front of と stays PLAIN — always, without exception. 「ミカは すしを たべると おもう」 is the casual sentence; speaking politely you change only the last word, to 'omoimasu', and the たべる inside the quotation does not move. 「たべますと おもう」 is not stiff Japanese, it is wrong Japanese. This is why the course taught you plain form first: the quoted clause is the plain sentence you have been building since module 5, and ます is a coat you put on the last word only.
      · ミカは すしを たべると おもう。  —  I think Mika will eat sushi.
      · トムは ほんを かうと おもう。  —  I think Tom will buy a book.
      · ケンは きょう はたらくと おもう。  —  I think Ken will work today.
      · ミカは えきで まつと おもう。  —  I think Mika will wait at the station.
      · たなかは ミカに おしえると おもう。  —  I think Tanaka will teach Mika.
   NOT: ミカは すしを たべますと おもう。 — the clause in front of と is ALWAYS plain — politeness belongs to the last verb only, never inside the quotation: ミカは すしを たべると おもう。

## Lesson 3 — 「こないと おもう」 — where the ない goes (ja-m18-neo-3)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「こないと おもう」 — where the ない goes
   English and Japanese negate different halves of this sentence, and the mismatch is the whole lesson. English says "I DON'T think he's coming". Japanese says 「こないと おもう」 — literally "I think he isn't coming". The ない goes INSIDE the quotation, on the quoted verb, and おもう stays positive. Every plain negative you own works there: いかない, たべない, みない, わからない, こない, いない, ない, and the noun negative じゃない. There IS a form 'omowanai', but it is a much heavier "I do not think so" — a flat rejection, not the everyday hedge. Negate the clause, not the thinking.
      · ケンは こないと おもう。  —  I don't think Ken is coming.
      · ミカは えいがを みないと おもう。  —  I don't think Mika will watch the movie.
      · トムは わからないと おもう。  —  I don't think Tom understands.
      · たなかは きょう こないと おもう。  —  I don't think Tanaka is coming today.
      · ミカは すしを たべないと おもう。  —  I don't think Mika will eat sushi.
   NOT: ケンは くないと おもう。 — くる is irregular — its plain negative is こない, never くない: ケンは こないと おもう。

## Lesson 5 — 「がくせいだと おもう」 — quoting nouns and adjectives (ja-m18-neo-4)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「がくせいだと おもう」 — quoting nouns and adjectives
   Not every opinion is about an action, and what you quote decides what has to be there. A NOUN sentence keeps its だ: 「ミカは がくせいだと おもう」 — the だ is what makes 「ミカは がくせい」 a sentence at all, so it cannot be dropped in front of と. A な-adjective behaves like a noun and keeps だ too: 「しずかだと おもう」. An い-ADJECTIVE is already a whole sentence by itself and takes NO だ: 「おいしいと おもう」, never 'oishii da to'. Three shapes, one question every time: is the thing in front of と a complete plain sentence? If it is, と will take it.
      · ミカは がくせいだと おもう。  —  I think Mika is a student.
      · この すしは おいしいと おもう。  —  I think this sushi is delicious.
      · あの きっさてんは しずかだと おもう。  —  I think that coffee shop over there is quiet.
      · この ほんは おもしろいと おもう。  —  I think this book is interesting.
      · たなかは せんせいだと おもう。  —  I think Tanaka is a teacher.
   NOT: ミカは がくせいと おもう。 — a noun on its own is not a sentence — だ is what finishes it, and it survives in front of と: ミカは がくせいだと おもう。

## Lesson 6 — 「トムと いう ひと」 — naming things (ja-m18-neo-5)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「トムと いう ひと」 — naming things
   The same と, a different verb, a different job. 〜と いう in front of a NOUN names it: 「トムと いう ひと」 = a person called Tom. と closes the name exactly as it closed a quoted sentence, and いう hands it to the noun behind. Reach for it the first time you mention something your listener does not know yet — 「さくらと いう みせ」 a shop called Sakura, 「ミカと いう がくせい」 a student called Mika. To ask, 「なんと いう ひと？」 — what is the person called? Once the name is common ground you drop the frame and just say the name. Same verb, plain quotation: 「ミカは くると いう」 = Mika says she's coming.
      · トムと いう ひとが くる。  —  A person called Tom is coming.
      · さくらと いう みせで かう。  —  I buy it at a shop called Sakura.
      · ミカと いう がくせいが いる。  —  There's a student called Mika.
      · これは さくらと いう はなだ。  —  This is a flower called sakura.
      · あれは なんと いう はな？  —  What is that flower called?
   NOT: トムの いう ひとが くる。 — の joins two nouns as owner and owned; a NAME is handed to its noun with と いう: トムと いう ひとが くる。

## Lesson 7 — 「もう たべたと おもう」 — the quote keeps its own tense (ja-m18-neo-6)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「もう たべたと おもう」 — the quote keeps its own tense
   The quoted clause carries its own tense and おもう stays in the present, because the thinking is happening now. 「ミカは もう たべたと おもう」 = I think Mika has already eaten — the past sits on たべた, inside the quotation, where the eating happened. Every plain past you own goes in: たべた, のんだ, みた, かった, きいた, わかった, あそんだ, and the noun past だった. There is a sentence 「たべたと おもった」 with the past on BOTH verbs, but it means "I thought she had eaten" — a report about your past self — and it is rarer than beginners expect. Keep おもう in the present and let the quotation do the time-travel.
      · ミカは もう たべたと おもう。  —  I think Mika has already eaten.
      · ケンは えいがを みたと おもう。  —  I think Ken watched the movie.
      · トムは ほんを かったと おもう。  —  I think Tom bought a book.
      · ミカは わかったと おもう。  —  I think Mika understood.
      · ケンは きのう ともだちと あそんだと おもう。  —  I think Ken played with a friend yesterday.
   NOT: ミカは もう たべただと おもう。 — a past VERB is already a whole sentence — だ finishes NOUNS, never verbs: ミカは もう たべたと おもう。

## Lesson 9 — 「ミカに いう」 と 「ミカと はなす」 — who the words go to (ja-m18-neo-7)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「ミカに いう」 と 「ミカと はなす」 — who the words go to
   A quotation needs somebody on the other end, and the particle you pick says what that person is to the sentence. に marks the person the words go TO: 「ミカに あした いくと いう」 = I'll tell Mika I'm going tomorrow. と marks the person you do it WITH: 「ミカと はなす」 = I talk with Mika — and that is the ordinary "and / with" と you have had since module 8, sitting one word away from the quotation と in the same sentence. きく takes both readings: 「ミカに きく」 is asking her, while 「ミカは くると きく」 is hearing that she'll come. Read the whole sentence, not the particle on its own.
      · ミカに あした いくと いう。  —  I'll tell Mika I'm going tomorrow.
      · トムに この みせは やすいと いう。  —  I'll tell Tom this shop is cheap.
      · たなかに わからないと いう。  —  I'll tell Tanaka I don't understand.
      · ケンは あした こないと きく。  —  I hear Ken isn't coming tomorrow.
      · ともだちに この ほんは おもしろいと おしえる。  —  I'll tell my friend this book is interesting.

## Lesson 10 — 人 · 水 · 行く — reading the kanji you already know (ja-m18-neo-8)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 人 · 水 · 行く — reading the kanji you already know
   You have been reading kanji since module 8 without ever being asked to prove it — the words you know quietly changed clothes, and their kana floated above until the glyph had been on screen long enough. From here the oldest ones read bare, so the question becomes a plain one: what does this say? A Japanese word carries its MEANING in the kanji and its SOUND in the kana around it, which is why 大きい is one glyph plus きい and the きい never changes. Most glyphs answer to two readings, a native Japanese one and a borrowed Chinese one — 三 is the みっ of みっつ and the さん you count with — so the same character can sound different in different words, and the word tells you which. You are never asked to WRITE one. Reading is the whole job.

## Lesson 11 — 「どう おもう？」 — asking for an opinion (ja-m18-neo-9)

1. [grammar_rule] RULE CARD — 「どう おもう？」 — asking for an opinion
   To ask what somebody thinks you need no new word, only the question you already own. 「どう おもう？」 is "what do you think?" — Japanese asks HOW you think about it, not WHAT. To test one specific opinion, quote it and hand it over: 「たかいと おもう？」 = do you think it's expensive? In casual speech the question is carried by the rising contour alone and か stays away; か comes back when you speak politely. 「なんと いう？」 asks what a thing is called. One thing you will HEAR constantly and are not asked to produce yet: Japanese often ends an explanation with 'n da' — 'takai n da', "the thing is, it's expensive" — which is a later lesson. Answering is the frame you have built all module: quote your opinion, add と, finish with おもう.
      · ミカは くると おもう？  —  Do you think Mika is coming?
      · どう おもう？  —  What do you think?
      · この みせは たかいと おもう？  —  Do you think this shop is expensive?
      · あれは なんと いう はな？  —  What is that flower called?
