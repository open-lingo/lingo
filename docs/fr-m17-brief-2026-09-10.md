## 2. Module thesis (design authority for this dispatch)

*"You already count to soixante-neuf. Eleven irregular words, and a handful
of composition rules, close the gap all the way to cent."* m17 is not a new
grammatical machine — it is the second and final installment of the number
system m12 opened, finishing the job with the two irregular zones French
pedagogy conventionally splits off: the teens (11–19, historically fused
forms, not tens+units compounds) and the 70–99 band (which does NOT
introduce new tens words past `soixante` — it recycles the teens inside a
vigesimal, base-20 counting fossil: 70 = "sixty-ten," 80 = "four-twenties,"
90 = "four-twenty-ten"). The teaching claim is explicitly that these are
**irregular, memorized exceptions layered on an already-known compositional
system** — not a new machine, a patch to the one from m12.

Three zones, taught in this order, never mixed silently:
1. **The teens (L1–L3):** six genuinely new, non-compositional words
   (onze…seize) plus three free compositions from already-taught pieces
   (dix-sept/dix-huit/dix-neuf = dix + sept/huit/neuf, exactly m12's
   hyphenated-tile convention). Debut `onze`'s elision exception explicitly,
   contrasted against an already-known vowel-elision case, so it reads as
   an exception to a known rule rather than an unexplained inconsistency.
2. **70–79, riding the teens (L4–L5):** no new atoms. `soixante` (m12) +
   `dix`/`onze`…`dix-neuf` (m1/m17 teens) = 70–79, proving the teens just
   taught are reused, not a closed set. The irregular `et` in «soixante et
   onze» (71) — present here exactly as it was at 21/31/41/51/61 (m12) — is
   the bridge; its ABSENCE at 81/91 (next zone) is the trap this sets up.
3. **80–99, the base-20 fossil + the checkpoint (L6–L8):** `quatre-vingts`
   (80, `quatre` + `vingt`, with a silent trailing -s that appears ONLY at
   the round 80 and disappears the instant a unit follows) and
   `quatre-vingt-dix` (90, + `dix`, no -s) anchor the zone; 81–99 compose
   from these plus the teens, explicitly WITHOUT «et» (quatre-vingt-un, not
   *quatre-vingt-et-un) — the module's central irregularity, taught by
   direct contrast against L4's «soixante et onze».

## 5. Homophone / liaison risks

- **No new `homophoneKey` pairs required.** Verified (§1): every new or
  composed number in this module's scope is phonetically distinct from
  every other — no minimal pairs the way allé/allée or grand/grande were.
  This is a genuinely lower audio-risk module than m14/m15/m16.
- **«onze» — the elision/liaison landmine.** Vowel-spelled but blocks BOTH
  («le onze», never «l'onze»; «les onze» has no liaison either, unlike
  «les‿amis», m10). `consonantOnset: true` is mandatory at registration
  (§1, §3) — `m17.test.ts` must independently pin "no article-elision
  string uses «l'onze»" the same way earlier modules pin their own
  consonant-onset exceptions, because a missed flag silently produces a
  well-formed-looking wrong form with no gate catching it otherwise.
- **The silent trailing -s on «quatre-vingts» (80 only)** is an
  ORTHOGRAPHY-ONLY rule, not a `failOnHomophoneDistractor()`-class risk —
  «quatre-vingts» and «quatre-vingt-un» are never graded as alternate
  answers to the same referent (they're different numbers, 80 vs 81).
  Still: never quiz the -s presence/absence as an isolated spelling MCQ
  divorced from the number itself; L6's info card is the one place this
  gets taught, by contrast, quoting both forms.
- **The «et» irregularity is the module's real teaching trap.** Present at
  «soixante et onze» (71, L4, continuing m12's 21/31/41/51/61 pattern) and
  ABSENT at «quatre-vingt-un» (81) and «quatre-vingt-onze» (91, L6–L7).
  Both L4 and L6 info cards must quote the contrasting course sentences
  explicitly — this is not inferable from the compositional pattern alone
  and native French pedagogy treats it as the single most commonly-missed
  point in this number range.
- **Liaison: «quatre-vingts euros» (s→z) and «soixante-dix euros»
  (x→z, dix's liaison)** are both real phonetic liaisons but, per m12's own
  precedent (§5 there — «vingt euros»), **recommend NO new `liaisonListen`
  item**: edge-tts produces the liaison correctly natively; nothing grades
  the phenomenon explicitly in m12 either, and this module should stay
  consistent rather than introduce a new class of graded liaison content.
- **`m13.test.ts`'s banned-lexical-item regex does not need updating** —
  verified (§1) it scopes to m13's own `LESSONS` only, not course-wide.
  m17 shipping onze/douze/…/quatre-vingt-dix is unaffected by and does not
  affect that pin.
