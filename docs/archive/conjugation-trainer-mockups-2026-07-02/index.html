Conjugation Trainer — three directions

The brief: make the trainer feel like a game — menu friendly for all ages, due markers that don't outweigh the content, no bloated form-toggle row. Grounded in two research traditions: kids' game menu psychology (Mario/Candy Crush saga maps, Toca Boca icon-first menus, Duolingo's single-path rationale) and airport/transit wayfinding (AIGA/DOT, Otl Aicher, Beck's Tube map, Tokyo Metro). All three directions share five fixes:

  • Progress lives ON the selector — each type shows its mastery as a fill, due as small pips; the menu is the dashboard (P2).
  • Each type owns a color + glyph + fixed position — identifiable without reading; six new theme tokens (--type-*) so themes restyle them (P4/P10). The kana glyph IS the icon: て・た・ない・ま・たい・い are already language-honest pictograms for this content.
  • The 12-checkbox row dies — free-drill config moves behind the free-drill entry (progressive disclosure, P11) and becomes family chips + a stack-depth switch, not per-form checkboxes.
  • One clear "next" — the highest-due type carries the single recommendation marker (P1).
  • Locked = visible carrot — the upcoming て-compounds type shows dimmed with its unlock condition, never hidden (P8). No streaks, no guilt.

What it is

The six types become medallion stops on a winding trail, ordered by unlock module — the same journey grammar as the Learn pathway, so the trainer immediately reads as "one of our games." The mastery ring around each medallion is the progress bar; due items are at most three small pips hanging off the medallion P2. The trail gives the six types a permanent geography — "past is the one down-left after the bend" P3.

The details that carry it

  • The recommendation chip breathes on exactly one stop P1; the locked て-compounds stop is a dimmed medallion with a factual unlock line P8.
  • Free drill is "Sparring" — a landmark off the trail (different shape: dashed pavilion). Its setup replaces 12 checkboxes with 4 family chips + a 3-position depth switch (Base / + Stacked / Everything) P5, P11.
  • Drill screen inherits the type's owned color: the progress bar is trail segments, the combo chip ticks quietly (in-session feedback survives the gamification backlash; streaks-across-days don't).

Trade-offs

Warmest and most kid-friendly of the three; strongest sense of journey. Costs vertical space (one screen ≈ 6 stops), and the trail implies sequence more strongly than conjugation strictly has.

What it is

The wayfinding metaphor, taken literally because it happens to be true of the content: a conjugation chain IS a route. Each form family is a colored line; the base form is the first station; stacked conjugations are the next stops down the same line (ない → なかった; たい → たくなかった; て → ている, てください… shown as future stops). Due items are the small filled core of a station; the "you are here" halo pulses on the recommended line P13, P1.

The details that carry it

  • Every station has a language-free address (T1–T5) and its line color follows into the drill, the results, and the cheat sheet P10.
  • The v2 te-compounds are already on the map as dimmed future stops — the roadmap is the UI, the carrot is built in P8.
  • Drill progress = a train moving stop-to-stop along the line strip. Finishing a ride "arrives"; no score anxiety, just arrival.
  • Free drill = the interchange station — semantically the place all lines meet; its config asks which lines to ride (5 chips), not which of 12 forms.

Trade-offs

Smartest and most language-agnostic; scales beautifully as stacked forms arrive (they're just new stops — no new UI). Reads slightly more "clever adult" than "kid game," though color+glyph keeps it pre-reader navigable. The map needs care at 320px widths.

What it is

The Toca-Boca answer: six chunky tiles, glyph-first, no reading required to navigate P4, P9. Mastery is ink filling the tile from the bottom — the sumi-ink conceit grounds it in the subject. Due items are little cards physically peeking over the tile's top edge ("things waiting for you"), capped at three — a 3-due type and a 9-due type look almost the same, which is exactly the point: no wall of red numbers P2, P8.

The details that carry it

  • Whole tile is the target; squash-press physics on tap (the app's existing 3D-button feel, turned up one notch) P6, P7.
  • The suggested tile wobbles once every few seconds — a nudge you notice, not a nag P1.
  • Mix tile spans the bottom in a washi-stripe pattern; its config sheet is the same family-chips + depth switch as Direction A P11.
  • Fixed grid positions forever — spatial memory does the navigation on the second visit P3.

Trade-offs

Lowest-anxiety, most toy-like, fastest to build (it's a grid). Weakest at conveying sequence and the stacked-form roadmap — the chains live inside the drill and cheat sheet rather than on the menu. Locked types are just a dimmed tile with an unlock line.

My take

B (Transit Map) is the one with a real idea in it — the metaphor isn't decoration, it's the actual structure of conjugation chains, and every v2 form we add becomes a new stop instead of new UI. If the goal weights "all ages + fastest ship" higher, C wins. A is the safe crowd-pleaser that matches the existing lesson pathway most closely. All three share the same fixes (due pips, owned colors, family-chip free drill, one "next" marker), so whichever you pick, the checkbox row and badge-wall die today. Mix-and-match notes welcome — e.g. B's map with C's press physics is a natural pairing.
