## 2. Module thesis (design authority for this dispatch)

*"«Suis»/«es»/«est» already told you who you are — now they tell you where
you've been."* m16 is the passé composé machine's second auxiliary: the
learner already owns "avoir chunk + participle = a completed action" from
m14; m16 reveals that a small, closed family of verbs — motion verbs, the
ones that answer "where did you go / come from" — use **être** instead,
and that the participle now **agrees with the subject like an adjective**
(m9's grand/grande pattern) rather than staying frozen (avoir's pattern).
This is the module's real teaching claim: *the SAME verb form the learner
has used since m2 for identity now does a second, unrelated job, and this
time the agreement it carries is invisible in speech.*

Three roles, taught in this order, never mixed silently:
1. **The bridge + the auxiliary switch (L1–L3):** one explicit info card
   naming all three faces of «être X» the learner has now met — «je suis
   Léa» (identity, m2), «il est grand» (adjective, m9), «je suis allé(e)»
   (event, m16) — same verb, three jobs; debut `aller` (bare infinitive)
   and `allé`; masculine forms first (L1–L2, reusing the male cast:
   Thomas, Hugo, Lucas), then `allée` against the female cast (L2–L3),
   making the written-only agreement contrast explicit and quoting BOTH
   course sentences side by side, the same ≤3-line-explanation discipline
   m15 used for halle/hôtel.
2. **A second verb, proving it's a family not a phrase (L4–L6):** debut
   `venir` (bare infinitive — a genuinely new verb, no present-tense
   chunk to lean on, unlike aller) and `venu`/`venue`; «venir de» reuses
   m2's already-taught «de» + m4 place nouns. Negation debuts here (L6):
   «je ne suis pas allé(e)» / «il n'est pas venu» / «elle n'est pas
   venue» compose for FREE — zero new atoms — from m13's proven ne…pas
   frame around already-bare être forms (see §1 evidence above); **«tu
   n'es pas» gets its first-ever live exercise in this module**, flagged
   because it is architecturally safe (elides for free) but textually
   novel.
3. **Composition with m14's time kit + optional 3rd verb (L7–L9):**
   "hier, je suis allé(e) au musée" / "elle est venue hier soir" thread
   m14's hier/déjà/pas encore through the être machine, proving the SAME
   time words work with either auxiliary; optional stretch `arrivé`/
   `arrivée` (§3) can debut here to show a third verb generalizing the
   pattern, mirroring m14's parlé / m15's content(e) optional-stretch
   convention.

1. **L1 «Je suis... allé»** — the bridge info card (§2.1): «je suis Léa»

   true. Debut `aller`, `allé`. Build "je suis allé au musée" / "tu es
   allé à la gare" (masculine forms only this lesson — male cast: Thomas,

2. **L2 «Il est allé, elle est allée»** — debut `allée`; first explicit
   written contrast, quoting both sentences: «il est allé» vs «elle est
   allée» — same sound, different spelling, the article-elision trick
   («l'») doesn't apply here, this is the OTHER kind of silent agreement.
   One line contrasting against m9's audible grand/grande so the learner
   isn't left assuming all être-agreement sounds different. `agreement_chain`
   debut for this module (written-only, no audioText — §1 evidence).
3. **L3 consolidation, je/tu/il/elle, no new atoms** — discrimination
   cloze/lc alternating allé/allée across the cast (Thomas est allé.../

4. **L4 «Je suis venu(e)»** — debut `venir` (bare infinitive, explicit

5. **L5 «Elle est venue»** — debut `venue`; second written-only silent

6. **L6 «Je ne suis pas allé(e)»** — no new atoms; debut the negation:

8. **L8 checkpoint** — zero-new, all-graded; aller/venir across je/tu/il/

## 5. Homophone / liaison risks

- **allé / allée — TRUE HOMOPHONE, [ale] either way.** This is the
  module's central risk and its whole teaching point at once: written-only
  distinction, NEVER graded by ear. `homophoneKey: "ale"` on both atoms
  makes any audio-bearing step that co-presents them architecturally
  impossible via `failOnHomophoneDistractor()` (§1) — but this guard only
  fires if the key is actually set on both atoms at registration time;
  `m16.test.ts` must independently pin "no audio-bearing step co-presents
  allé/allée" the way m14 pinned manger/mangé and m15 (expected) pins
  visiter/visité, because a missed `homophoneKey` field would silently
  disable the automatic guard.
- **venu / venue — same class, [vəny] either way.** Identical law:
  written-only, `homophoneKey` shared, own `m16.test.ts` pin.
- **arrivé / arrivée (if the stretch ships)** — same class, same rule.
- **est / es / à / a — no NEW collision this module.** m14 already
  flagged a/à (§7.4 there) and this module does not touch that pair
  again; `est` vs `es` are already distinct-enough by ear and pinned by
  m13's own machinery. No new liaison item recommended (per m12/m14/m15
  §5 precedent — nothing in this module's core sentences forces one).
- **«venir de» vs the untaught partitive «de»** — «venir de Paris»
  (origin, already-taught sense since m2's «je suis de Paris») must not
  be confused with the partitive «du/de la» (still deferred, playbook
  §6). Keep every `de` in this module in the origin sense only; don't let
  it read as licensing a partitive construction.
- **The copula/auxiliary collision (§2.1, §7.3) is NOT a phonetic
  homophone risk** — «je suis Léa» and «je suis allé» sound completely
  different once the rest of the sentence is spoken. It is a
  comprehensibility risk (same opening two words, unrelated job), not a
  listening risk; handled by the L1 bridge card, not by any audio-gating
  mechanism.

## 7. Decisions inferred (recorded, not parked)

1. **Être-auxiliary passé composé is m16's own module — re-verified, not
   re-litigated.** m14 §7.1 flagged it; m15 §7.2 resolved it against
   folding into m15 and named m16 as the recommended home. This brief
   re-checked the reasoning against code (§1) and found no code-level
   contradiction; the "one machine at a time" logic that drove m15's
   rejection is sound and this brief adopts it rather than re-deriving it.
2. **Verbs: aller + venir are core (given); arriver ships as ONE optional
   stretch pair; partir and rester are REJECTED outright.** Reasoning:
   aller's participle is regular (-er→-é, matches allé/mangé/visité's own
   formation — zero new irregularity); venir's participle is irregular
   (venu, not -ir→-i) — already a second new thing riding alongside the
   auxiliary switch itself, which is the deliberate "one extra thing"
   this module accepts (it's the whole point: prove être-auxiliary
   generalizes past the single verb "aller" the forward notes floated).
   Adding partir would introduce a THIRD irregular participle pattern
   (parti/partie, a different shape again) for no unique thematic gain
   over venir — both are "showed up" verbs; redundant. Adding rester
   ("to stay") is thematically flat against a go/come narrative and would
   be a fourth verb purely for coverage, which is the "volume, not
   coverage" mistake m15's own arc explicitly rejects. arriver is
   regular (-er→-é, zero new irregularity, matches allé's formation
   exactly) and thematically apt (pairs naturally with venir — "venu" and
   "arrivé" both describe someone showing up) — cheap enough to justify
   as the module's one optional stretch, same status class as m14's
   parlé and m15's content(e).
3. **Audible-silent agreement (allé/allée) is taught by explicit
   contrast against m9's audible pattern, not by silent extension.**
   The risk named in the task — "decide how the course teaches gender
   agreement without lying about sound" — is resolved by giving the
   learner ONE explicit sentence pairing an audible pair they already
   know (il est grand / elle est grande, m9) against this module's
   silent pair (il est allé / elle est allée) inside L2's info card,
   rather than assuming the m9 pattern transfers silently. This also
   corrects m15's own characterization (§7.2(b)) that the required
   machinery is "never exercised anywhere" — `homophoneKey` ships in
   three prior modules (§1); what's new is applying it to GENDER rather
   than number/person, which is a real but smaller gap than m15's brief
   implied.
4. **The copula/auxiliary collision («je suis» identity vs «je suis»
   auxiliary) gets its own dedicated L1 bridge card, not a silent
   reuse.** Unlike m14's avoir reuse (which mirrors English's own "I
   HAVE eaten" and needed no explicit bridge), être-auxiliary has NO
   English parallel — English never says "I am gone" for plain movement,
   it says "I have gone." This makes the reuse cognitively harder than
   avoir's was, not easier, despite both being "verb already known, new
   job." The L1 card explicitly sequences all three faces of «être X»
   the learner has now met (identity → adjective → event) so the third
   reads as a continuation of a pattern, not an unexplained collision.
   This is a genuine judgment call (the forward notes don't address it)
   with a stated position, per the escalation-filter norm.
5. **Self-referential je/tu gender grading is flagged as an open
   architecture question, not silently resolved.** For graded build/
   typed/speaking steps built around «je suis allé(e)», the learner's
   own gender is unknown to the course. Recommend: put graded je/tu
   positions on FIXED-GENDER cast members inside dialogue framing (e.g.
   a first-person line voiced as a specific named NPC/cast member whose
   gender is established) rather than an anonymous free-standing "je"
   prompt with only one correct spelling; reserve any genuinely anonymous
   self-reference for recognition-only steps (no single graded spelling)
   or confirm `alsoAccepted` (playbook, "Build alternates") can express
   an allé/allée alternate within a build's tile bank — **traced, not
   executed**: the mechanism as documented is for alternate TILE ORDERS
   from one bank, not confirmed to cover swapping which of two gendered
   tiles is "correct." Flag as UNVERIFIED; resolve in `m16.test.ts`
   before drafting L1/L4's graded build banks, not after.
