**FR-specific wrinkle worth naming (not present in ES):** FR's singular -er
endings (je/tu/il/elle/on) are pronounced **identically** — «parle»/«parles»
are homophones (/paʁl/ both). This is not a new fact to discover; the engine

(agreementChain, cloze, listening) that offers two singular cells of the same
verb as options will be REJECTED by the existing guard — this is correct
behavior, not a bug to route around. See §6 design note.

## 4. Module thesis (design authority for this dispatch)

*"You already speak the machine — you just didn't know its name."* Since m3
the learner has produced «j'aime»/«tu aimes» as memorized blobs. m11 reveals
aimer's five singular cells are one predictable shape (stem + silent
ending — all SOUND IDENTICAL, /ɛm/), completes the paradigm (il/elle/on
aime), then TRANSFERS the machine to a brand-new verb (parler) never seen
conjugated — proving generation, not recall. Continues the arc: m9 taught
AUDIBLE agreement, m10 taught SILENT plural, m11 teaches **SILENT
PERSON** — for -er verbs in the singular you hear the SUBJECT, never the
ending (nous/vous are the two audible endings, parked past m11, §3).

Three verbs, three roles:
- **aimer** — 2/5 cells already taught; m11 completes il/elle/on, makes
  the paradigm EXPLICIT (a reveal, not a debut).
- **parler** — wholly new; debuts as bare infinitive, then its full
  singular is taught (mirroring aimer's shape), paired with `français`/
  `anglais` for natural sentences ("je parle français").
- **habiter** — wholly new, reserved ONLY for the L8 checkpoint transfer
  test: debuts as a bare quoted infinitive in a teaching lesson (never
  conjugated there — mirrors existing FR "infinitive-only until its
  module" pins), then L8 demands its first-ever conjugated cell from a
  build bank holding ≥2 singular cells — the ES-precedent-verified SAFE
  shape (§6).

first). Don't add further atoms without checking
`FR_FUNCTION_WORDS`/`FR_PROPER_NAMES`/prior modules — every surface must
