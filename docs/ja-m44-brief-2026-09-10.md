   but its courseAtoms.ts row count was unaffected (`DERIVED_KINDS`-kind atoms are
   never separately registered as courseAtoms.ts rows — only the IR carries them).
2. **`verb-form` is not a legal `CourseAtomKind`.** m43's earlier draft also wrongly
   gave かもしれません its own courseAtoms.ts row with `kind: "verb-form"` — a real

   courseAtoms.ts-row count for m43 is 14** (11 named domain words + ぜったい +
   3 grammar-carrier atoms かもしれない/はず/にちがいない), not the 15 its brief guessed.

## §1 Corrections to the spine's own numbers

The spine's n4-15 vocab list was written without checking the live atom registry.
Verified against `src/features/languages/ja/courseAtoms.ts` (current tree) below.
Three corrections, one of them a hard stop:

### 1a. きこえる / みえる — already taught, not new

Both are already-registered, already-blocked atoms from m24, same lesson:

- `courseAtoms.ts:1441` — `{ id: "mieru", kana: "みえる", ..., fromModule: "m24", introducedByLessonId: "ja-m24-neo-6", blocked: true, ... }`
- `courseAtoms.ts:1442` — `{ id: "kikoeru", kana: "きこえる", ..., fromModule: "m24", introducedByLessonId: "ja-m24-neo-6", blocked: true, ... }`

Use both as **carriers only** (they can appear in example sentences and cloze
distractors as already-known vocabulary) — do NOT re-declare them in m44's
`newAtoms`. This mirrors m42/m43's own §1 correction-table pattern for
already-taught "must" words.

### 1b. かんじ — collides with an existing, heavily-embedded atom; recommend CUT

The spine's "must" word かんじ is intended as 感じ ("feeling, sense," freqRank 2968
in `docs/data/ja-core6k-order.json`). But `courseAtoms.ts:823` already registers
かんじ as **漢字** ("kanji character," `id: "kanji"`, `fromModule: "m38"`,
`introducedByLessonId: "ja-m38-neo-9"`, freqRank 155 — a load-bearing, high-frequency
atom). Both words share the identical kana string かんじ. `atomIndex()`
(`moduleCompiler.ts` ~639-680) resolves kana→atom through
`JA_COURSE_ATOMS_BY_KANA` first, so any m44 reference to かんじ intending "feeling"
would silently resolve to the already-taught "kanji character" atom instead —
wrong gloss, wrong image, wrong meaning, and no compile-time error.

**Recommendation: cut 感じ from m44 entirely.** Two independent reasons converge:
the collision itself (no positional/register fix exists — unlike みたい below, これ
isn't a register-pair morpheme, it's just an accidental homograph with nothing to
disambiguate on), and its low standalone frequency value (rank 2968, near the tail
of the 3000-word list) makes it a weak "must" pick in the first place. This mirrors
m43's own precedent of cutting weak/collision-prone sidequest words rather than
inventing a workaround. If a future module wants 感じ, it would need an explicit
`JA_PRIMARY_ATOM_BY_KANA` ruling (the mechanism used for はやい/とる/etc. at
`courseAtoms.ts:1797-1816`) — not recommended here given the frequency math doesn't
justify the complexity.

### 1c. みたい — flagship collision; hard rule, not a cut

m44's own headline NEW grammar point, みたい (casual simile/comparison suffix,
"looks like / seems like"), collides with an **already-live, heavily-used IR-local
derived atom** of the identical kana string: `みたい` is registered in
`ir/m13.ir.yaml:275` as `{ kana: みたい, romaji: mitai, gloss: "want to watch",
imageable: false, kind: tai-form, derivedFrom: みる, verbClass: ru }` — the たい-form
desiderative of みる ("want to see/watch"). This atom has propagated forward through
every module's `priorAtoms` since m13 (confirmed present, verbatim, in m43's own
compiled `m43.ir.json` → `priorAtoms` array, 961 entries) and is used live across
at least 9 modules (m13, m14, m16, m21, m23, m24, m28, m30, m31 — confirmed via
grep on compiled `.ir.json` files).

This is the same severity/class as m42's いって/いった-for-言う firm ruling: two
distinct meanings claim the identical kana string, and once m44 registers its own
`みたい` in `newAtoms`, `atomIndex()`'s last-wins layering
(`JA_COURSE_ATOMS_BY_KANA` → `priorAtoms` → **own `newAtoms`, last-wins**) means
m44's own compile context will resolve **every** occurrence of みたい — including
any leftover "want to watch" usage inside m44's own review/filler sentences — to
the comparison-particle meaning. There is no positional fix once two atoms claim
the same kana key.

**Hard rule for m44: never use みる+たい ("want to see/watch") anywhere in m44's own
authored content once みたい (comparison) is registered.** This is a one-way door —
the desiderative meaning is permanently shadowed within m44's compile scope from
the moment `newAtoms` declares the comparison みたい. Downstream modules (m45+) are
unaffected in their OWN compile scope (their own `newAtoms`, if any, still resolve
last-wins in their own context) but will inherit m44's みたい (comparison) as the
`priorAtoms` entry going forward — which is correct and intended, since comparison
みたい is by far the higher-frequency, more central meaning of the two, and the
desiderative "want to watch" sense is a minor collocation that can be paraphrased
(み たかった, or avoided) in any later module that needs it.

**No other candidate word collides.** Confirmed CLEAN (no `courseAtoms.ts` row, no
kana-string collision) for: におい, あじ, ようす, こおり, けむり, かがみ, にんぎょう,
そっくり, にる, ちがい (as a bare noun — `ちがいます` at `courseAtoms.ts:1085` is a
different, longer kana string, same root but no map collision), とくちょう, ふんいき,
きざし, のように, のような, よう (bare, aside from unrelated another/day-name words).

- `youda-direct-evidence` — ようだ, conjecture from direct/observable evidence
- `mitai-casual` — みたい as the casual register twin of youda-direct-evidence,
  explicitly including the syntax difference (bare noun vs の+ようだ)
- `you-ni-simile` — のように (adverbial simile: "like X, ...")
- `you-na-simile` — のような (adnominal simile: "an X-like ...")
- `evidential-synthesis` — the four-way SYNTHESIS CARD (そう/appearance ·
  そうだ/hearsay · らしい · ようだ・みたい), assembled once, referencing (not
  re-teaching) `sou-appearance` (m36), `sou-hearsay`/`rashii-hearsay` (m42) by id

**Registration mechanism for ようだ/みたい themselves — UNVERIFIED, author's
judgment call**, same class of decision as m43's own hazu-sub-id split. Given the
みたい collision (§1c), the strong recommendation is: **no bare みたい courseAtoms.ts
row at all** — same treatment m43 gave かも (never given its own atom, decomposed
into か+も) and m42 gave bare そう (kept off the registry, only そうだ/appearance-そう

grammar cards (no dedicated lemma atom), with any fused example-sentence forms

## §6 Explicitly rejected approaches

1. **Giving みたい (comparison) its own atom and also keeping みたい (desiderative)
   as a separate atom under a disambiguating id.** Rejected: `atomIndex()`/
   `JA_COURSE_ATOMS_BY_KANA` key on the raw kana string, not on a disambiguating id —
   two atoms cannot share one kana key with different meanings and both resolve
   correctly at tokenization time (same class of impossibility as m41's つく/つける,
   which had to be dropped outright). One meaning must win; comparison-みたい wins
   on frequency/centrality grounds (§1c).
2. **Keeping 感じ and adding a `JA_PRIMARY_ATOM_BY_KANA` ruling to force it primary
   over 漢字.** Rejected: 漢字 is a high-frequency (rank 155), heavily-embedded
   atom since m38 with real downstream dependents; overriding it in favor of a
   rank-2968 "feeling" word is not justified by frequency value, and a ruling
   entry adds ongoing maintenance surface for a marginal vocab pick. Cut 感じ
   instead (§1b).
3. **Registering ようだ/みたい as fused per-example-sentence atoms (one atom per
   full collocation like かぜをひいたようだ) rather than pure rule cards.**
   Rejected as the default: this is the pattern m43 flagged as UNVERIFIED
   author's-judgment for はず's sub-id split, and multiplying fused atoms per
   sentence would balloon the atom budget past ~20 without adding teaching value
   over a `kind: "rule"` card. Reserved only for a genuinely fixed idiom if one
   turns out to be pedagogically necessary (§3).
4. **Falling back to N3 spine reasoning.** Rejected: the N4 spine is not
   exhausted — m44 is n4-15 of 51 mapped modules, directly following m43 (n4-14)
   with no gap in `docs/spine-n4.md`.
5. **Teaching ように-purpose (the "in order to" sense, n4-19) alongside
   ように-simile here to save a forward-reference.** Rejected: spine explicitly
   flags this as a deliberate forward-flag, not a teaching point, to avoid
   conflating two distinct jobs of the same morpheme in one module (mirrors the
   evidential family's own "sprinkling without reassembly is fragmentation"
   principle the spine cites from N5 m16).
