## 2. Module thesis (design authority for this dispatch)

*"«Je suis venu de Paris» already means «I came FROM Paris» — now «venir
de» means «I just DID something.»"* The learner has known `venir de` since
m16 as a fixed phrase naming where someone came FROM (a place). m20's
teaching claim: that exact same two-word chunk, followed by a bare
infinitive instead of a place, stops talking about origin and starts
talking about TIME — «je viens de parler» (I just spoke), not "I'm coming
from speaking." This is the same slot-generalization move `ne…pas` made
into `jamais`/`rien`/`plus` (m18) and `je vais` made into the near-future
frame (m19), applied a third time to a different piece of course chrome —
and it is deliberately designed as the near-future frame's mirror image:
m19 leans forward from now, m20 reaches back from now, using the same
"chunk + bare infinitive, zero conjugation" shape both times.

Two beats, taught in this order, never mixed silently:
1. **The frame generalizes (L1–L3):** debut by direct contrast against
   the already-known m16 course sentence — quote «je suis venu de Paris»
   (origin, known) beside the new «je viens de parler» (recent action,
   new) so the slot-fill insight is explicit: what follows «de» changed
   from a PLACE to an ACTION, and the whole sentence's job changed with
   it. Consolidate `je viens de` / `tu viens de` fronting each of the
   three available consonant-onset infinitives (`parler` m11, `manger`
   m14, `visiter` m15 — `habiter` explicitly EXCLUDED, §1 d'-elision trap).
2. **The cast widens (L4–L7):** debut `il vient de`/`elle vient de` (§3) —
   the same chunk convention extended to third person, so NPC dialogue can
   report what someone else just finished, not only the learner's own
   recent action. A cross-tense bridge at L6 contrasts the new frame
   against m14's passé composé on the SAME verb (`j'ai mangé` vs `je viens
   de manger` — both describe a completed eating event, but the new frame
   marks it as JUST NOW, a genuine, low-cost tense-register discrimination
   beat as a side effect, mirroring the avoir-vs-être taste m18's L9 folded
   in rather than building a whole module around it).

## 5. Homophone / liaison risks

- **`viens`/`vient` are pronounced IDENTICALLY — [vjɛ̃] for je/tu/il/elle
  alike (both the -s and the -t are silent).** This is a genuine,
  UNVERIFIED spoken-production risk (§6): a learner's ear (and any ASR
  scorer) cannot distinguish subject from the verb sound alone — the
  SUBJECT PRONOUN, not the verb ending, carries all the identifying
  information, unlike m19's `va`/`vais`/`vas` (which the m19 brief
  confirmed are each phonetically distinct). No `homophoneKey` mechanism
  applies here (that machinery guards ONE word's own gendered/derived
  forms sharing a sound, not five different phrase atoms sharing an
  internal verb sound) — flagged as a design constraint instead (§6, §7):
  any `speaking`/`agreementChain` audio-graded step must never ask the
  learner to produce the bare verb in isolation as proof of subject;
  always require the full subject+verb chunk.
- **`vient`/`viens` [vjɛ̃] is a near-minimal pair against `bien` [bjɛ̃]
  (m2, already taught)** — same onset-consonant-swap risk class
  `frSpeechNegation.test.ts` already measured for `rien`/`bien` (0.75–0.923
  passing scores on a DIFFERENT word pair, not this one). UNVERIFIED for
  this specific pair — no probe has been run — flagged for the coordinator
  checklist (§9) rather than assumed safe by analogy.
- **No new elision landmine inside this module's own scope** — `de` before
  the three IN-SCOPE infinitives (`parler`, `manger`, `visiter`, all
  consonant-onset) never elides; the one vowel/mute-h infinitive that
  WOULD force it (`habiter`) is explicitly excluded (§1, §3) specifically
  to avoid touching the still-deferred d'-elision rule. This exclusion is
  the module's single most load-bearing scope decision — a content author
  substituting `habiter` back in by habit (it was in m19's set) would
  silently reopen a banned construction.
- **No new `liaisonListen` item** — consistent with m12–m19 precedent;
  nothing in this module's core sentences forces one.
