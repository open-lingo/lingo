## 2. Module thesis (design authority for this dispatch)

*"«Ne… pas» was never the only way to say no — it was the first member of
a family."* The learner has used `ne…pas` as a fixed, memorized frame since
m13 without ever being told it's a **slot**, not a phrase. m18's whole
teaching claim: the position between `ne` and the verb-or-participle is a
socket that accepts different fillers, each with a distinct meaning —
`pas` (not), `jamais` (never), `rien` (nothing), `plus` (no longer) — and
every sentence the learner already knows how to negate can be renegated
with any of them, for free, with zero new sentence structure.

Three fillers, taught in this order, never mixed silently:
1. **`jamais` — the frame generalizes (L1–L3):** debut by direct contrast
   against an already-known `ne…pas` course sentence (m13's own «je ne
   sais pas», quoted beside the new «je ne sais jamais») so the slot-fill
   insight is explicit, not inferred. Consolidation across the verb cast
   (savoir m13, aimer/parler/habiter m11, avoir m7, être m2).
2. **`rien` — the frame takes an object slot too (L4–L6):** debut «je n'ai
   rien», «je ne mange rien» — same position `pas` occupies, this time as
   a negative pronoun object rather than an adverb; explicit contrast
   against `jamais` (never happens vs. nothing exists) so the two aren't
   read as interchangeable "generic negative" words.
3. **`plus` — the collision zone, taught as its own bridge (L7–L9):** debut
   «je ne fume plus» / «il n'habite plus à Paris» (no longer). Central info
   card (the module's highest-risk teaching point, mirroring m16's «je
   suis» bridge and m17's «et» bridge): quote «moi non plus» (m13, already
   known — `plus` fossilized inside a fixed idiom) beside the NEW «ne…
   plus» (a live, productive frame slot) so the learner sees these as two
   different jobs for the same spelling, not a contradiction. A second,
   smaller contrast against `encore` (m6, "more/another") closes the
   "more" confusion. L7–L9 also compose the frame through passé composé
   with BOTH auxiliaries already taught («je n'ai jamais mangé» / «elle
   n'est jamais allée» / «il n'a rien dit»[if `dire` is in scope — verify,
   likely cut, see §3] / «je n'ai plus faim»[needs `faim`, likely cut] —
   see §3 for what actually composes from EXISTING atoms only), a genuine,
   low-cost taste of avoir-vs-être discrimination as a side effect, without
   spending a whole module on it (m16's own separately-named "être-vs-avoir
   discrimination" idea, folded in as recall texture here rather than
   built as this module's spine — §7).

## 5. Homophone / liaison risks

- **No `homophoneKey` pairs required.** `jamais` [ʒamɛ], `rien` [ʁjɛ̃],
  `plus` [ply]/[plys] (see below), `pas` [pa] are all phonetically distinct
  from each other and from every existing atom — verified by inspection,
  no minimal pairs. Lower audio-risk than m14/m15/m16's participle
  modules, comparable to m17.
- **«plus» inside «moi non plus» vs «plus» as the new `ne…plus` filler is
  a COMPREHENSION collision, not a phonetic one** (§1, §2.3) — same class
  as m16's «je suis» copula/auxiliary collision: the two uses don't sound
  different from each other in a way that confuses grading, they mean two
  different things from the same spelling and need an explicit bridge
  card (L7), not a silent reuse. No gate catches this; it's why the L7
  card is treated as load-bearing (§7).
- **The pronunciation of negative «plus» is context-sensitive and worth
  flagging for TTS verification (§6):** standard French drops the final -s
  sound in the NEGATIVE «ne…plus» ([ply]) regardless of what follows,
  while the (untaught, §1) comparative/additive «plus» can be [plys] or
  liaise as [plyz] before a vowel. This module's «plus» is ALWAYS the
  negative sense, so every course sentence using it should render as
  [ply] — UNVERIFIED whether edge-tts's French voice gets this right by
  default; confirm with a real generated clip before treating the deck as
  correct, not assumed from the array-based spoken-safety check alone
  (that check only covers ASR/loose-match, not TTS pronunciation
  correctness).
- **`encore` (more/another, m6) vs `plus` (no more, m18) near-opposite
  collision** (§1) — addressed by the L7 secondary contrast note, not a
  gate-catchable risk (both are legitimate, unrelated atoms; the risk is
  purely a learner-comprehension one).
- **No new `liaisonListen` item** — consistent with m12/m14/m15/m16/m17
  precedent; nothing in this module's core sentences forces one (jamais/
  rien/plus are all consonant-final or nasal-final in a way that doesn't
  create a new liaison teaching moment).
