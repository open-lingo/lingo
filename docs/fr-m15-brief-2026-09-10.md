## 2. Module thesis (design authority for this dispatch)

*"You've been all over this town — now show someone around it."* m15 is not
a new grammar module; it is the course's first **consolidation** module —
every new piece is a small unlock that lets the learner narrate a tour of
the town they've owned since m4, in the past tense they earned in m14, with
one new phonological class (h aspiré) and one new locative phrase («en
ville»). By design this module is **reuse-heavy, atom-light** — "coverage,
not volume": the win condition is a learner who can compose "hier, on a
visité la halle et l'hôtel, mais on n'a pas visité le musée" from pieces
spanning six prior modules, not a learner who memorized new vocabulary.

Two small unlocks, taught in this order, never mixed silently:
1. **«En ville» + h aspiré (L1–L3):** the locative phrase that m5 banned
   becomes legal; the first aspiré atom («halle») debuts against the
   already-known mute-h anchor («hôtel»), giving the learner their first
   real le/la/l' contrast that ISN'T vowel-vs-consonant at the letter level.
2. **The visit itself (L4–L10):** «visite» (noun) / «visiter» (bare
   infinitive) / «visité» (participle) extend m14's avoir+participle machine
   to a second verb, proving it generalizes; the rest of the module is
   built entirely from recall — places (m4), aller (m5), food (m6),
   family/possessives (m7), days (m8), adjectives (m9), the -er machine
   (m11), negation (m13), and m14's full time/aspect kit — composed into a
   tour narrative.

## 4. Lesson plan (10 lessons, checkpoint L8, mastery L10 ends on
dialogue_sim)

1. **L1 «En ville»** — debut `en ville`; "on va en ville" (recall m5 «on
   va»); wide recall pass over every m4 place noun (gare, musée, école,
   hôtel, parc, plage, restaurant, café) inside the new phrase ("qu'est-ce
   qu'il y a en ville ?" reusing m4's «il y a»). No new grammar — this
   lesson is the deferred-registry unlock and nothing else.
2. **L2 «La visite commence»** — debut `visite` (noun), `visiter` (bare
   infinitive, brief info card mirroring m14's manger→mangé explanation:
   "visiter → visité, the same -é you already know"), `visité`. Build
   "j'ai visité le musée" / "j'ai visité la gare" reusing m14's avoir chunk
   + m4 places. Recall «j'ai» provenance (m7→m14).
3. **L3 «Une halle en ville»** — debut `halle`; explicit ≤3-line contrast
   quoting both course sentences: «la halle» (h aspiré — no squeeze) vs
   «l'hôtel» (mute h — squeezes) — the FIRST le/la/l' contrast that isn't
   plain vowel-vs-consonant. "on a visité la halle" recall from L2's
   machine. Light interleave: one m6 food-word recall (a halle sells food).
4. **L4 consolidation, no new atoms** — discrimination cloze/lc alternating
   visité/mangé (m14) across places and foods; recall m9 adjectives
   describing the visit ("c'est grand", "la halle est grande" — agreement
   recall); interleave break from the visit frame entirely.
5. **L5 «On a visité... avec»** — no new atoms; recall m7 possessives/family
   ("j'ai visité le musée avec mon frère", "avec ma sœur"); debut the
   negated form "je n'ai pas visité le musée" / "on n'a pas visité la
   plage" (m13's «ne…pas» machine + m14's frozen «je n'ai pas» chunk,
   composed — no new atom, confirm this composes cleanly, §7.6).
6. **L6 «Hier, on a visité...»** — no new atoms; full recall of m14's
   time/aspect kit («hier», «hier soir», «ce matin», «déjà», «pas encore»)
   riding the visiter/visité machine: "hier, j'ai déjà visité la gare",
   "je n'ai pas encore visité l'hôtel". Cross-module day recall (m8
   aujourd'hui vs hier).
7. **L7 «Il/elle a visité»** — no new atoms; recall m14's bare `a` (3rd
   person) + m2 pronouns: "il a visité", "elle a visité"; negation
   "il n'a pas visité" / "elle n'a pas visité" composes for FREE from the
   bare `a` atom's elision derivation (m14 §7.2's finding, reused here —
   confirm it still resolves with «visité» in the slot, not just
   «mangé»). **Ne-drop is legal in any `dialogue_sim` NPC line/option from
   here on** — this is a COURSE-ORDER law unlocked at m13 L7, not a
   per-module reset (m14 §7.7's "from here on" reading, not "L7 of m15
   specifically"); written answer positions stay full form throughout.
8. **L8 checkpoint** — zero-new, all-graded; en ville, halle/hôtel elision,
   visite/visiter/visité across je/tu/il/elle, all m14 time words,
   negation; transfer test: an untaught-combination prompt (e.g. "say you
   haven't visited the market hall yet") demanding composition, mirrors
   m12/m14's checkpoint shape.
9. **L9 integration** — ≥10 steps, no info cards, one big `dialogue_sim`
   tail: a learner-as-guide (or NPC-as-guide) narrating a town tour —
   la halle, l'hôtel, le musée, hier/déjà/pas encore, negation, all
   composed; ne-drop legal in NPC lines/options (§4 L7 note); 2–3
   cross-module recalls reaching back further than m14 (m6 food, m9
   adjectives, or m1 café).
10. **L10 mastery** — all-graded, every m15 atom present, ends on
    `dialogue_sim`: full recap "la visite" conversation. Soft promise for

## 5. Homophone / liaison risks

- **halle / hôtel are NOT homophones** ([al] vs [otɛl]) — unlike m14's
  a/à or manger/mangé, this contrast carries **no listening risk**. The
  risk here is entirely orthographic/grammatical: never writing «l'halle»
  or «l'halle est là-bas». Because `withArticle()` derives the article from
  `elidesBefore()` (`grammarHelpers.ts:944`, `courseAtoms.ts:119-121`), a
  correctly-flagged `halle` atom (`consonantOnset: true`) makes this
  architecturally hard to get wrong at render time — same mechanism m14
  §7.2 used to auto-derive «n'a». **Not independently executed against the
  actual `halle` atom in this worktree — flag as unverified, confirm in
  `m15.test.ts` before shipping L3/L8** (identical caveat shape to m14's
  own §7.2 note).
- **visiter / visité — same -er/-é homophone class as m14's manger/mangé**
  ([vizite] both). Apply the identical law m14 established: never
  co-present `visiter` and `visité` as options in any audio-bearing or
  listening step; the distinction is written-only (build/cloze/translate-
  adjacent MCQ).
- **«halle» plurals/liaison are OUT OF SCOPE for m15** — «les halles» (no
  liaison, aspiré) vs «les hôtels» (liaison, mute h) would be the textbook
  minimal-pair demonstration, but it requires a plural noun and a
  liaison-bearing context. m15 stays **singular-only** for `halle`
  (mirrors m11's singular-only verb precedent) and does NOT add a new
  `liaisonListen` item — same recommendation m12 §5 and m14 §5 both made
  ("nothing in this module's core sentences forces one"); whether the
  `liaisonListen` factory could even express an h-aspiré no-liaison
  contrast was not traced in this brief (unverified, and deliberately
  sidestepped by staying singular).
- **«en» — locative only, never partitive.** The new `en ville` phrase
  atom must not be read by any learner-facing material as licensing the
  partitive pronoun «en» (e.g. «j'en veux») — that sense is untaught and
  stays deferred (playbook §6, partitives). Keep every `en` occurrence
  inside the frozen `en ville` phrase; don't let «en» float free as a
  reusable token in build banks.

## 7. Decisions inferred (recorded, not parked)

1. **Deferred number ranges (teens 11–19, 70–99) are NOT part of m15,
   rejected outright.** They have no thematic home in a town-tour
   consolidation module — m12's own deferral language calls them "their
   own beat" (`fr-m12-brief`, §7.2: "deferred... to a NAMED slot"), and
   playbook §8's m15 description never mentions numbers. Cramming them in
   here would be volume, not coverage. They stay in the deferred registry
   for a future numbers-specific module.
2. **Être-auxiliary passé composé (je suis allé) is REJECTED for m15**,
   despite m14 §7.1 flagging m15's "grand consolidation" as a candidate
   home. Reasoning, asking what the project's goal actually needs
   (deduction-first, one machine at a time, never re-litigated per the
   escalation-filter norm — but this is a genuine judgment call m14
   explicitly left open, so it gets one): m15 already introduces TWO new
   subsystems this module (h aspiré as a phonological/orthographic class;
   the avoir+participle machine's first generalization to a second verb)
   plus the «en ville» unlock. Être-auxiliary would add a THIRD and much
   heavier one — a new auxiliary system AND the course's first
   audible-silent gender-agreement pair (allé/allée, true homophones,
   needing `homophoneKey` machinery never exercised anywhere in the FR
   course) — in the same module that is also asking the learner to
   internalize an unrelated phonological class (h aspiré). That violates
   "one machine at a time," the discipline m11, m13, and m14 all followed
   and m14 §7.1 itself invoked to justify deferring être in the first
   place. **Recommendation for whoever next edits the playbook §8 arc:**
   give être-auxiliary its own dedicated module (candidate m16), not a
   second consolidation slot — m15's own mastery tease (§4 L10) reflects
   this.
3. **«visiter»/«visité» chosen over «voir»/«vu»** as the vehicle for
   proving the passé-composé machine generalizes. «voir» (to see) reads
   more naturally for "a tour" in English, but its past participle «vu» is
   IRREGULAR (not the -er→-é pattern) — teaching it here would open a
   second new machine (irregular participles) inside a module already
   carrying h aspiré, which is the same "one machine at a time" violation
   as decision 2. «visiter» is fully regular, pays off the module's own
   name, and reuses m4's place nouns as objects with zero new noun atoms.
4. **No futur proche («je vais visiter», aller + infinitive).** Verified
   `je vais`/`tu vas`/`on va` (m5) are attested ONLY before a place (à +
   noun) anywhere in m1–m13 on disk — never before a bare infinitive.
   Aller+infinitive is a genuinely separate construction (immediate
   future) from the movement chunk the learner owns; adding it here would
   be a fourth new grammar point in one module. Stays out; flagged as a
   future deferred-registry candidate for whoever authors m16+.
5. **«halle» chosen over «héros»** (the mechanism's own canonical
   docstring example, `grammarHelpers.ts:944`) for the h-aspiré debut.
   «héros» has no natural home in a plain town tour without inventing a
   statue/monument beat; «halle» (market hall) is a real, ordinary French
   town building, ties back to m6's food vocabulary, and needs no
   supporting noun of its own.
6. **The optional «content»/«contente» stretch atom is genuinely optional**
   — cut first if the lesson count is tight, same status m13 gave «pas
   encore» and m14 gave «aimé»/«parlé». It adds nothing the module's core
   claim needs; it only gives L4's interleave break slightly richer
   material.
7. **The negated visiter chain (L5, L7) was traced, not executed** — this
   brief confirms by reading `courseAtoms.ts`/`grammarHelpers.ts` that
   m13's ne…pas machine and m14's frozen «je n'ai pas»/bare-`a` elision
   should compose with «visité» exactly as they did with «mangé» (nothing
   in the mechanism is verb-specific), but did not run the actual gates
   against drafted L5/L7 sentences. Flag as unverified, confirm in
   `m15.test.ts`.
