# Jouzu Juls + Cure Dolly — Pedagogy Recon & Backing Psychology

**Date:** 2026-07-02
**Purpose:** Make two YouTube educators' teaching approaches available as *backing
psychology* for the whole Lingo Japanese course. PRIMARY = **Jouzu Juls** (how he
teaches and understands Japanese). SECONDARY = **Cure Dolly / Organic Japanese**.
This is a recon doc + the extraction pipeline that feeds it. We adopt *distilled
principles in our own words/examples* — never verbatim scripts (see §E, ethics).

**Headline finding:** these are **one lineage, not two philosophies.** Jouzu Juls
is an explicit Cure Dolly disciple — he uses her exact structural model (the
single-function が, the hidden zero-が `Øが`, self-move/other-move verbs, `分かる` =
"makes sense" not "I understand") and layers a modern **immersion + Anki + sentence-
mining toolchain** on top. Treat Cure Dolly as the *grammar operating system* and
Jouzu Juls as the *learner-facing method + delivery* built on it. Where they differ
it's emphasis, not doctrine.

## A. Who each is + philosophy distilled

### Jouzu Juls (PRIMARY)
- **Channel:** "Jouzu Juls (上手 ジューズ)" — <https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCzovHrDif7q9QB4XY1dS98Q>
  (handle **@JouzuJuls**). Site **jouzujuls.com**, Patreon (jouzujuls), Medium
  (@jouzujuls), Discord "Jouzu Gumi". Goes by "Cheif" on camera. ~85 videos.
- **Origin story he sells the method with:** started 2020-08-30, zero to
  watching anime/VTubers without subtitles in ~6 months via immersion. Uses his
  own result as the argument for the method.

**Philosophy — 5 concrete, testable principles:**
1. **Input before output (Krashen's Comprehensible Input).** Language is *acquired*
   by understanding messages, not built by drilling output. He explicitly rejects
   "more output / just speak" and calls traditional L2 education "ineffective with a
   shockingly high failure rate." Listening/reading are optimized first; speaking
   comes later and largely takes care of itself.
2. **Grammar is a small logical core, not a list of ~200 points.** Signature move:
   *"almost all these grammar points don't exist and don't need to be studied
   separately"* — e.g. ている / ていく / てくる / ては / ても are just **て-form (a
   linker) + a normal verb** (`いる` = "exist/be in a state of"). Learn the core
   piece; the "60% of grammar" that looks like separate points falls out of it.
   (Video: *60% of Japanese Grammar follows This Pattern*.)
3. **One-function particles; reject the "particle sometimes means X" cop-out.**
   `が` marks the subject — one function, always. `を` marks the affected object.
   He attacks the textbook habit of saying は/が/を "sometimes mark subject,
   sometimes object, sometimes topic" as the *cause* of learner confusion.
   (Video: *The ULTIMATE Guide to Japanese Particles*.)
4. **Translate the structure, not the dictionary gloss.** `分かる` isn't "to
   understand" — it's an intransitive (自動詞) "does understandable / makes sense."
   So `本が分かる` = "the book makes sense," NOT "I understand the book" (there's no
   hidden 私 object because 自動詞 can't take を). He teaches *self-move (自動詞) vs
   other-move (他動詞)* + the **hidden zero-が (`Øが`)** as the fix. This is Cure
   Dolly's model verbatim in framing, with his own examples. (Video: *Japanese
   Transitivity Pairs are NOT What You Think*.)
5. **Immersion is a built system + a tooling stack, not "move to Japan."** His
   5-step framework: (1) learn how acquisition works, (2) kana, (3) Anki SRS on
   high-frequency vocab *with example sentences*, (4) look up grammar only when it
   blocks comprehension (just-in-time, not front-loaded), (5) **sentence-mine**
   vocab from real content. Heavy practical toolchain: Anki, Migaku, Yomitan,
   sentence-mining, furigana add-ons.

**Tone:** casual, meme-y, "Cheif"-to-the-viewer, myth-busting ("this will break your
Japanese," "here's what these sources don't tell you"), confident and anti-textbook.

### Cure Dolly — "Organic Japanese" (SECONDARY)
- **Channel:** "Organic Japanese with Cure Dolly" —
  <https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCkdmU8hGK4Fg3LghTVtKltQ> (~204 videos).
  Site **learnjapaneseonline.info**. Book **"Unlocking Japanese."** Presented as an
  android doll avatar + synthesized voice.
- **Status:** creator **deceased ~2021**. Content is **frozen** (no new material, no
  corrections, no takedown authority exercised); the community maintains archives
  and transcript sites. Implication: safe to *study* long-term, but treat as a
  fixed historical corpus — cite the community archives, don't expect updates.

**Philosophy — 5 concrete, testable principles:**
1. **The train / engine model — every sentence has exactly two core elements.**
   A "carriage" (A) + an "engine" (B); the engine is always a verb or adjective/
   copula and always comes at the end of the logical clause. "A is B" or "A does B"
   are the only two sentence types in any language. Understand the core and every
   complex sentence decomposes. (Lesson 1.)
2. **が is the linchpin of EVERY sentence — and it's often invisible.** Every clause
   revolves around が marking the subject; when you can't see it, the **zero-が** is
   still there doing the same job. Rejecting the textbook's failure to teach this is
   her stated reason learners get confused. (This is the seed of Jouzu's #3/#4.)
3. **は is a topic-flag, NOT a subject/grammatical marker.** It sits *outside* the
   core engine—carriage logic and flags "as for X"; conflating は and が is a primary
   textbook error she attacks.
4. **Reject "conjugation"; verbs take helper-word attachments.** Japanese doesn't
   conjugate like European languages — you attach helpers (て-form links, いる, the
   だ/です copula as `de aru`, etc.). Endings are logical, gluable pieces, not an
   irregular paradigm to memorize. (Directly mirrored by Jouzu's #2.)
5. **Modification structure explains 80–90% of complex sentences; two word-order
   laws.** (i) the engine comes last in its clause; (ii) *anything that modifies
   anything comes before it.* A whole clause can modify a noun ("putting the
   preceding sentence into a `koto` box"), then be handled as a simple noun. This +
   compounding are the *only* two ways to grow a sentence. (Video: *Modification
   Structure*.)

**Tone:** calm, precise, "schools never taught you this," pure/logical-language
framing ("Japanese with no harmful additives"), systematic.

## B. How they map onto Lingo's pillars (and where they conflict)

Lingo's stated pillars (CLAUDE.md + memory): **no babying / treat 10+ as capable
adults**; **comprehensibility gate** — machine-enforced "would they know this word
yet?" (every content word decomposes into already-introduced atoms); **concept
separation** — trainers teach the smallest pieces, lessons reinforce combinations;
statically-authored curriculum with intro-before-review conformance.

**Strong alignment:**
- **Concept separation ⇄ "grammar points don't exist separately."** Jouzu's "learn
  て-form once, ている/ていく/… fall out" and Cure Dolly's "attach helpers, don't
  conjugate" ARE Lingo's trainer/lesson split stated pedagogically: teach the
  smallest piece (て-form, いる, が) in a trainer; let lessons reinforce the
  *combinations* (ている, compound clauses). The **Conjugation Trainer** (per-type
  drills for te/ta/nai/masu-neg/tai/i-adj) is a near-perfect instantiation of "verbs
  take helper attachments, drill the formation, don't memorize a paradigm."
- **Comprehensibility gate ⇄ input-before-output / just-in-time grammar.** Jouzu's
  "only look up grammar when it blocks comprehension" and Krashen's *i+1* are the
  human-facing version of Lingo's atom-decomposition gate. Both say: never put a
  piece in front of a learner before its prerequisites exist.
- **No babying ⇄ both educators' respect for the learner.** Both refuse the
  "Japanese is hard, here are 200 exceptions" framing and instead hand the learner a
  clean logical model. That's exactly "treat users as capable adults, no fake
  gamification." Cure Dolly's "this is the best way to *start*" backs the anti-
  dumbing-down stance.
- **The single-function が model** is authorable as a hard invariant: mark subject
  with が everywhere, surface the zero-が explicitly, and never teach は as a subject
  marker. This could become a lesson-authoring rule the agents test against.

**Where they'd CONFLICT with current course choices:**
1. **Input-primacy vs. Lingo's structured lesson/SRS scaffolding.** Pure Jouzu/AJATT
   says *immersion + mining is the curriculum* and explicit exercises are minimized.
   Lingo is a **statically-authored, SRS-scheduled, exercise-driven** course. We
   borrow their *models and sequencing logic*, not their "throw away the textbook,
   just immerse" stance. Don't let "input is everything" argue against Lingo's
   authored drills — the gate + trainers are our comprehensible-input delivery.
2. **"Grammar points don't exist" vs. Lingo's ~22 discrete grammar points + Track B
   SRS.** Lingo *does* enumerate grammar points and schedule them. Reconcile by
   framing points as **compositions of atoms** (which the comprehensibility gate
   already enforces) rather than atomic facts — matches "learn the core piece."
   The 9 conjugation-formation points already living in the trainer (not as flip
   cards) is exactly this move; the remaining 13 te-compound/aux points are where a
   Jouzu-style "it's just て + X" decomposition would most improve the course.
3. **Romaji.** Both are hardcore kana-first; Jouzu step 2 is "master kana" before
   anything. Lingo's romaji fade (hira@M10 / kata@M17) is *more* permissive than
   either would endorse. Not a bug — Lingo serves a broader/younger audience — but
   note the philosophical tension if anyone argues for extending romaji.
4. **Translation glosses.** Jouzu/Dolly militantly reject "natural English" glosses
   (`分かる` ≠ "understand"). Lingo vocab cards lean on emoji + English meaning. Any
   place we gloss a 自動詞 as a transitive English verb is exactly what they'd flag —
   a concrete audit lens for card meanings.
5. **Speaking/production.** Their "output is overrated early" clashes with Lingo's
   production-practice roadmap (flick keyboard, AI scenarios). Reconcile on timing:
   production *after* substantial comprehensible input, not instead of it.

## D. Extraction pipeline — status, sample, location

**Script:** `scripts/scrape_yt_transcripts.py` (Python; header documents usage).
**Dependency:** `yt-dlp` (installed via `pip install --user`, v2026.06.09).

What it does: given a channel/playlist URL + `--slug`, lists videos (id, title,
duration, upload_date), writes `research/<slug>/index.json`, and downloads subtitles
as **clean plain text** — strips WEBVTT headers, timestamps, `<c>`/inline tags,
cue indices; collapses the rolling-caption prefix-duplication of auto captions;
light sentence-per-line wrapping; preserves Japanese. **Prefers manual (human)
subtitles**, falls back to auto captions. Polite `--sleep` (default 3s), resumable
(skips existing files), `--only`/`--limit` for sampling, `--list-only` to catalog
without downloading.

```bash
# Sample (what was run):
python3 scripts/scrape_yt_transcripts.py \
  --url "https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCzovHrDif7q9QB4XY1dS98Q/videos" \
  --slug jouzu-juls --only i9yqJggtYzo,a5HHq0HZbkM,isnR2kGolgk
python3 scripts/scrape_yt_transcripts.py \
  --url "https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCkdmU8hGK4Fg3LghTVtKltQ/videos" \
  --slug cure-dolly --only pSvH9vH60Ig,HmU2l0pM2gk
```

**Validation result — quality is excellent.** Both channels ship **manual, hand-
written English subtitles** (not just auto captions), so cleaned output is near-
pristine prose with zero duplicate lines and Japanese intact. Sampled:
- `research/jouzu-juls/` — particles (~2.9k words), transitivity (~1.8k), 60%-grammar
  (~2.5k). `index.json`.
- `research/cure-dolly/` — Lesson 1 core sentence (~1.4k), Modification Structure
  (~1.8k). `index.json`.

**Tradeoff — YouTube subs vs. the kellenok transcript archive:** for Cure Dolly the
YT manual subs are already clean, so the pipeline is sufficient. The
kellenok/onlyduyy archives add value where they (a) provide the *whole* ~90-lesson
course in one place with a TOC, (b) survive if a video is delisted, and (c) come
pre-corrected by fans. For a full Cure Dolly pull, scraping the archive HTML (stable
`N-slug.html` URLs) is a reasonable alternative/supplement to per-video subs — but
attribute nunko/dinuz/Kellen. A full scrape is the owner's call (see §F).

**Note:** `research/` holds working transcripts for internal study only — it should
be gitignored / never bundled into the app (see §E).

## E. Copyright & ethics

**Safe (what we're doing):**
- **Studying** transcripts to understand and *distill* teaching approaches.
- **Adopting explanatory models** (the が-linchpin, zero-が, self/other-move, train/
  engine, modification structure, "helpers not conjugation") — these are *ideas /
  methods*, not copyrightable expression. We re-express them **in our own words with
  our own examples**.
- Citing the creators as inspiration in internal docs.

**Not safe (do NOT):**
- Copy scripts, subtitle text, or the *Unlocking Japanese* book text verbatim into
  the course or any shipped asset.
- Re-host the transcript `.txt` files or the community archives inside the app or a
  public repo.
- Reproduce their exact example sentences 1:1 as course content — paraphrase or use
  fresh examples that pass Lingo's comprehensibility gate.

**Housekeeping:** keep `research/` out of git and out of the build. Cure Dolly is
deceased and can't consent — lean extra-conservative: study freely, ship nothing
verbatim, credit the fan transcribers if their text is ever quoted internally.
