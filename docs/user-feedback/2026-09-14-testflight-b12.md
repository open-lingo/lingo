## §4 — #69 build-tile sizing: Duolingo reference (from memory, UNVERIFIED — no web fetch performed)

Spencer asked to check recent Duolingo screenshots for how the "build what you hear" / word-bank step is formatted. No web research was done (out of scope for this pass); the following is a starting spec from general knowledge of Duolingo's tile conventions, to be verified against an actual current screenshot before implementing:

- Tile height: roughly 44–48 pt, with ~8 pt gaps between tiles (both bank and placed row).
- The target/answer area grows to fit content rather than reserving a large fixed minimum — it does not force a scroll before any tile is placed (directly relevant to #75 too).
- The audio play button for a "listen and build" prompt sits as a small, single-line control at the left edge of the instruction line, not as a large stand-alone circle above the tile area — this is the most likely target for the "build what you hear takes up too much space" complaint, since Lingo's current play button (screenshot #69) is a full-width oversized circle above the tiles.

Flag this whole section as unverified: confirm against real Duolingo screenshots before locking tile-size numbers.

## §5 — #80 FSRS test-out seeding: full quote + rule table

**Spencer's full quote:** "Also, we want to make it so when people test out modules, their fsrs state gets coded on an exponential scale or something if we can, or maybe a linear function at 5 days per module I.e if you test out of module 30 and all below, then module one gets 120 days fsrs? And then maybe for anything over 90 days we mark the word as "known" and they don't see it anymore or we exponentially do this somehow so they see it almost never and anything over 60 days or something gets a little further, that make sense? I don't want people reviewing words they already know if they don't have to"

**Translation into a concrete rule.** Spencer's own numeric anchor is "5 days per module of distance," with the earliest (most-practiced) modules getting the longest interval. Module distance = (module the learner tested out to) − (module the atom was introduced in).

| Module distance | FSRS interval (days) | Learner-facing state |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (atom from the just-tested module) | 5 | due (normal review) |
| 1–5 | 5 × distance (5–25) | due, spaced out |
| 6–11 | 30–55 | due, spaced out |
| 12–17 | 60–85 | due, rarely surfaced |
| ≥ 18 (≈ module 30 testing out to module 1, distance 29 → Spencer's own "120 days" example, though 29×5=145; his example undershoots the formula — flag this gap to him) | 90+ | "known" — suppressed from review per his ≥90-day rule |

Two things to confirm with Spencer before implementing (both genuine judgment calls, not mechanical bugs):
1. His own worked example (module 30 → module 1, "120 days") doesn't match his stated formula (5 days/module × 29 modules = 145 days) — pick one as the source of truth.
2. Whether "known" (≥90 days) is a hard suppression (card never resurfaces) or just a very long FSRS interval that could still come due eventually — his text hedges between both ("they don't see it anymore" vs. "anything over 60 days gets a little further").
