## 2. Module thesis (design authority for this dispatch)

*"«Je vais» already means «I'm going» — now it means «I'm going to…» too."*
The learner has used `je vais`/`tu vas`/`on va` since m5 as fixed, whole
phrases meaning literal motion toward a place («je vais au cinéma»). m19's
teaching claim: that exact same chunk, unchanged, can front a bare
infinitive instead of a destination, and the sentence becomes about the
FUTURE, not about MOTION — «je vais parler» (I'm going to speak), not "I'm
going [to] speak" as a place. This is a slot generalization, structurally
identical to m18's `ne…pas` → `ne…jamais/rien/plus` move: the chunk already
known does a second job, at the cost of an explicit bridge card, not a new
grammatical machine.

Two beats, taught in this order, never mixed silently:
1. **The frame generalizes (L1–L3):** debut by direct contrast against an
   already-known m5 course sentence — quote «je vais au cinéma» (motion,
   known) beside the new «je vais parler» (future action, new) so the
   slot-fill insight is explicit. Consolidate across `je vais`/`tu vas`/
   `on va` (all known since m5) fronting each of the four available
   infinitives (`parler` m11, `habiter` m11, `manger` m14, `visiter` m15).
2. **The cast widens (L4–L7):** debut `il va`/`elle va` (§3) — the same
   chunk convention m5 used, extended to third person so NPC dialogue can
   describe future plans, not just the learner's own. A second, smaller
   bridge note for «on va» specifically: it already carries BOTH an
   invitation sense («On va au cinéma ?», m5) and now a plain future-plan
   sense («on va manger») — same collision class as `je vais`, flagged
   once more where it appears.

| surface | meaningEn | kind | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `il va` | he's going / he's going to | phrase | fromModule: "m19"; mirrors `m5.ts`'s own `je vais`/`tu vas`/`on va` convention exactly (whole chunk, not conjugated); widens the future-frame to third-person masculine cast (Thomas, Hugo, Lucas, Paul, Louis) |

`aller` itself as the fronted infinitive (technically tokenizes, "je vais
aller" reads as stilted/redundant in natural French — exclude by content

## 4. Lesson plan (10 lessons, checkpoint L8, mastery L10 ends on
dialogue_sim)

1. **L1 «Je vais parler»** — the bridge info card (§2.1): quote m5's «je
   vais au cinéma» beside the new «je vais parler», ≤3 lines, explicit
   "same words, new job — no destination, no comma, straight into the
   infinitive." Debut the frame with `parler` (already known, consonant
   onset, simplest case). Build/cloze across `je vais`/`tu vas`. Spoken
   step: UNVERIFIED (§6) — recommend written-first (build/cloze/word_map)
   for L1 pending a real spoken-safety check, same discipline the m18
   brief used for its own first `plus`-bearing lesson.
2. **L2 «Tu vas habiter où ?»** — consolidate `tu vas` + `habiter`
   (vowel/mute-h onset — a deliberate second data point that «vais»/
   «vas»/«va» never elide before a following infinitive, contrasted
   implicitly against m4's article-elision rule); recall m7's family/home
   vocabulary if still live, or keep to already-known place nouns.
3. **L3 consolidation, no new atoms** — discrimination cloze/lc: motion
   sense («je vais au parc») vs. future sense («je vais manger») across
   mixed sentences, learner picks by meaning, not surface pattern (m6's
   `manger`-adjacent food nouns if available, else keep to `manger` bare);
   interleave break (one m8 day-of-week recall, unrelated to the frame).
4. **L4 «Il va visiter»** — debut `il va`; explicit note (not a full
   bridge card, a short one-liner) that it's the exact same chunk pattern
   as `je vais`/`tu vas`/`on va`, just a new subject. Build/cloze across
   `il va` + `visiter`/`parler`; recall m9's adjectives on the same NPC
   line for cast continuity.
5. **L5 «Elle va manger»** — debut `elle va`; consolidation across the
   full now-five-strong chunk set (`je vais`/`tu vas`/`il va`/`elle va`/
   `on va`) × the four infinitives; interleave break (one m12 price-frame
   recall, unrelated to the future frame).
6. **L6 «On va manger ?»** — the second bridge card (§2.2): quote m5's
   «On va au cinéma ?» (invitation, known) beside the new «on va manger»
   (plain future-plan statement, new) — ≤3 lines, same "same chunk, new
   job" discipline as L1's card, this time flagging that `on va` alone,
   without a rising "?", reads as future-plan rather than invitation;
   full discrimination drill across all five subject chunks; recall m14's
   passé composé `j'ai mangé` directly contrasted against the new `je vais
   manger` (past vs. future, same verb) — a genuine, low-cost tense
   discrimination beat as a side effect, not this module's spine.
7. **L7 consolidation, no new atoms** — cast-wide production drill (all
   five subject chunks, all four infinitives); interleave break.
8. **L8 checkpoint** — zero-new, all-graded; frame mixed across the cast
   and across all four infinitives; transfer test: an untaught
   subject+infinitive combination never co-presented before (e.g. produce
   "elle va parler" or "il va habiter" from pieces seen only in other
   pairings), mirrors m12/m14/m15/m16/m17's checkpoint shape.
9. **L9 integration** — ≥10 steps, no info cards, one big `dialogue_sim`
   tail: the cast makes plans for the week using the new frame across
   multiple infinitives and subjects (a natural fit — "future plans" is
   exactly what m5's own "make plans that stick" promise gestured at, now
   with real content instead of only destinations); 2–3 cross-module
   recalls reaching further back than m11–m17 (m5's own destination chunks,
   m8's days, m12's price frame).
10. **L10 mastery** — all-graded, every m19 atom present, ends on
    `dialogue_sim`: full recap mixing motion-sense and future-sense uses of
    the same chunks across the cast. **Soft promise for m20: left
    deliberately open**, same discipline m16/m17/(recommended by) m18 used
    — the playbook §8 arc does not name a module past m15 either, so this
    brief does not invent one; flag for whoever next edits the playbook §8
    arc that it should be extended to cover m16, m17, m18, AND m19 (this
    module), opening m20.

## 5. Homophone / liaison risks

- **No new `homophoneKey` pairs required.** `va`/`vais`/`vas` are each
  phonetically distinct from every other atom and from each other — no
  minimal pairs. Lower audio-risk than m14/m15/m16's participle modules,
  comparable to m17/m18.
- **No new elision landmine.** `va`/`vais`/`vas` are NOT on the
  eliding-clitic list (`courseAtoms.ts`'s eliding-word set is `le/la/je/
  ce/que`-class clitics preceding a vowel-onset word, not full conjugated
  verb forms) — verified against m5's own already-shipped sentences
  (`je vais à l'école`, `je vais à l'hôtel`) where `vais` sits directly
  before a vowel/mute-h word with zero elision, a live precedent, not a
  new inference. `il va habiter`/`elle va habiter` (L2's mute-h infinitive)
  follow the identical pattern — no new rule, same one already shipped.
- **Comprehension collision, not phonetic — the module's real risk.**
  `je vais`/`tu vas`/`on va`'s established motion sense vs. this module's
  new future sense is the same CLASS of risk as m16's «je suis»
  copula/auxiliary collision and (expected from) m18's «plus» idiom/frame
  collision (§7 there) — the two uses don't sound different from each
  other or confuse grading, they mean two different things from the same
  surface and need an explicit bridge card, not a silent reuse. No gate
  catches this; L1's and L6's cards are treated as load-bearing content
  for exactly this reason (§7 below).
- **No new `liaisonListen` item** — consistent with m12–m17 precedent
  (and m18's own recommendation); nothing in this module's core sentences
  forces one.
